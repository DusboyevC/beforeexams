# 6 Automated and emerging technologies

## Automated systems
- An **automated system** combines **sensors**, a **microprocessor** and **actuators** to perform a task without human input.
- **How it works**:
  1. The **sensor** takes readings continuously and sends the data to the microprocessor. If the sensor is analogue, an **ADC** converts the data to digital.
  2. The microprocessor **compares** the reading with a **stored value** (preset value or range).
  3. If the reading is outside the range, the microprocessor sends a **signal to an actuator** (e.g. it turns a heater on, opens a valve or sounds an alarm).
  4. This repeats continuously (it is a **loop**).
- **Example (greenhouse)**: a temperature sensor sends readings; the microprocessor compares them with the preset range; if the temperature is too low, it signals an actuator to switch on the heater; if it is too high, it opens the windows.
- Where it is used: industry, transport, agriculture, weather, gaming, lighting, science.
- **Advantages**: faster and more accurate than humans; works 24/7 without breaks; safer in dangerous environments; reduces labour costs; gives consistent results.
- **Disadvantages**: expensive to set up and maintain; can malfunction or be hacked; can't respond well to unexpected situations; can cause job losses.

## Robotics
- **Robotics**: the branch of computer science that combines the design, construction and operation of robots.
- **Characteristics of a robot**: it has a **mechanical structure** (a frame); it has **electrical components** (sensors, a microprocessor, actuators); it is **programmable**.
- Uses: factories (welding, painting, assembly lines), domestic (robot vacuum cleaners, lawnmowers), surgery, agriculture (harvesting), deliveries (drones), and exploring dangerous environments.
- **Advantages**: works in dangerous environments; works continuously without breaks; precise and consistent; productive.
- **Disadvantages**: expensive; cannot think for itself; can cause job losses; needs maintenance.

## Artificial intelligence (AI)
- **AI**: a branch of computer science dealing with the **simulation of intelligent behaviour** by computers.
- **Characteristics of AI**: the collection of **data and rules**; the ability to **reason**; the ability to **learn and adapt**.
- **Expert system**: emulates a human expert in a specific field, e.g. medical diagnosis.
  - **Knowledge base**: a database of facts.
  - **Rule base**: a set of IF–THEN rules.
  - **Inference engine**: applies the rules to the facts to reach conclusions.
  - **User interface**: asks questions and shows the results, with explanations.
- **Machine learning**: the program can **adapt its own processes and data** without being explicitly programmed; it improves as it gets more data, e.g. spam filters and recommendations.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In an automated-system answer, always mention the **sensor → microprocessor → compares with stored value → signal to actuator → continuous loop**.
- Never say "the sensor controls the heater"; the **microprocessor** makes the decision.
- Name a **specific sensor** for the scenario, e.g. a moisture sensor for watering plants.
- For questions like these, expect a link to **Hardware** (sensors) as well.
