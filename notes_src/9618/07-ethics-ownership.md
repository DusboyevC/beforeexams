# 7 Ethics and ownership

## What you must be able to do
- Explain why ethics matter for computing professionals, and the role of professional bodies and codes of conduct.
- Explain copyright and why it is needed.
- Compare software licences: free (open source), freeware, shareware and commercial.
- Discuss the impact of artificial intelligence on society, the economy and the environment.

## 7.1 Ethics

### Key terms
- **Ethics**: moral principles about what is right and wrong that guide behaviour.
- **Code of conduct**: a set of rules and standards a professional body expects its members to follow.
- **Professional bodies**: e.g. **ACM**, **IEEE** and **BCS**. They publish codes of conduct, promote standards, and can discipline members.

### Why ethics matter for computing professionals
- Their software affects the public's safety, privacy and wellbeing.
- They often have access to confidential data and systems.
- Clients trust them to work honestly and to an appropriate standard.

### The principles found in professional codes (ACM / IEEE joint code)
| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Public** | Act in the public interest; consider safety, privacy and the environment |
| **Client and employer** | Act in the client's best interests, consistent with the public interest; keep information confidential |
| **Product** | Make sure products meet the highest professional standards; test thoroughly |
| **Judgement** | Keep professional integrity and independence; don't accept bribes |
| **Management** | Managers promote an ethical approach to development and maintenance |
| **Profession** | Advance the reputation and integrity of the profession |
| **Colleagues** | Be fair to and supportive of colleagues; give credit for their work |
| **Self** | Keep learning throughout your career; stay competent |

### Applying it (typical scenario question)
A programmer is told to release software with known bugs to meet a deadline. Ethical response: tell the manager or client about the risks honestly; don't hide the defects; put public safety first; document the issues; refuse to sign off work they know is unsafe.

## 7.2 Ownership

### Copyright
- **Copyright**: the legal right of the creator to control how their original work (software, music, text, images) is copied, distributed and changed.
- **Why it is needed**: it protects the creator's income and investment, rewards innovation, and stops others claiming or selling the work as their own.
- **Plagiarism**: presenting someone else's work as your own without credit.

### Software licences
| Licence | Cost | Source code | Can modify and redistribute? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free software / open source** (e.g. GNU GPL) | Usually free | Available | Yes; modified versions are usually released under the same licence |
| **Freeware** | Free | Not available | No; you may use it but not change it |
| **Shareware** | Free trial, then payment | Not available | No; some features may be limited or the trial expires |
| **Commercial** | Must be bought (one-off or subscription) | Not available | No; restricted by the licence (e.g. number of users) |

**Open source**: the source code is published so anyone can study, change and improve it. Benefits: free, many people find bugs, can be customised. Drawbacks: support may be limited, and quality and security vary.

## 7.3 Artificial intelligence
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: the ability of a computer system to perform tasks that normally need human intelligence, e.g. learning, problem-solving, recognising speech and images, and making decisions.

| Area | Positive impact | Negative impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Social** | Medical diagnosis; assistive technology; safer transport (driverless cars); personalised learning | Job losses; bias in training data leads to unfair decisions; privacy concerns (facial recognition); deepfakes; over-reliance |
| **Economic** | Automation lowers costs; greater productivity; new industries and jobs (AI development) | Unemployment in routine jobs; wealth concentrated in AI companies; cost of retraining workers |
| **Environmental** | Optimises energy use (smart grids); climate modelling; precision farming | Training large models uses huge amounts of energy and water; hardware production and e-waste |

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In scenario questions, **apply** the principle to the situation. Name the principle (e.g. "Public") **and** say what the person should do.
- Freeware is **not** the same as free (open source) software. Freeware is free *of charge* but the source code is not available.
- For AI impact questions, give balanced points and be **specific** to the scenario.
