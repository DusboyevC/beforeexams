# 1.7 Differentiation

## Rules
- **d/dx (xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹** (for any rational n). Write roots and fractions as powers first: √x = x^½, 1/x² = x⁻².
- **Chain rule**: d/dx [f(g(x))] = f′(g(x)) × g′(x). For example, d/dx (3x + 1)⁵ = 15(3x + 1)⁴.
- dy/dx is the **gradient** of the curve; d²y/dx² is the rate of change of the gradient.

## Tangents and normals
- The gradient of the tangent at x = a is m = f′(a).
- **Normal**: perpendicular to the tangent, with gradient −1/m.
- Use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).

## Stationary points
1. Solve dy/dx = 0.
2. Classify each point:
   - d²y/dx² > 0: **minimum**.
   - d²y/dx² < 0: **maximum**.
   - (If d²y/dx² = 0, check the sign of dy/dx on each side.)
- **Increasing function**: dy/dx > 0; **decreasing function**: dy/dx < 0.

## Rates of change (connected rates)
- **dA/dt = dA/dr × dr/dt** (the chain rule with time).
- Example: a spherical balloon's volume increases at 50 cm³ s⁻¹. Find dr/dt when r = 5.
  V = (4/3)πr³, so dV/dr = 4πr² = 100π. dr/dt = 50/100π = **0.159 cm s⁻¹**.

## Practical maximum and minimum problems
1. Write the quantity to optimise in terms of **one** variable, using the constraint to eliminate the other.
2. Differentiate and set the derivative equal to 0.
3. Show it is a maximum or minimum with the second derivative.
4. Answer the question (e.g. the maximum volume, not just x).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Rewrite terms as powers **before** differentiating.
- Always **justify** the nature of a stationary point.
- In rate questions, write the chain rule with the variables named before substituting.
