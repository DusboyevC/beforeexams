# 3.9 Complex numbers

## Basics
- i² = −1. z = x + iy, where Re(z) = x and Im(z) = y.
- **Conjugate**: z* = x − iy. zz* = x² + y² (a real number).
- **Division**: multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.
- **Roots of real polynomials** occur in **conjugate pairs**: if 2 + 3i is a root, so is 2 − 3i.

## Modulus–argument form
- **Modulus** |z| = r = √(x² + y²).
- **Argument** arg z = θ, measured from the positive real axis, with −π < θ ≤ π. Check the **quadrant**.
- **z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) = re^(iθ)**.
- **Multiplication**: multiply the moduli and add the arguments. **Division**: divide the moduli and subtract the arguments.

## Square roots
To find √(5 + 12i), let (a + ib)² = 5 + 12i. Then a² − b² = 5 and 2ab = 12, giving a = 3, b = 2. So the roots are **±(3 + 2i)**.

## Argand diagrams and loci
| Locus | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \|z − a\| = r | A circle with centre a and radius r |
| \|z − a\| = \|z − b\| | The perpendicular bisector of the line joining a and b |
| arg(z − a) = θ | A half-line from a (not including a) at angle θ |
| \|z − a\| ≤ r | The region inside (and on) the circle |
- In shading questions, check which side of each line or circle is included.
- **Greatest and least values** of |z| or arg z in a region: use geometry, e.g. the distance from the origin to the centre ± the radius.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Find arguments in **radians** (unless told otherwise) and in the **correct quadrant**.
- For conjugate-pair questions, form the quadratic factor (x² − 2Re(α)x + |α|²), then divide.
- Draw loci accurately and label key points; use a dashed boundary for a strict inequality.
