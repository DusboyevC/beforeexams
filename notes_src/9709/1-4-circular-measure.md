# 1.4 Circular measure

## Radians
- **One radian** is the angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius.
- π rad = 180°. To convert degrees to radians, × π/180; radians to degrees, × 180/π.
- Common values: 30° = π/6, 45° = π/4, 60° = π/3, 90° = π/2.

## Formulae (θ in radians)
- **Arc length**: s = rθ.
- **Sector area**: A = ½r²θ.
- **Triangle area**: ½ab sin C.
- **Segment area** = sector area − triangle area = ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ = ½r²(θ − sin θ).
- **Chord length**: 2r sin(θ/2), or use the cosine rule.

## Typical problem pattern
Composite shapes: add or subtract sectors, triangles and segments. Use right-angled triangle trigonometry to find the missing angles in radians. Perimeters combine arc lengths and straight edges.

## Worked example
A sector OAB has radius 8 cm and angle 1.2 rad. Find the area of the segment cut off by the chord AB.
Sector = ½ × 64 × 1.2 = 38.4; triangle = ½ × 64 × sin 1.2 = 29.82.
Segment = **8.58 cm²**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Set your calculator to **radians** in this topic.
- "Exact answer" means leave π and surds in your answer.
- Write each angle's working clearly, e.g. "angle AOC = π/3 because cos AOC = 4/8".
