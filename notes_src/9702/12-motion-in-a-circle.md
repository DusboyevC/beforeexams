# 12 Motion in a circle

## Key definitions
- **Radian**: the angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius. 2π rad = 360°.
- **Angular displacement (θ)**: the angle through which an object has moved around the circle.
- **Angular speed (ω)**: the rate of change of angular displacement: ω = Δθ/Δt = 2π/T = 2πf.
- **Linear speed**: v = rω.
- **Centripetal acceleration**: a = rω² = v²/r, directed **towards the centre**.
- **Centripetal force**: the **resultant** force towards the centre needed for circular motion: F = mrω² = mv²/r.

## Why an object moving in a circle at constant speed accelerates
Its **direction** keeps changing, so its **velocity** changes, so it is accelerating. The acceleration (and the resultant force) is towards the centre and perpendicular to the velocity, so no work is done and the speed stays constant.

## Where the centripetal force comes from
| Situation | Force providing it |
|---|---|
| Satellite in orbit | Gravitational force |
| Car turning on a flat road | Friction between the tyres and the road |
| Mass on a string (horizontal circle) | Tension |
| Electron in a magnetic field | Magnetic force (Bqv) |
| Banked track or aircraft turning | Horizontal component of the normal contact force or lift |

## Vertical circles
- **At the top**: T + mg = mv²/r, so the tension (or normal force) is smallest here.
- **At the bottom**: T − mg = mv²/r, so the tension is largest here.
- The minimum speed at the top for a string to stay taut: T = 0 gives v = √(gr).

## Conical pendulum
- T cos θ = mg (vertical) and T sin θ = mv²/r (horizontal), so **tan θ = v²/(rg)**.

## Worked example
A 0.20 kg mass on a 0.50 m string is whirled in a horizontal circle at 3.0 revolutions per second. Find the tension (ignore gravity).
ω = 2π × 3.0 = 18.8 rad s⁻¹; F = mrω² = 0.20 × 0.50 × 18.8² = **35.5 N**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Centripetal force is **not** an extra force. It is the **resultant** of the real forces (tension, gravity, friction…).
- Use radians, and convert rpm to rad s⁻¹ (× 2π / 60).
- At the top of a vertical circle, weight acts **towards** the centre and helps provide the centripetal force.
