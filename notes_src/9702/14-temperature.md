# 14 Temperature

## Key definitions
- **Thermal equilibrium**: when objects in thermal contact are at the **same temperature**, so there is no **net** transfer of thermal energy between them.
- Thermal energy flows from a region of **higher** temperature to one of **lower** temperature.
- **Thermodynamic (absolute) temperature scale**: does not depend on the property of any particular substance. Its zero, **absolute zero (0 K)**, is the temperature at which a substance has **minimum internal energy**.
- **Conversion**: T (K) = θ (°C) + 273.15.
- **Specific heat capacity (c)**: the energy needed per unit mass to raise the temperature by 1 K: E = mcΔθ.
- **Specific latent heat (L)**: the energy needed per unit mass to change the state of a substance **at constant temperature**: E = mL (of fusion for solid ↔ liquid, of vaporisation for liquid ↔ gas).

## Thermometers
- A thermometer uses a physical property that **changes with temperature**, e.g. the volume of a liquid, the resistance of a thermistor, or the e.m.f. of a thermocouple.
- **Thermistor**: resistance changes with temperature; robust; can be read electronically; non-linear.
- **Thermocouple**: two different metal wires joined; produces an e.m.f. that depends on the temperature difference between the junctions. Wide range; very small junction, so a fast response and it can measure at a point; can be read remotely; non-linear.

## Experiments
**Specific heat capacity of a metal block**
- Heat the block with an immersion heater; measure V, I and the time t, and the temperature rise Δθ. Then **c = VIt/(mΔθ)**.
- Insulate the block to reduce heat loss; use the **maximum** temperature reached, since it keeps rising after the heater is switched off.

**Specific latent heat of vaporisation (electrical method)**
- Measure the mass of water boiled away in a time t with the heater at power P. Repeat at a different power and **subtract** the two results to cancel heat losses: (P₁ − P₂)t = (m₁ − m₂)L.

## Why the temperature stays constant during a change of state
The energy supplied increases the **potential energy** of the molecules: it is used to break or weaken the intermolecular bonds, not to increase their kinetic energy. So the temperature does not change.
- L_vaporisation > L_fusion: in boiling, the molecules must be completely separated (much more potential energy is needed), and work is also done pushing back the atmosphere as the volume increases greatly.

## Worked example
Find the energy needed to heat 0.50 kg of ice at −10 °C to water at 20 °C.
(c_ice = 2100, c_water = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, L_f = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹)
Ice −10 → 0 °C: 0.5 × 2100 × 10 = 10 500 J; melting: 0.5 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 167 000 J; water 0 → 20 °C: 0.5 × 4200 × 20 = 42 000 J.
Total = **2.2 × 10⁵ J**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Absolute zero means **minimum internal energy**, not "molecules stop moving".
- A temperature *difference* is the same in K and °C.
- In latent heat explanations, link the energy to **potential energy** (bonds), not kinetic energy.
