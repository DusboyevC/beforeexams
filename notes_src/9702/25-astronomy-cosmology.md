# 25 Astronomy and cosmology

## Key definitions
- **Luminosity (L)**: the **total power** of radiation emitted by a star.
- **Radiant flux intensity (F)**: the radiant power passing **normally** through unit area.
- **Inverse square law**: **F = L/(4πd²)**, as the power spreads over a sphere of area 4πd².
- **Standard candle**: an astronomical object of **known luminosity**, e.g. Cepheid variable stars or type Ia supernovae. Measuring its radiant flux intensity on Earth gives its **distance**.

## Stellar radii
- **Wien's displacement law**: **λ_max ∝ 1/T**, so λ_max T = constant (2.9 × 10⁻³ m K). It gives the surface temperature from the peak wavelength.
- **Stefan–Boltzmann law**: **L = 4πσr²T⁴** (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴).
- **Finding a star's radius**: use Wien's law to get T from λ_max, use a standard candle method (or a known distance) with the inverse square law to get L, then use Stefan–Boltzmann to find r.

## Hubble's law and the Big Bang
- **Redshift**: light from distant galaxies is shifted to **longer wavelengths**, because they are moving away from us.
- **Δλ/λ ≈ Δf/f ≈ v/c** (for v much less than c).
- **Hubble's law**: the recession speed of a galaxy is proportional to its distance: **v ≈ H₀d**.
- **Interpretation**: all distant galaxies are moving away, and the farther ones move faster. So the **universe is expanding**, which is evidence that it began in a hot, dense state: the **Big Bang**.
- Estimated age of the universe ≈ 1/H₀.

## Worked example
A spectral line of wavelength 656 nm is observed at 670 nm in light from a galaxy. Find its recession speed and its distance (H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹).
v = (14/656) × 3.00 × 10⁸ = 6.4 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹; d = v/H₀ = **2.9 × 10²⁴ m**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Luminosity is the **total power** (W); radiant flux intensity is the power per unit area (W m⁻²).
- Use T in **kelvin** in Wien's law and the Stefan–Boltzmann law.
- Redshift shows **recession**; Hubble's law (redshift increasing with distance) shows **expansion**.
