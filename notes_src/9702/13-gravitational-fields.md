# 13 Gravitational fields

## Key definitions
- **Gravitational field**: a region in which a mass experiences a force.
- **Gravitational field strength (g)**: the gravitational force per unit mass: g = F/m (N kg⁻¹).
- **Newton's law of gravitation**: the force between two point masses is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: **F = GMm/r²**.
- **Gravitational potential (φ)** at a point: the work done per unit mass in bringing a small test mass from **infinity** to that point: **φ = −GM/r**.
- **Gravitational potential energy**: Eₚ = mφ = −GMm/r.
- **Geostationary orbit**: an equatorial orbit with a period of **24 hours**, moving west to east (the same direction as the Earth's rotation), so the satellite stays above the same point on the Earth.

## Key equations
- Field strength of a point mass: **g = GM/r²** (radial field).
- Near the Earth's surface the field is approximately **uniform**: parallel, equally spaced field lines.
- Change in GPE between two radii: ΔEₚ = GMm (1/r₁ − 1/r₂).
- **The potential is negative**: zero potential is defined at infinity, the force is attractive, and work must be done **on** a mass to move it out to infinity.
- g = −dφ/dr: the field strength is minus the potential gradient.

## Orbits
- For a satellite in a circular orbit, gravity provides the centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r = mrω².
- So **v = √(GM/r)**: orbital speed falls as r rises.
- **Kepler's third law** (derive it): GM/r² = 4π²r/T² gives **T² = (4π²/GM) r³**, so T² ∝ r³.
- Geostationary radius ≈ 4.2 × 10⁷ m (about 36 000 km above the surface). Uses: communications and TV satellites; the dish can point in a fixed direction.
- **Low orbits**: higher resolution imaging and weather monitoring, but the satellite moves across the sky.

## Worked example
Find the gravitational field strength at the surface of Mars (M = 6.4 × 10²³ kg, R = 3.4 × 10⁶ m).
g = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6.4 × 10²³ / (3.4 × 10⁶)² = **3.7 N kg⁻¹**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- r is measured from the **centre** of the planet (radius + height).
- The definition of potential must include "**from infinity**" and "**per unit mass**".
- Field lines point **towards** the mass, in the direction of the force on a test mass.
- The satellite's mass cancels out in orbit equations.
