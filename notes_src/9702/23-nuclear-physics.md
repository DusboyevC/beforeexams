# 23 Nuclear physics

## Key definitions
- **Mass defect**: the difference between the total mass of the separate nucleons and the mass of the nucleus.
- **Binding energy**: the energy needed to **separate a nucleus completely into its individual nucleons** (equivalent to the mass defect: E = Δm c²).
- **Binding energy per nucleon**: binding energy ÷ nucleon number. A higher value means a **more stable** nucleus.
- **Nuclear fusion**: two light nuclei join to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy.
- **Nuclear fission**: a heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei (plus neutrons), releasing energy.
- **Activity (A)**: the number of decays per unit time (becquerel, Bq).
- **Decay constant (λ)**: the probability per unit time that a nucleus will decay.
- **Half-life (t½)**: the time for the number of undecayed nuclei (or the activity) to fall to half its initial value.

## Mass–energy
- **E = mc²**. Use **1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg**; 1 u is equivalent to about 931.5 MeV.
- **Energy released** in a reaction = (total mass before − total mass after) × c².

## Binding energy per nucleon curve
- It rises steeply for light nuclei, peaks at **iron-56** (the most stable nucleus), then falls slowly for heavy nuclei.
- **Fusion** of light nuclei and **fission** of heavy nuclei both move towards the peak, so the products have a **higher** binding energy per nucleon, and energy is released.

## Radioactive decay
- **Spontaneous**: it is not affected by external conditions such as temperature or pressure.
- **Random**: you cannot predict when a particular nucleus will decay; each nucleus has the same probability of decaying per unit time. Evidence: the count rate **fluctuates** about a mean.
- **A = λN**.
- **x = x₀ e^(−λt)**, where x is N, A or the count rate.
- **λ = ln 2 / t½ ≈ 0.693/t½**.

## Worked example
A sample has an initial activity of 8000 Bq and a half-life of 6.0 hours. Find its activity after 15 hours.
λ = 0.693/6.0 = 0.1155 h⁻¹; A = 8000 e^(−0.1155 × 15) = **1400 Bq** (2 s.f.)

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Binding energy is the energy **needed** to separate the nucleus. It is **not** energy stored in the nucleus.
- Keep λ and t in **consistent units** (s⁻¹ with s).
- Use the measured count rate minus **background** count rate in decay experiments.
- Explain energy release using the **increase in binding energy per nucleon**.
