# 16 Thermodynamics

## Key definitions
- **Internal energy**: the sum of the **random** distribution of **kinetic and potential energies** of the molecules in a system.
- **First law of thermodynamics**: **ΔU = q + W**, where ΔU = increase in internal energy, q = thermal energy **supplied to** the system, and W = work done **on** the system.

## Using the first law
- **Work done on a gas**: W = −pΔV. When the gas expands, ΔV is positive, so W is negative (the gas does work on its surroundings).
- **Ideal gas**: there are no intermolecular forces, so there is **no potential energy**, and the internal energy is all kinetic: **U ∝ T**. A change in temperature means a change in internal energy.

| Process | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Heating at constant volume | W = 0 | ΔU = q |
| Isothermal (constant temperature) for an ideal gas | ΔU = 0 | q = −W |
| Rapid compression (no time for heat transfer) | q = 0 | ΔU = W > 0, so the temperature rises |
| Free expansion of a gas into a vacuum | W = 0 | — |

## Changes of state
- When a solid melts at constant temperature, its internal energy increases (the potential energy rises) even though the temperature, and so the mean kinetic energy, stays constant.

## Cycles on p–V graphs
- The area under the graph = the work done by or on the gas.
- Around a complete cycle, the net ΔU = 0 (the gas returns to its starting state), so the net heat supplied = the net work done **by** the gas = the area enclosed by the loop.

## Worked example
A gas is supplied with 500 J of heat and expands at a constant pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa from 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³. Find ΔU.
W (on the gas) = −pΔV = −1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = −200 J.
ΔU = q + W = 500 − 200 = **+300 J**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Keep to the sign convention: q is heat **supplied to** the system; W is work done **on** the system.
- The internal energy definition needs **random** and **kinetic + potential**.
- For an ideal gas, a temperature rise means an internal energy rise, because there is no potential energy term.
