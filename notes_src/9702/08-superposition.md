# 8 Superposition

## Key definitions
- **Principle of superposition**: when two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the **sum of the individual displacements**.
- **Stationary wave**: formed when two progressive waves of the **same frequency, speed and (similar) amplitude**, travelling in **opposite directions**, superpose. Energy is **not** transferred.
- **Node**: a point on a stationary wave with zero amplitude. **Antinode**: a point with maximum amplitude.
- **Diffraction**: the spreading of a wave when it passes through a gap or around an edge.
- **Interference**: the superposition of waves from two or more **coherent** sources, giving regions of maximum and minimum amplitude.
- **Coherent**: having a **constant phase difference** (and so the same frequency).
- **Path difference**: the difference in the distances travelled by two waves to the same point.

## Stationary waves
- The distance between adjacent nodes (or adjacent antinodes) = **λ/2**.
- All points between two adjacent nodes oscillate **in phase**; points either side of a node are in **antiphase**.

| Progressive wave | Stationary wave |
|---|---|
| Transfers energy | Does not transfer energy |
| Every point has the same amplitude | Amplitude varies from zero (nodes) to a maximum (antinodes) |
| Adjacent points have different phases | Points between adjacent nodes are in phase |

**Strings** (both ends fixed are nodes): fundamental L = λ/2; harmonics at L = nλ/2.
**Air columns**: a closed end is a **node** and an open end is an **antinode**.
- Closed at one end: fundamental L = λ/4; then 3λ/4, 5λ/4 … (odd harmonics only).
- Open at both ends: fundamental L = λ/2.

**Determining the speed of sound with a resonance tube**: change the length of the air column above water (or move a piston) until resonance is heard at successive lengths L₁ and L₂. Then L₂ − L₁ = λ/2, so v = fλ = 2f(L₂ − L₁). This removes the end-correction error.

**Microwaves**: reflect microwaves from a metal plate and move a probe between the transmitter and the plate. The distance between adjacent nodes = λ/2.

## Diffraction
- The spreading is greatest when the **gap width ≈ wavelength**.
- Demonstrated with a ripple tank: plane waves pass through a gap and spread out as circular waves.

## Two-source interference
- **Constructive** interference (maximum): path difference = **nλ** (the waves arrive in phase).
- **Destructive** interference (minimum): path difference = **(n + ½)λ** (the waves arrive in antiphase).
- Conditions for an observable pattern: coherent sources, similar amplitudes, and (for light) the same plane of polarisation.

### Young's double slit
- **λ = ax/D**: a = slit separation, x = fringe separation, D = slit-to-screen distance.
- Measure across several fringes and divide, to reduce the percentage uncertainty in x.
- A laser is used because it is **coherent** and monochromatic. (With a white-light source, a single slit before the double slits makes the light coherent.)

## Diffraction grating
- **d sin θ = nλ**: d = line spacing = 1/(lines per metre), n = order.
- The highest order seen: n_max = d/λ, rounded **down** (θ cannot be more than 90°).
- Gratings give sharper, brighter maxima with a wider separation than a double slit, so they measure λ more precisely.

Worked example: a grating has 500 lines per mm, and light of wavelength 600 nm passes through it. Find the angle of the 2nd-order maximum.
d = 1/(500 × 10³) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ m; sin θ = 2 × 600 × 10⁻⁹ / 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.60, so θ = **36.9°**.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- The distance between adjacent nodes is **λ/2**, not λ.
- Coherent means a **constant phase difference**. "Same frequency" alone is not enough.
- Convert "lines per mm" to the spacing d in metres.
- In closed-tube questions, remember a node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end.
