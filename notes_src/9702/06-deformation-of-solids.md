# 6 Deformation of solids

## Key definitions
- **Tensile force**: stretches an object. **Compressive force**: squashes it.
- **Extension (x)**: the increase in length when a force is applied.
- **Hooke's law**: the extension of a spring or wire is proportional to the applied force, provided the **limit of proportionality** is not exceeded: F = kx.
- **Spring constant (k)**: force per unit extension (N m⁻¹).
- **Stress (σ)**: force per unit cross-sectional area: σ = F/A (Pa).
- **Strain (ε)**: extension per unit original length: ε = x/L (no unit).
- **Young modulus (E)**: stress/strain, in the region where Hooke's law is obeyed: E = FL/(Ax).
- **Elastic deformation**: the material returns to its original shape when the load is removed.
- **Plastic deformation**: the material does **not** return to its original shape when the load is removed (permanent deformation).
- **Elastic limit**: the maximum load beyond which the deformation is no longer elastic.
- **Limit of proportionality**: the point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to load.

## Strain energy (elastic potential energy)
- The area under a force–extension graph = the work done = the strain energy stored.
- Within Hooke's law: **Eₚ = ½Fx = ½kx²**.

## Springs in combination
- In **series**: 1/k_total = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ (less stiff).
- In **parallel**: k_total = k₁ + k₂ (stiffer).

## Experiment: measuring the Young modulus of a wire
1. Use a long thin wire (so the extension is large enough to measure), clamped at one end and passing over a pulley with masses hung from it. (Or use Searle's apparatus with a reference wire.)
2. Measure the original length L with a metre rule, and the diameter d with a **micrometer** at several points in different directions; take the mean. A = πd²/4.
3. Add masses in steps and measure the extension x each time using a marker against a scale (or a vernier).
4. Plot force F against extension x. The gradient = F/x in the linear region.
5. **E = gradient × L / A**.
6. Precautions: remove kinks first with a small starting load; check the wire returns to its original length when unloaded (it has not passed the elastic limit); wear safety goggles in case the wire snaps.

## Worked example
A steel wire 2.0 m long with cross-sectional area 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ m² stretches by 1.0 mm under 10 N. Find E.
Stress = 10 / 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ = 1.0 × 10⁸ Pa; strain = 1.0 × 10⁻³ / 2.0 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴
E = 1.0 × 10⁸ / 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **2.0 × 10¹¹ Pa**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Use the **diameter** to find A = πd²/4; don't use the diameter as the radius.
- Strain energy is ½Fx only while the graph is a straight line; otherwise use the area under the curve.
- When loading and unloading curves differ, the area between them is the energy dissipated (e.g. as heat in rubber).
- The Young modulus is a property of the **material**; the spring constant depends on the object's shape and size.
