# 9 Metals

## Metals and non-metals
| Property | Metals | Non-metals |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal conductivity | good | poor |
| Electrical conductivity | good | poor (graphite is an exception) |
| Malleability and ductility | yes | no, usually brittle |
| Melting and boiling points | usually high | usually low |

**Chemical properties**: many metals react with dilute acids (salt + hydrogen), with cold water or steam, and with oxygen (metal oxide).

## Uses of metals
- **Aluminium in aircraft**: low density.
- **Aluminium in overhead cables**: low density and good electrical conductivity.
- **Aluminium in food containers**: resistance to corrosion.
- **Copper in wiring**: good electrical conductivity and ductility.

## Alloys
- An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with other elements.
- **Brass**: copper and zinc.
- **Stainless steel**: iron with elements such as chromium, nickel and carbon.
- Alloys can be harder and stronger than pure metals, so they are more useful.
- **Stainless steel in cutlery**: hard and resistant to rusting.
- In a diagram, an alloy shows atoms of different sizes mixed in a lattice.
- **Extended**: the different sized atoms mean the layers can no longer slide over each other, so the alloy is harder.

## Reactivity series
Potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, **carbon**, zinc, iron, **hydrogen**, copper, silver, gold.

| Metals | Reaction |
|---|---|
| K, Na, Ca with cold water | hydroxide and hydrogen; K is most violent |
| Mg with steam | magnesium oxide and hydrogen |
| Mg, Zn, Fe with dilute HCl | salt and hydrogen; Mg is fastest |
| Cu, Ag, Au with dilute HCl | no reaction, as they are below hydrogen |

**Extended**
- More reactive metals have a greater tendency to form positive ions.
- **Displacement**: a metal displaces a less reactive metal from a solution of its ions. Mg + CuSO₄ → MgSO₄ + Cu. Ionic: Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu.
- **Aluminium** seems unreactive because of a protective layer of **aluminium oxide**, which stops further reaction.

## Corrosion
- Rusting needs **oxygen and water**. It forms **hydrated iron(III) oxide**.
- **Barrier methods**: painting, greasing, coating with plastic. They keep out oxygen or water.

**Extended**
- **Galvanising** coats iron with zinc. It is a barrier and it also gives sacrificial protection.
- **Sacrificial protection**: zinc is more reactive than iron, so zinc loses electrons in preference to iron. Zinc corrodes instead, even if the coating is scratched.

## Extraction of metals
- Metals high in the reactivity series are harder to extract from their ores.
- Metals below carbon can be reduced with carbon. Metals above carbon need electrolysis.

**Iron from hematite in the blast furnace**
1. Coke burns to give heat and carbon dioxide: C + O₂ → CO₂
2. Carbon dioxide is reduced to carbon monoxide: C + CO₂ → 2CO
3. Carbon monoxide reduces iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
4. Limestone decomposes: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
5. Calcium oxide removes silica as slag: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃

The equations are Extended.

**Aluminium**
- The main ore is **bauxite**. Aluminium is extracted by **electrolysis**.
- **Extended**: aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten **cryolite**. This lowers the melting point and so saves energy.
- **Extended**: at the cathode, Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al. At the anode, 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻.
- **Extended**: the **carbon anodes** must be replaced, because oxygen reacts with them to form carbon dioxide.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Rust needs both oxygen and water. Say both.
- Carbon monoxide is the main reducing agent in the blast furnace.
- Stainless steel is an alloy, so it is a mixture, not a compound.

## Quick self-check
1. Name the alloy of copper and zinc. *(Brass)*
2. Does copper react with dilute hydrochloric acid? *(No)*
3. Why is cryolite used? *(It lowers the melting point of the electrolyte, saving energy.)*
