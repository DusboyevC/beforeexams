# 3 Stoichiometry

## Formulae
- **Molecular formula**: the number and type of different atoms in one molecule.
- **Empirical formula (Extended)**: the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms or ions in a compound. For example, C₂H₄ has the empirical formula CH₂.
- **Ionic formulae (Extended)**: balance the charges. Al³⁺ and O²⁻ give Al₂O₃.

## Equations
- State symbols: **(s)** solid, **(l)** liquid, **(g)** gas, **(aq)** aqueous.
- Balance by changing the big numbers in front, never the formulae.
- Example: Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
- **Ionic equations (Extended)** leave out spectator ions. Example: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).

## Relative masses
- **Relative atomic mass (Aᵣ)**: the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12 of the mass of an atom of ¹²C.
- **Relative molecular mass (Mᵣ)**: the sum of the relative atomic masses. **Relative formula mass** is used for ionic compounds.
- Example: Mᵣ of CO₂ = 12 + 16 + 16 = **44**.

**Reacting masses in simple proportions**: if 12 g of carbon gives 44 g of CO₂, then 3 g of carbon gives 11 g of CO₂.

- Concentration can be measured in **g/dm³** or **mol/dm³**.

## The mole (Extended)
- One **mole** contains **6.02 × 10²³** particles. This is the **Avogadro constant**.
- **moles = mass ÷ molar mass**
- **Gas volume**: 1 mol of any gas occupies **24 dm³** at r.t.p. So moles = volume (dm³) ÷ 24.
- **Concentration**: mol/dm³ = moles ÷ volume (dm³).
- 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³.

## Mole calculations (Extended)
**Reacting masses**: find the mass of CO₂ from 10 g of CaCO₃. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂.
- Moles of CaCO₃ = 10 ÷ 100 = 0.10 mol
- Ratio 1 : 1, so 0.10 mol of CO₂
- Mass = 0.10 × 44 = **4.4 g**
- Gas volume = 0.10 × 24 = **2.4 dm³** at r.t.p.

**Limiting reactant**: 2.4 g of Mg (0.10 mol) reacts with 0.10 mol of HCl. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. Mg needs 0.20 mol of HCl, so **HCl is limiting**. Only 0.050 mol of H₂ forms.

**Titration**: 25.0 cm³ of NaOH is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.10 mol/dm³ HCl. NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O.
- Moles of HCl = 0.10 × (20.0 ÷ 1000) = 0.0020 mol
- Moles of NaOH = 0.0020 mol
- Concentration of NaOH = 0.0020 ÷ (25.0 ÷ 1000) = **0.080 mol/dm³**

**Empirical formula**: a compound is 80% C and 20% H by mass.
- C: 80 ÷ 12 = 6.67. H: 20 ÷ 1 = 20.
- Divide by the smallest: C = 1, H = 3. Empirical formula: **CH₃**.
- If Mᵣ = 30, the molecular formula is **C₂H₆**.

**Percentages**
- Percentage yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
- Percentage by mass of an element = (total Aᵣ of that element ÷ Mᵣ) × 100
- Percentage purity = (mass of pure substance ÷ mass of impure sample) × 100

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000 before using concentration.
- Always use the ratio from the balanced equation.
- Show working, as most calculation marks are for method.

## Quick self-check
1. Find the Mᵣ of H₂SO₄. *(98)*
2. How many moles in 9.0 g of water? *(0.50 mol)*
3. What volume does 0.25 mol of gas fill at r.t.p.? *(6.0 dm³)*
