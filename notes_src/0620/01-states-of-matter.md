# 1 States of matter

## Solids, liquids and gases
| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | regular | irregular | random |
| Separation | very close | close | far apart |
| Motion | vibrate on the spot | slide past each other | fast and random |
| Shape | fixed | takes the shape of its container | fills the container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | not fixed |
| Compressible | no | hardly | yes |

## Changes of state
- **Melting**: solid to liquid.
- **Boiling** and **evaporating**: liquid to gas. Boiling happens at the boiling point. Evaporating happens at the surface, below the boiling point.
- **Condensing**: gas to liquid.
- **Freezing**: liquid to solid.

## Heating and cooling curves (Extended)
On a heating curve, the temperature rises, then stays flat, then rises again.
- **Sloping parts**: particles gain kinetic energy, so the temperature rises.
- **Flat parts**: this is a change of state. The energy overcomes the forces between particles, so the temperature does not change.
- A cooling curve has the same shape in reverse. Flat parts occur at the same temperatures, because energy is released as forces form.

## Gas volume
- **Higher temperature**: volume increases (at constant pressure).
- **Higher pressure**: volume decreases (at constant temperature).

**Explanation (Extended)**
- When heated, particles gain kinetic energy, move faster and hit the walls harder. The gas expands to keep the pressure constant.
- When pressure rises, particles are pushed closer together, so they take up less space.

## Diffusion
- **Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from a higher concentration to a lower concentration, because of their random movement.
- Examples: a smell spreading across a room; potassium manganate(VII) colouring still water.
- Diffusion is fast in gases and very slow in liquids.

**Effect of relative molecular mass (Extended)**
- Lighter gases diffuse faster. At the same temperature, lighter particles move faster.
- Example: in a long tube with ammonia at one end and hydrogen chloride at the other, a white ring of ammonium chloride forms closer to the HCl end. Ammonia (Mᵣ = 17) travels faster than HCl (Mᵣ = 36.5).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- On flat parts of a curve, energy is still being supplied. It does not raise the temperature.
- Particles themselves do not expand or melt. Only their arrangement and motion change.
- Use the words kinetic energy and forces of attraction in your explanations.

## Quick self-check
1. Name the change from gas to liquid. *(Condensing)*
2. Why does the temperature stay constant during melting? *(The energy overcomes the forces between particles.)*
3. Which diffuses faster, CO₂ or H₂? *(H₂, because it has the lower Mᵣ.)*
