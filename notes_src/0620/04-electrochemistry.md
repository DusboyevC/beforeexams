# 4 Electrochemistry

## Electrolysis
- **Electrolysis** is the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.
- **Anode**: the positive electrode.
- **Cathode**: the negative electrode.
- **Electrolyte**: the molten or aqueous substance that undergoes electrolysis.
- Metals or hydrogen form at the cathode. Non-metals, other than hydrogen, form at the anode.

**Molten binary compounds**: the metal forms at the cathode and the non-metal at the anode.

## Products to know (inert electrodes)
| Electrolyte | Cathode (−) | Anode (+) | Observations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Molten lead(II) bromide | lead | bromine | silvery liquid metal; red-brown vapour |
| Concentrated aqueous NaCl | hydrogen | chlorine | bubbles at both electrodes |
| Dilute sulfuric acid | hydrogen | oxygen | bubbles at both electrodes |

**Aqueous copper(II) sulfate (Extended)**

| Electrodes | Cathode | Anode | Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon or graphite | copper forms, pink-brown coating | oxygen, bubbles | blue colour fades |
| Copper | copper forms, cathode gains mass | copper dissolves, anode loses mass | stays blue |

**Halide solutions (Extended)**: at the anode, a **concentrated** halide gives the halogen and a **dilute** one gives oxygen. At the cathode, hydrogen forms if the metal is more reactive than hydrogen.

## Charge transfer (Extended)
- Electrons move through the external circuit, from anode to cathode.
- Ions move through the electrolyte: cations to the cathode and anions to the anode.
- Electrons are gained at the cathode and lost at the anode.

## Half-equations (Extended)
**At the cathode, reduction (gain of electrons):**
- Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb
- 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

**At the anode, oxidation (loss of electrons):**
- 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻
- 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
- 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻
- Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (with a copper anode)

## Electroplating
- Metal objects are electroplated to improve their **appearance** and their **resistance to corrosion**.
- **How**: the object is the **cathode**. The anode is the plating metal. The electrolyte contains ions of the plating metal.
- Example: to plate a spoon with silver, use a silver anode and silver nitrate solution.

## Hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells
- A fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to produce electricity, with **water as the only chemical product**.

**Compared with petrol engines (Extended)**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| only water is produced | hydrogen is hard to store and transport |
| no CO₂ or other polluting gases in use | few refuelling stations |
| more efficient | making hydrogen often uses fossil fuels |
| | fuel cells are expensive |

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Remember **PANIC**: positive anode, negative is cathode.
- Oxidation is at the anode. Reduction is at the cathode.
- A solid ionic compound cannot be electrolysed, as its ions cannot move.

## Quick self-check
1. Name the anode product for molten NaCl. *(Chlorine)*
2. Why does a copper sulfate solution stay blue with copper electrodes? *(Copper ions lost at the cathode are replaced from the anode.)*
3. What is the object to be plated connected as? *(The cathode)*
