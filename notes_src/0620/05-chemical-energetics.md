# 5 Chemical energetics

## Exothermic and endothermic reactions
- An **exothermic** reaction transfers thermal energy to the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings **rises**. Examples: combustion, neutralisation.
- An **endothermic** reaction takes in thermal energy from the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings **falls**. Examples: thermal decomposition, dissolving ammonium nitrate.

## Enthalpy change (Extended)
- The transfer of thermal energy during a reaction is the **enthalpy change, ΔH**.
- **ΔH is negative** for exothermic reactions.
- **ΔH is positive** for endothermic reactions.

## Activation energy (Extended)
- **Activation energy, Eₐ**, is the minimum energy that colliding particles must have to react.

## Reaction pathway diagrams
- **Exothermic**: products are **lower** than reactants, as energy has left to the surroundings.
- **Endothermic**: products are **higher** than reactants.

**Labelling a diagram (Extended)**
- Reactants on the left and products on the right.
- **Eₐ**: an arrow from the reactants up to the top of the curve.
- **ΔH**: an arrow from the reactants level to the products level. It points down for exothermic and up for endothermic.

## Bond energies (Extended)
- **Bond breaking is endothermic.** Energy goes in.
- **Bond making is exothermic.** Energy comes out.
- If more energy is released making bonds than is used breaking them, the reaction is exothermic.

**ΔH = energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds**

**Worked example**: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Bond energies in kJ/mol: H–H = 436, Cl–Cl = 242, H–Cl = 431.
- Bonds broken: 436 + 242 = 678 kJ
- Bonds made: 2 × 431 = 862 kJ
- ΔH = 678 − 862 = **−184 kJ/mol**, so the reaction is exothermic.

**Worked example 2**: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. C–H = 413, O=O = 498, C=O = 805, O–H = 464.
- Broken: 4 × 413 + 2 × 498 = 1652 + 996 = 2648 kJ
- Made: 2 × 805 + 4 × 464 = 1610 + 1856 = 3466 kJ
- ΔH = 2648 − 3466 = **−818 kJ/mol**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Use the temperature of the surroundings to decide. Exothermic: the thermometer reading goes up.
- Count every bond. CH₄ has four C–H bonds, and 2H₂O has four O–H bonds.
- Always give the sign of ΔH.

## Quick self-check
