# 6 Chemical reactions

## Physical and chemical changes
- **Physical change**: no new substance forms and it is usually easy to reverse. Examples: melting, dissolving.
- **Chemical change**: a new substance forms and it is usually hard to reverse. Examples: burning, rusting.

## Rate of reaction
**The rate increases with:**
- higher concentration of solutions
- higher pressure of gases
- larger surface area of solids (smaller pieces)
- higher temperature
- a catalyst, including enzymes

A **catalyst** increases the rate of a reaction and is unchanged at the end. **Extended**: it decreases the activation energy.

## Collision theory (Extended)
Particles react only if they collide with energy at least equal to the activation energy.

| Change | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Concentration or pressure | more particles per unit volume, so more frequent collisions |
| Surface area | more particles exposed, so more frequent collisions |
| Temperature | particles have more kinetic energy, so collisions are more frequent, and more have energy above Eₐ |
| Catalyst | lowers Eₐ, so more collisions have enough energy |

## Measuring rate
- **Gas formed**: collect in a gas syringe and record volume against time.
- **Change in mass**: put the flask on a balance and record the loss in mass as gas escapes.

**Reading graphs**: a steeper line means a faster reaction. A flat line means the reaction has finished.

**Evaluating methods (Extended)**: a gas syringe is accurate, but a very soluble gas gives low results if collected over water. Loss of mass does not work well with a light gas such as hydrogen.

## Reversible reactions
- Some reactions are reversible, shown by **⇌**.
- Heating hydrated copper(II) sulfate: **blue → white**. Adding water to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate: **white → blue**.
- Heating hydrated cobalt(II) chloride: **pink → blue**. Adding water: **blue → pink**.

## Equilibrium (Extended)
In a closed system, a reversible reaction is at equilibrium when:
- the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction
- the concentrations of reactants and products no longer change

**Moving the position of equilibrium**

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Raise temperature | shifts in the endothermic direction |
| Lower temperature | shifts in the exothermic direction |
| Raise pressure | shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas |
| Add more reactant | shifts to the products side |
| Add a catalyst | no change, it only reaches equilibrium faster |

## Haber process (Extended)
- **N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)**, and the forward reaction is exothermic.
- Sources: nitrogen from **air**, hydrogen from **methane**.
- Conditions: **450 °C, 20 000 kPa (200 atm), iron catalyst**.

## Contact process (Extended)
- **2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)**, and the forward reaction is exothermic.
- Sources: sulfur dioxide from **burning sulfur or roasting sulfide ores**, oxygen from **air**.
- Conditions: **450 °C, 200 kPa (2 atm), vanadium(V) oxide catalyst**.

## Why these conditions (Extended)
- **Temperature**: low temperature gives a higher yield, but the rate would be too slow. 450 °C is a **compromise**.
- **Pressure**: high pressure favours the side with fewer gas moles and speeds up the reaction. But very high pressure is **expensive** and **dangerous**. The Contact process gives a good yield at low pressure, so 200 kPa is enough.
- **Catalyst**: gives a faster reaction, with no effect on yield.

## Redox
- A Roman numeral shows the oxidation number of an element: iron(III) has an oxidation number of +3.
- **Redox** reactions involve simultaneous oxidation and reduction.
- **Oxidation** is gain of oxygen. **Reduction** is loss of oxygen.

**Extended**
- **Oxidation**: loss of electrons, an increase in oxidation number.
- **Reduction**: gain of electrons, a decrease in oxidation number.
- **OIL RIG**: oxidation is loss, reduction is gain.

**Oxidation number rules (Extended)**
- An uncombined element is 0.
- A monatomic ion equals its charge.
- The numbers in a compound add up to 0.
- The numbers in an ion add up to its charge.

**Agents (Extended)**
- An **oxidising agent** oxidises another substance and is itself reduced.
- A **reducing agent** reduces another substance and is itself oxidised.

**Colour tests (Extended)**
- Acidified potassium manganate(VII) goes from **purple to colourless** with a reducing agent.
- Aqueous potassium iodide goes from **colourless to brown** with an oxidising agent.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- A catalyst does not change the position of equilibrium.
- Say that collisions are more frequent, not only more collisions.
- In redox, name the species that is oxidised and the one that is reduced.
