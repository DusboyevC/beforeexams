# 1 Physical quantities and measurement

## Measuring length, volume and time
- **Length**: use a ruler. Read it with your eye directly above the mark to avoid **parallax error**.
- **Volume of a liquid**: use a measuring cylinder. Read the bottom of the **meniscus** at eye level.
- **Time**: use a clock for long intervals and a digital timer for short ones. A stopwatch reading includes your reaction time.

## Measuring multiples to get a better average
Small distances and short times are hard to measure once, so measure many and divide.
- **Thickness of a sheet of paper**: measure a stack of 100 sheets and divide by 100.
- **Period of a pendulum**: time 20 oscillations and divide by 20. One oscillation is a full swing, there and back.
- Repeat the reading and take the mean to reduce the effect of random errors.

## Scalars and vectors
- A **scalar** has magnitude (size) only.
- A **vector** has magnitude and direction.

| Scalars | Vectors |
|---|---|
| distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature | force, weight, velocity, acceleration, momentum, electric field strength, gravitational field strength |

## Resultant of two vectors at right angles (Extended)
This is limited to forces or velocities.
- **By calculation**: use Pythagoras for the size and tan for the direction.
- **By drawing**: choose a scale, draw the vectors nose to tail, then measure the line that closes the triangle and its angle.
- Worked example: forces of 3.0 N east and 4.0 N north. Resultant = √(3.0² + 4.0²) = **5.0 N**. Angle from east: tan θ = 4.0 / 3.0, so θ = **53°** north of east.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- A length measured with a ruler should show the zero mark or a start and end reading.
- Weight is a vector and mass is a scalar. Do not mix them up.
- For a resultant, always give the direction as well as the size.

## Quick self-check
1. Why time 20 swings of a pendulum rather than one? *(It reduces the percentage error from reaction time.)*
2. Is temperature a scalar or a vector? *(Scalar)*
3. A plane flies at 120 m/s north in a 50 m/s wind blowing east. Find the resultant speed. *(130 m/s)*
