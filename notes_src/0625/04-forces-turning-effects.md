# 4 Forces and turning effects

## What forces do
- Forces can change the **size and shape** of an object.
- A **resultant force** can change an object's velocity, by changing its speed or its direction.
- An object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed, unless a resultant force acts on it.
- Forces in the same direction add. Forces in opposite directions subtract.

## Friction and drag
- **Solid friction** acts between two surfaces. It may impede motion and it produces heating.
- **Drag** is friction on an object moving through a liquid or a gas. Air resistance is drag in a gas.

## Load–extension graphs
- **Experiment**: hang a spring from a clamp beside a ruler. Record its original length. Add masses one at a time and record the new length each time. **Extension = new length − original length.**
- Plot load against extension. Up to a point, the line is straight and passes through the origin.
- **Extended**: the point where the line stops being straight is the **limit of proportionality**.
- **Extended**: the **spring constant** is force per unit extension: **k = F / x**. Unit: N/m.

## Force and acceleration (Extended)
- **F = ma**. Force and acceleration are in the same direction.
- Worked example: a resultant force of 600 N acts on a 1200 kg car. a = 600 / 1200 = **0.50 m/s²**.

## Circular motion (Extended)
An object moves in a circle because a force acts perpendicular to its motion, towards the centre.
- Speed increases if the force increases, with mass and radius constant.
- Radius decreases if the force increases, with mass and speed constant.
- More mass needs more force to keep speed and radius constant.

## Moments
- The **moment** of a force measures its turning effect.
- **Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot.** Unit: N m.
- Everyday examples: a spanner, a door handle far from the hinge, a see-saw.
- **Principle of moments**: when balanced, total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment.
- **Equilibrium** means no resultant force and no resultant moment.

**Worked example**: a 300 N child sits 2.0 m from a see-saw pivot. A second child sits 1.5 m on the other side. For balance: 300 × 2.0 = W × 1.5, so W = **400 N**.

**Showing equilibrium (Extended)**: balance a metre rule on a pivot and hang known masses at different distances on both sides. When it balances, calculate the moments. The clockwise total equals the anticlockwise total.

## Centre of gravity
- The **centre of gravity** is the point through which the whole weight of an object appears to act.
- **Finding it for an irregular lamina**: hang the shape from a pin so it swings freely. Hang a plumb line from the same pin and mark its line. Repeat from another point. The lines cross at the centre of gravity.
- **Stability**: an object is more stable with a **low** centre of gravity and a **wide** base. It topples when the vertical line through the centre of gravity falls outside the base.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Use the perpendicular distance from the pivot, not the length along a slanted bar.
- Extension is not the same as length.
- Convert cm to m if the answer must be in N m.

## Quick self-check
1. A 12 N force acts 0.25 m from a pivot. Find the moment. *(3.0 N m)*
2. A spring stretches 4.0 cm under 2.0 N. Find k in N/cm. *(0.50 N/cm)*
3. Why are racing cars built low? *(A low centre of gravity makes them harder to topple.)*
