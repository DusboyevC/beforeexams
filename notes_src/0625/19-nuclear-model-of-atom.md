# 19 The nuclear model of the atom

## The atom
- An atom has a small, positively charged **nucleus** with negatively charged **electrons** in orbit around it.
- Atoms can form **positive ions** by losing electrons, or **negative ions** by gaining electrons.

## Alpha scattering (Extended)
Alpha particles were fired at a thin metal sheet.

| Observation | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Most went straight through | the atom is mostly empty space, with a very small nucleus |
| A few deflected at large angles | the nucleus is positively charged, so it repels alpha particles |
| A very few bounced back | the nucleus contains most of the mass of the atom |

## The nucleus
- The nucleus contains **protons** and **neutrons**, which are called nucleons.

| Particle | Relative charge |
|---|---|
| Proton | +1 |
| Neutron | 0 |
| Electron | −1 |

- **Proton number Z**: the number of protons, also called atomic number.
- **Nucleon number A**: the number of protons plus neutrons, also called mass number.
- **Number of neutrons = A − Z.**
- **Nuclide notation**: A is written at the top left of the symbol and Z at the bottom left, as in ²³⁵₉₂U.
- **Extended**: the relative charge on a nucleus equals its proton number, and its relative mass equals its nucleon number.

## Isotopes
- **Isotopes** are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
- An element may have more than one isotope. Examples: ¹²C and ¹⁴C.

## Fission and fusion (Extended)
- **Nuclear fission**: a large nucleus splits into smaller nuclei. Example: ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + 3 ¹₀n.
- **Nuclear fusion**: two small nuclei join to make a larger one. Example: ²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n.
- In both, the total mass after is slightly less than before. This lost mass is released as energy.
- In a nuclide equation, the top numbers and the bottom numbers each balance on both sides.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- A is the top number and Z is the bottom number.
- Isotopes have the same chemical properties, as they have the same number of electrons.
- Fission splits and fusion joins. Fusion powers the Sun.

## Quick self-check

2. What does a very few alpha particles bouncing back show? *(Most of the mass is in a small nucleus.)*
3. How do the isotopes ¹²C and ¹⁴C differ? *(¹⁴C has 2 more neutrons.)*
