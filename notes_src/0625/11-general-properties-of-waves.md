# 11 General properties of waves

## What waves do
- Waves transfer **energy without transferring matter**.
- Wave motion can be shown with vibrations in ropes and springs, and with water waves.

## Wave features
- **Wavefront**: a line joining points on a wave that are in step, such as a line along a crest.
- **Wavelength (λ)**: the distance from one crest to the next. Unit: m.
- **Frequency (f)**: the number of waves passing a point per second. Unit: Hz.
- **Crest (peak)**: the highest point. **Trough**: the lowest point.
- **Amplitude**: the maximum displacement from the rest position.
- **Wave speed (v)**: the distance a wave travels per second. Unit: m/s.

## The wave equation
- **v = fλ**
- Worked example: a wave has frequency 5.0 Hz and wavelength 0.40 m. v = 5.0 × 0.40 = **2.0 m/s**.

## Transverse and longitudinal waves
| | Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of vibration | at right angles to the direction of travel | parallel to the direction of travel |
| Examples | electromagnetic radiation, water waves, seismic S-waves | sound waves, seismic P-waves |

## Reflection, refraction and diffraction
- **Reflection** at a plane surface: waves bounce off. The wavelength and speed do not change.
- **Refraction**: waves change direction because of a **change of speed**.
- **Diffraction**: waves spread out through a narrow gap, or around an edge.

## Using a ripple tank
- **Reflection**: place a straight barrier in the water and watch the waves bounce off it.
- **Refraction**: place a glass sheet to make shallow water. Waves slow down, so the wavelength gets shorter, and they change direction if they meet the boundary at an angle.
- **Diffraction by a gap**: place two barriers with a gap between them.
- **Diffraction by an edge**: place one barrier and watch the waves bend around its end.

**Frequency does not change** in reflection, refraction or diffraction.

## Diffraction in detail (Extended)
- Most diffraction happens when the gap size is about the same as the wavelength.
- A wide gap gives very little spreading.
- **Longer wavelengths** diffract more, through a gap and at an edge.
- Diffraction does not change the wavelength.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Amplitude goes from the middle to the crest, not from crest to trough.
- Wavelength is one full wave. Check you measure a full cycle.
- When drawing diffraction, keep the wavelength the same on both sides.
- When drawing refraction into shallow water, draw the wavefronts closer together.

## Quick self-check
1. Find the wavelength of a wave with v = 340 m/s and f = 170 Hz. *(2.0 m)*
2. Is a sound wave transverse or longitudinal? *(Longitudinal)*
3. What happens to the speed and wavelength of water waves entering shallow water? *(Both decrease, and the frequency stays the same.)*
