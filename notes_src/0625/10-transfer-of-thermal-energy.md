# 10 Transfer of thermal energy

## Conduction
- Good thermal conductors, such as metals, transfer thermal energy well. Thermal insulators, such as wood, plastic and air, do not.
- **Experiment**: fix wax and a small pin to the ends of rods of different materials and heat the other ends equally. The pin falls first on the best conductor.

**How it works (Extended)**
- In **all solids**, atoms or molecules vibrate more when heated and pass the vibrations through the lattice.
- In **metals**, free (delocalised) electrons also move and carry energy quickly, so metals conduct well.
- **Gases and most liquids** conduct badly, because their particles are far apart and rarely collide. Many solids lie between good conductors and insulators.

## Convection
- Convection is an important method of thermal energy transfer in liquids and gases.
- The heated fluid expands, so its **density falls** and it rises. Cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place. This sets up a **convection current**.
- **Experiment**: put a crystal of potassium manganate(VII) at the bottom of a beaker of water and heat gently below it. Coloured streams rise and circulate.
- Convection cannot happen in solids, because the particles cannot flow.

## Radiation
- **Thermal radiation** is infrared radiation. All objects emit it.
- It does **not need a medium**, so it can travel through a vacuum. This is how energy reaches us from the Sun.

| Surface | Emitter | Absorber | Reflector |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dull black | good | good | poor |
| Shiny white or silver | poor | poor | good |

**Extended points**
- To stay at a constant temperature, an object must transfer energy away at the same rate it receives it.
- If it receives energy faster than it loses it, its temperature rises. If it loses energy faster, its temperature falls.
- The Earth's temperature depends on the balance between incoming radiation and radiation emitted from its surface.
- The rate of emission rises with **higher surface temperature** and **larger surface area**.

**Experiments (Extended)**
- **Emitters**: use a Leslie cube with different faces, filled with hot water. Place an infrared detector the same distance from each face and compare the readings.
- **Absorbers**: put a black and a shiny plate at equal distances from a heater, with wax and a coin on the back of each. The coin falls first from the black plate.

## Applications
- **Kitchen pans**: metal bases conduct energy to the food. Plastic or wooden handles are insulators.
- **Heating a room**: a radiator warms the air next to it, which rises. Cool air sinks and a convection current spreads the heat.

**More complex examples (Extended)**
- **Fire burning wood or coal**: radiation warms the room near the fire, and hot gases rise up the chimney by convection, drawing in fresh air.
- **Car radiator**: hot water from the engine flows through thin metal fins. Energy conducts through the metal, then passes to the air by convection and radiation.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Never say that heat rises. Say that the warm fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises.
- Molecules do not expand. The space between them increases.
- Compare surfaces in the same conditions: same distance, same starting temperature.

## Quick self-check
1. Why do metals conduct better than plastics? *(They also have free electrons that carry energy.)*
2. How does the Sun's energy cross space? *(By radiation, which needs no medium.)*
3. Why are refrigerator cooling units at the top? *(Cooled air becomes denser and sinks, setting up convection.)*
