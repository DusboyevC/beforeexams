# 21 Earth and the Solar System

## The Earth and Moon
- The Earth rotates on its **tilted axis** once in about **24 hours**. This causes day and night, and the apparent daily motion of the Sun.
- The Earth orbits the Sun once in about **365 days**. The tilt causes the **seasons**: the half of the Earth tilted towards the Sun has summer.
- The Moon orbits the Earth in about **one month**. This causes the cycle of the Moon's phases.

## Orbital speed (Extended)
- **v = 2πr / T**, where r is the average radius of the orbit and T is the orbital period.
- Worked example: the Earth has r = 1.5 × 10¹¹ m and T = 3.2 × 10⁷ s. v = 2 × π × 1.5 × 10¹¹ / 3.2 × 10⁷ = **2.9 × 10⁴ m/s**.

## The Solar System
It contains:
- one star, the **Sun**
- the eight planets
- minor planets, including **dwarf planets** such as Pluto and **asteroids** in the asteroid belt
- moons that orbit planets
- smaller bodies, including **comets** and natural satellites

**Planets in order from the Sun**: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

- The four nearest the Sun are **rocky and small**.
- The four furthest are **gaseous and large**.

## How the Solar System formed
**The accretion model**
1. Interstellar clouds of gas and dust contain many elements.
2. **Gravity** pulled the cloud together.
3. The material rotated and formed an **accretion disc**.
4. Near the hot centre, only rocky material could stay solid, so small rocky planets formed.
5. Further out it was cooler, so gases collected into large planets.

## Gravity in the Solar System
- The gravitational field at a planet's surface depends on the **planet's mass**.
- The field gets weaker with **distance** from the planet.
- The Sun contains most of the mass of the Solar System, which explains why the planets orbit the Sun.
- The force that keeps an object in orbit is the **gravitational attraction of the Sun**.

**Extended**
- Planets, minor planets and comets have **elliptical** orbits. The Sun is not at the centre of the ellipse, unless the orbit is almost circular.
- The Sun's field gets weaker, and orbital speeds get lower, further from the Sun.
- An object in an elliptical orbit travels **faster when closer to the Sun**. As it falls closer, gravitational potential energy changes to kinetic energy, and total energy is conserved.
- Planetary data on orbital distance, orbital time, density, surface temperature and gravitational field strength can be analysed. Look for patterns, such as a longer orbital time further from the Sun.

## Light travel time
- Time = distance ÷ speed, with light at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.
- Worked example: Sun to Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. t = 1.5 × 10¹¹ / 3.0 × 10⁸ = **500 s**, about 8 minutes.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Seasons are caused by the Earth's tilt, not by its distance from the Sun.
- Day and night are caused by the Earth's rotation, not by its orbit.
- A moon orbits a planet, and a planet orbits a star.

3. Where in its orbit does a comet move fastest? *(Closest to the Sun)*
