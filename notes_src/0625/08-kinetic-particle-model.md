# 8 Kinetic particle model of matter

## States of matter
| State | Arrangement | Separation | Motion | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solid | regular pattern | close together | vibrate about fixed positions | fixed shape and volume |
| Liquid | irregular | close together | move around each other | fixed volume, takes the container's shape |
| Gas | random | far apart | move fast in all directions | no fixed shape or volume, easily compressed |

**Changes of state**: melting (solid to liquid), boiling or evaporation (liquid to gas), condensation (gas to liquid), solidification or freezing (liquid to solid).

**Extended**: the forces and distances between particles, and how the particles move, explain these properties. Particles means atoms, molecules, ions and electrons.

## Temperature and particles
- When temperature rises, particles move faster.
- **Absolute zero** is −273 °C, the lowest possible temperature. At this temperature particles have the least kinetic energy.

## Kelvin scale
- **T (in K) = θ (in °C) + 273**
- 0 °C = 273 K and 100 °C = 373 K.

## Gas pressure
- Gas particles move and collide with the walls of the container. Each collision pushes on the wall.
- **Extended**: particles colliding with surfaces exert a force. This creates a force per unit area, which is pressure.

**Effect of temperature (constant volume)**: particles move faster and hit the walls harder and more often, so pressure **increases**.

**Effect of volume (constant temperature)**: a smaller volume means particles hit the walls more often, so pressure **increases**.

## Boyle's law (Extended)
- For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, **pV = constant**.
- Also written p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
- A graph of p against V is a curve. A graph of p against 1/V is a straight line through the origin.
- Worked example: 200 cm³ of gas at 100 kPa is squeezed to 50 cm³. p₂ = 100 × 200 / 50 = **400 kPa**.

## Brownian motion
- Microscopic particles in a suspension, such as smoke in air or pollen in water, move in a jerky, random way.
- They are hit at random by the particles of the gas or liquid. This is evidence for the kinetic particle model.
- **Extended**: microscopic particles are moved by collisions with **light, fast-moving molecules**. Use the words atoms or molecules for the gas or liquid, and microscopic particles for the smoke or pollen.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Say that particles collide **more often** or **more frequently**. Do not only say that there are more collisions.
- Particles do not expand when heated. They move further apart.
- Add 273 when changing °C to K.

## Quick self-check
1. Convert 27 °C to kelvin. *(300 K)*
2. Why does heating a sealed can raise the pressure? *(Particles move faster and hit the walls harder and more often.)*
3. A gas at 150 kPa has a volume of 40 cm³. Find its volume at 300 kPa, with temperature constant. *(20 cm³)*
