# 5 Chemical energetics

## Key definitions (standard conditions: 298 K, 101 kPa, 1 mol dm⁻³ solutions, substances in their standard states)
- **Enthalpy change (ΔH)**: the heat energy change at constant pressure. **Exothermic**: ΔH is negative. **Endothermic**: ΔH is positive.
- **Standard enthalpy change of reaction (ΔH_r)**: the enthalpy change when the amounts shown in the equation react under standard conditions.
- **Standard enthalpy change of formation (ΔH_f)**: the enthalpy change when **one mole** of a compound is formed from its **elements in their standard states** under standard conditions.
- **Standard enthalpy change of combustion (ΔH_c)**: the enthalpy change when **one mole** of a substance is **completely burned in oxygen** under standard conditions.
- **Standard enthalpy change of neutralisation (ΔH_neut)**: the enthalpy change when acid and alkali react to form **one mole of water** under standard conditions.
- **Activation energy (Eₐ)**: the **minimum energy** colliding particles need in order to react.
- **Bond energy**: the energy needed to break **one mole** of a specified covalent bond in the **gaseous** state.

## Energy profile diagrams
- Exothermic: the products are lower than the reactants. Endothermic: the products are higher.
- Eₐ is the gap from the reactants to the peak (the transition state).

## Calorimetry
- **q = mcΔT** (m = mass of the **solution or water** heated, c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹).
- **ΔH = −q / n** (moles of the limiting reactant). Include the sign: a temperature **rise** means ΔH is negative.
- Sources of error: heat loss to the surroundings, incomplete combustion, heat absorbed by the container, and evaporation. Fix by insulating, using a lid, and drawing a cooling curve to extrapolate to the maximum temperature.

## Hess's law
- **Hess's law**: the total enthalpy change for a reaction is **independent of the route taken**, provided the initial and final conditions are the same.
- **Using ΔH_f**: ΔH_r = ΣΔH_f(products) − ΣΔH_f(reactants).
- **Using ΔH_c**: ΔH_r = ΣΔH_c(reactants) − ΣΔH_c(products).
- **Using bond energies**: ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds made).
  - The answer is only approximate, because **mean** bond energies are averages across many compounds, and the substances must be gaseous.

## Worked example
Find ΔH_f of methane, CH₄, given ΔH_c: C = −394, H₂ = −286, CH₄ = −890 kJ mol⁻¹.
C + 2H₂ → CH₄: ΔH = [−394 + 2(−286)] − (−890) = **−76 kJ mol⁻¹**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In definitions, state **one mole** and **standard conditions**, and for ΔH_f, elements in their standard states.
- In calorimetry, m is the mass of the **water or solution**, not the mass of the fuel.
- Always give the **sign** and units (kJ mol⁻¹).
- Bond energy data gives **approximate** values; explain why if asked.
