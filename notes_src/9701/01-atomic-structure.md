# 1 Atomic structure

## Particles in the atom
| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | 1 | +1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 1 | 0 | Nucleus |
| Electron | 1/1836 | −1 | Orbitals around the nucleus |

- **Behaviour in an electric field**: protons are deflected towards the negative plate and electrons towards the positive plate, with electrons deflected far more because their mass is much smaller. Neutrons are not deflected.
- **Proton (atomic) number Z**: the number of protons. **Nucleon (mass) number A**: the number of protons + neutrons.
- **Isotopes**: atoms of the same element with the **same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons**. They have the same chemical properties (same electron configuration) but different physical properties (mass, density).

## Electrons: shells, subshells and orbitals
- **Orbital**: a region in space that can hold up to **2 electrons with opposite spins**.
- s orbitals are **spherical**; p orbitals are **dumbbell-shaped** (px, py, pz).
- Subshells: s (1 orbital, 2 e⁻), p (3, 6 e⁻), d (5, 10 e⁻).
- **Filling order**: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p **4s 3d** 4p. The 4s subshell fills before 3d, but **4s electrons are lost first** when ions form.
- Electrons occupy orbitals of the same energy **singly** before pairing (Hund's rule).
- **Exceptions**: Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (a half-filled or full d subshell is more stable).
- Example: Fe = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²; Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵.
- **Free radical**: a species with an unpaired electron.

## Ionisation energy
- **First ionisation energy**: the energy needed to remove **one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms** to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻.
- **Factors**:
  - **Nuclear charge**: more protons → stronger attraction → higher IE.
  - **Distance** of the electron from the nucleus (atomic radius): further away → weaker attraction → lower IE.
  - **Shielding** by inner electrons: more shielding → lower IE.
- **Successive IEs** always increase, because each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion. A **big jump** shows that an electron is being removed from a new shell nearer the nucleus; the position of the jump gives the group number.
- **Trend across a period**: generally **increases**, because the nuclear charge increases while the shielding is similar.
  - Dip from Group 2 to 13 (e.g. Be → B): the electron is removed from a 2p orbital, which is **higher in energy** (further from the nucleus) than 2s.
  - Dip from Group 15 to 16 (e.g. N → O): the electron in O is removed from a **paired** p orbital, and repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove.
- **Trend down a group**: **decreases**, because there are more shells, the electron is further from the nucleus, and there is more shielding.

## Atomic and ionic radii
- Across a period the atomic radius decreases (greater nuclear charge, same shielding).
- Positive ions are **smaller** than their atoms (a shell is lost, and the same nuclear charge pulls on fewer electrons). Negative ions are **larger** than their atoms (more electron repulsion).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In the IE definition, include: **one mole**, **gaseous** atoms, and **1+ ions**.
- Explain trends using **all three** factors: nuclear charge, distance, shielding.
- For transition-metal ions, remove the 4s electrons **before** the 3d electrons.
