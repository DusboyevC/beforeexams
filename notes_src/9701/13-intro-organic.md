# 13 An introduction to organic chemistry

## Formulae
- **Molecular formula**: the actual number of each type of atom (C₄H₁₀).
- **Structural formula**: shows the arrangement without drawing every bond (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃).
- **Displayed formula**: shows every atom and every bond.
- **Skeletal formula**: lines for the C–C bonds; C and H atoms on carbon are not shown.
- **Empirical formula**: the simplest whole-number ratio.
- **Homologous series**: compounds with the same functional group and general formula, where each member differs from the next by CH₂, with similar chemical properties and a trend in physical properties.
- **Functional group**: the atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.

## Naming (IUPAC)
Find the longest carbon chain (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-…), number it so the functional group or substituents get the **lowest numbers**, and list substituents alphabetically. For example, 2-methylbutane; but-2-ene; propan-2-ol; 2-chloropropane.

## Classifying carbon atoms and compounds
- **Primary, secondary, tertiary**: the C carrying the functional group is attached to 1, 2 or 3 other carbon atoms.

## Types of reaction and reagent
- **Homolytic fission**: each atom takes one electron of the bond, forming **free radicals**. **Heterolytic fission**: one atom takes both electrons, forming ions.
- **Nucleophile**: an electron-pair **donor** that attacks a δ+ carbon (OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃).
- **Electrophile**: an electron-pair **acceptor** that attacks an electron-rich region, such as a C=C bond (Br₂, H⁺, NO₂⁺).
- **Reaction types**: addition, substitution, elimination, hydrolysis, condensation, oxidation, reduction.
- **Curly arrows** show the movement of an **electron pair**. They start from a bond or a lone pair.

## Isomerism
- **Structural isomers**: the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
  - **Chain** isomerism (a different carbon skeleton), **positional** isomerism (the functional group in a different position), **functional group** isomerism (e.g. an alcohol and an ether).
- **Stereoisomers**: the same structural formula but a different spatial arrangement.
  - **Geometrical (cis/trans) isomerism**: needs **restricted rotation** about a C=C bond **and two different groups on each carbon** of the double bond.
  - **Optical isomerism**: needs a **chiral centre**, a carbon atom with **four different groups** attached. The two isomers are non-superimposable mirror images (**enantiomers**) that rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions. A 50:50 mixture is a **racemic mixture**, which is optically inactive.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In curly-arrow mechanisms, the arrow must start at the electron pair and point to where it goes. Show the lone pairs and partial charges.
- For cis/trans isomerism, give **both conditions**.
- Mark chiral centres with an asterisk (*) and draw the 3D enantiomers with wedges.

## Quick self-check
1. Draw and name the structural isomers of C₄H₁₀. *(butane, 2-methylpropane)*
2. Explain why but-2-ene shows geometrical isomerism but but-1-ene does not.
3. Identify the chiral centre in butan-2-ol.
