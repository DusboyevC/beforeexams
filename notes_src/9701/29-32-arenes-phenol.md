# 29–30 Arenes (benzene)

## Structure of benzene
- C₆H₆: a planar ring; every C–C bond length is equal (between single and double); bond angles 120°.
- Each carbon is sp² hybridised. The p orbitals overlap sideways to form a **delocalised π ring** above and below the plane.
- **Evidence**: all the bonds are the same length; the enthalpy of hydrogenation is **less exothermic** than expected for three C=C bonds (about −208 instead of −360 kJ mol⁻¹), showing it is more stable (delocalisation energy); it does **not** decolourise bromine water.
- Benzene undergoes **substitution, not addition**, because addition would destroy the stable delocalised system.

## Electrophilic substitution
| Reaction | Reagents and conditions | Electrophile |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitration** | Concentrated HNO₃ + concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C | NO₂⁺ (HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻) |
| **Halogenation** | Br₂ or Cl₂ with an AlCl₃, FeBr₃ or Fe catalyst | Br⁺ (formed with the halogen carrier) |
| **Friedel–Crafts alkylation** | RCl + AlCl₃ | R⁺ |
| **Friedel–Crafts acylation** | RCOCl + AlCl₃ | RCO⁺ |
- **Mechanism**: the electrophile attacks the delocalised ring and forms a positively charged intermediate (the ring is partly broken); then H⁺ is lost and the delocalised ring is restored.
- **Side chains**: methylbenzene + hot alkaline KMnO₄ (then acidify) gives **benzoic acid**. Methylbenzene + Cl₂ in UV light gives substitution on the **CH₃ group**, not the ring.
- **Directing effects**: –OH, –NH₂ and –CH₃ direct new groups to the 2- and 4-positions; –NO₂ and –COOH direct to the 3-position.

# 31 Halogenoarenes
- Chlorobenzene is **much less reactive** than chloroalkanes in nucleophilic substitution. A lone pair on Cl overlaps with the delocalised ring, which strengthens the C–Cl bond, and the electron-rich ring repels nucleophiles.

# 32 Phenol
- Phenol is **more acidic than alcohols** (but weaker than carboxylic acids). The negative charge of the phenoxide ion is **delocalised into the ring**, which stabilises it.
- **Reactions**: with NaOH → sodium phenoxide + water; with Na → H₂. **No reaction with carbonates**: phenol is not acidic enough.
- **With bromine water** at room temperature: a **white precipitate** of 2,4,6-tribromophenol forms, with no catalyst needed. A lone pair on O is **delocalised into the ring**, which increases the electron density and makes the ring more reactive.
- **Nitration** with **dilute** HNO₃ at room temperature gives 2- and 4-nitrophenol.
- **Acidity order**: carboxylic acid > phenol > water > alcohol.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In electrophilic substitution mechanisms, draw a **horseshoe** for the partially broken ring with + in the middle, and show the loss of H⁺.
- Explain the reactivity of phenol (greater) and chlorobenzene (lower) in terms of a **lone pair interacting with the ring**.
