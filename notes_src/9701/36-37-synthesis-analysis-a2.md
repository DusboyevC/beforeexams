# 36 Organic synthesis (A Level)
- Plan routes of 2–4 steps, giving the **reagent, conditions and intermediate** for each step.
- **Useful steps to add**:
  - benzene → nitrobenzene → phenylamine → diazonium salt → azo dye;
  - benzene → methylbenzene (Friedel–Crafts) → benzoic acid (KMnO₄);
  - acid → acyl chloride → ester or amide.
- **Chiral drugs**: often only one enantiomer is active. Making a single enantiomer (using chiral catalysts, enzymes, or a chiral starting material) **avoids side effects**, lowers the dose needed, and avoids separating a racemic mixture.

# 37 Analytical techniques (A Level)

## Chromatography
- **Rf = distance moved by the spot / distance moved by the solvent front**.
- **Thin-layer chromatography (TLC)**: the stationary phase is silica or alumina on a plate; the mobile phase is a solvent. Separation depends on the relative **adsorption** to the stationary phase versus solubility in the mobile phase. Colourless spots are located with UV light, iodine or ninhydrin (for amino acids).
- **Gas–liquid chromatography (GLC)**: the mobile phase is an inert carrier gas; the stationary phase is a non-volatile liquid on a solid support. Separation depends on the **partition** between the gas and the liquid and on volatility. Each component has a characteristic **retention time**, and the **area of each peak is proportional to the amount** of that component.

## NMR spectroscopy
- **Proton (¹H) NMR**:
  - The **number of peaks** = the number of different H environments.
  - **Chemical shift (δ, in ppm)** shows the type of environment (e.g. R–CH₃ 0.9; CH₃–C=O 2.1; –O–CH₃ 3.3–3.8; arene H 6.5–8; aldehyde H 9.5–10; COOH 11–12).
  - The **integration** (peak area ratio) = the relative number of H atoms in each environment.
  - **Splitting (n + 1 rule)**: a peak is split into n + 1 lines by n H atoms on **adjacent** carbons (doublet, triplet, quartet…).
  - **O–H and N–H** protons: the peak disappears when **D₂O** is added (the H exchanges with D).
  - **TMS** (tetramethylsilane) is the reference at δ = 0: it is inert, volatile, and gives one strong peak upfield.
  - Solvents such as **CDCl₃** are used so the solvent itself gives no ¹H signal.
- **Carbon-13 NMR**: the number of peaks = the number of different carbon environments; the chemical shift shows the type (e.g. C=O 160–220 ppm).

## Worked example
¹H NMR of C₂H₅OH: three peaks. CH₃ (a triplet, 3H, δ ≈ 1.2); CH₂ (a quartet, 2H, δ ≈ 3.6); OH (a singlet, 1H, which disappears with D₂O).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In NMR analysis, list each peak's **shift, integration, splitting and adjacent H count** before you propose a structure.
- In GLC, the retention time identifies a component; the **peak area** gives the amount.
- Explain why D₂O is used: to identify O–H or N–H protons.
