# 24 Electrochemistry (A Level)

## Electrolysis
- **Faraday constant**: F = Le = 96 500 C mol⁻¹ (the charge on one mole of electrons).
- **Charge**: Q = It. Moles of electrons = Q/F; use the half-equation to find the moles of product.
- **Products of aqueous electrolysis**:
  - **Cathode**: the less reactive metal is deposited (e.g. Cu, Ag); for reactive metals (above H), H₂ forms instead.
  - **Anode**: O₂ forms, unless the halide is concentrated (Cl⁻ gives Cl₂) or the anode is an active metal (Cu dissolves).
- **Determining the Avogadro constant by electrolysis**: weigh a copper cathode before and after passing a measured current for a measured time. Moles of Cu = Δm/63.5, and moles of electrons = 2 × moles of Cu. Then **L = Q/(e × moles of electrons)**.

## Standard electrode potentials
- **Standard electrode potential (E°)**: the e.m.f. of a half-cell measured against the **standard hydrogen electrode** under standard conditions (298 K, 1 mol dm⁻³, 101 kPa).
- **Standard hydrogen electrode**: H₂ gas at 101 kPa bubbling over a **platinum** electrode in 1.00 mol dm⁻³ H⁺, at 298 K. Defined as 0.00 V.
- For ion/ion half-cells (e.g. Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺), use a **Pt** electrode in a solution with both ions at 1 mol dm⁻³.
- A **salt bridge** (filter paper soaked in KNO₃) completes the circuit and allows ions to move, without mixing the solutions.
- **E°cell = E°(positive, right) − E°(negative, left)**.
- **Feasibility**: a reaction is feasible if **E°cell > 0**. The more positive half-cell is reduced, and its species is the stronger **oxidising agent**.
- **Limitation**: the reaction may still be too slow (high Eₐ), and the conditions may not be standard.

## Nernst equation
**E = E° + (0.059/z) log([oxidised form]/[reduced form])**
- For a metal ion and its metal: E = E° + (0.059/z) log[Mⁿ⁺].
- **Decreasing [Mⁿ⁺]** makes E less positive.
- Qualitatively, use Le Chatelier: increasing the concentration of the oxidised species makes E more positive.
- **Relationship**: ΔG° = −nE°cell F.

## Batteries and fuel cells
- **Fuel cell**: converts the chemical energy of a fuel (e.g. H₂) and an oxidant directly into electricity. For the hydrogen fuel cell, the only product is water.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Draw a standard cell **fully labelled**: Pt, H₂ at 1 atm, 1 mol dm⁻³ H⁺, the salt bridge, a voltmeter, and 298 K.
- When combining half-equations, **don't multiply E°** by the stoichiometric numbers.
- In Faraday calculations, include the **number of electrons** in the half-equation.

## Quick self-check
1. Find the mass of copper deposited by 2.00 A for 30.0 min. *(1.18 g)*
2. Given E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V and E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76 V, find E°cell and identify the positive electrode. *(1.10 V; Cu)*
3. Predict how E for Cu²⁺/Cu changes if [Cu²⁺] is reduced to 0.010 mol dm⁻³. *(more negative: E = 0.34 + 0.0295 log 0.01 = 0.28 V)*
