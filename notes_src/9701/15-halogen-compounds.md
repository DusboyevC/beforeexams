# 15 Halogen compounds (halogenoalkanes)

## Making halogenoalkanes
- Free-radical substitution of an alkane; electrophilic addition of HX or X₂ to an alkene.
- From alcohols: HX (made from KBr + H₂SO₄), PCl₃ or PCl₅, or **SOCl₂** (the by-products SO₂ and HCl are gases, so the product is easy to separate).

## Nucleophilic substitution
The C–X bond is **polar** (C is δ+), so nucleophiles attack the carbon.
| Nucleophile / conditions | Product |
|---|---|
| NaOH(aq), heat under reflux | Alcohol (hydrolysis) |
| KCN in ethanol, heat | **Nitrile** (adds one carbon to the chain) |
| Excess NH₃ in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube | Amine |

**Mechanisms**
- **S_N2** (mainly **primary** halogenoalkanes): a **one-step** reaction. The OH⁻ attacks the δ+ C from the side **opposite** the halogen, through a **transition state** with five groups around the carbon (show it in brackets with a negative charge); X⁻ leaves. The rate depends on both [RX] and [OH⁻].
- **S_N1** (mainly **tertiary** halogenoalkanes): **two steps**. The C–X bond breaks heterolytically, forming a **tertiary carbocation** (the slow step); OH⁻ then attacks the carbocation. The rate depends only on [RX].
- Tertiary halogenoalkanes use S_N1 because the tertiary carbocation is **stabilised** by three electron-donating alkyl groups, and the crowded carbon hinders the attack needed for S_N2.

## Reactivity and the C–X bond
- The rate of hydrolysis is **RI > RBr > RCl**. Down the group the C–X bond gets **weaker** (longer), so it breaks more easily, even though it is less polar.
- **Experiment**: warm with **aqueous AgNO₃ in ethanol** and time how long the precipitate takes to appear. The iodo compound gives a precipitate fastest (yellow AgI); the chloro compound slowest (white AgCl).

## Elimination
- **NaOH in ethanol**, heated: HX is eliminated, forming an **alkene**. (OH⁻ acts as a **base**.)
- Conditions decide the reaction: **aqueous** gives substitution; **ethanolic** gives elimination.

## Uses and environment
- CFCs were used in refrigerants and aerosols because they are unreactive, non-toxic and non-flammable.
- In the stratosphere, **UV breaks the C–Cl bond** to form Cl• radicals, which **catalyse the breakdown of ozone**:
  - Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂
  - ClO• + O₃ → Cl• + 2O₂
  One chlorine radical can destroy thousands of O₃ molecules. The replacements (HFCs) contain no C–Cl bonds.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- S_N2: show the attack on the opposite side, the transition state, and the inversion of configuration. S_N1: show the carbocation intermediate.
- State the **conditions** precisely: aqueous NaOH under reflux versus NaOH in ethanol.
- Explain reactivity with **C–X bond strength**, not polarity.
