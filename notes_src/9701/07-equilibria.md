# 7 Equilibria (AS)

## Dynamic equilibrium
- **Dynamic equilibrium**: in a **closed system**, the **rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal** and the **concentrations of reactants and products stay constant**.
- **Le Chatelier's principle**: if a change is made to a system at equilibrium, the position of equilibrium moves to **minimise (oppose) the change**.

## Effect of changing conditions
| Change | Effect on the position | Effect on K |
|---|---|---|
| Increase the concentration of a reactant | Shifts right (more product forms) | No change |
| Increase the pressure (gases) | Shifts to the side with **fewer gas moles** | No change |
| Increase the temperature | Shifts in the **endothermic** direction | **Changes** (increases if the forward reaction is endothermic) |
| Add a catalyst | No change; equilibrium is reached **faster** | No change |

## Equilibrium constants
- For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD: **Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ)**. Work out its units for each expression.
- **Kp** (gases): uses **partial pressures**: Kp = p(C)ᶜ p(D)ᵈ / (p(A)ᵃ p(B)ᵇ).
- **Partial pressure** = mole fraction × total pressure.
- **Only temperature changes the value of K.**
- **ICE table method**: initial moles → change (using the equation ratio) → equilibrium moles → divide by volume for concentration → substitute into Kc.

## Industrial processes
- **Haber process**: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹. Conditions: about 200 atm, 400–450 °C, iron catalyst.
  - The **compromise temperature**: a lower temperature gives a higher yield, but the rate is too slow.
  - The **pressure**: a higher pressure gives a higher yield and rate, but costs more and is a safety risk.
- **Contact process**: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, ΔH negative. Conditions: about 1–2 atm, 450 °C, V₂O₅ catalyst. (High pressure isn't needed, because the yield is already high.)

## Acids and bases (Brønsted–Lowry)
- **Acid**: a proton (H⁺) donor. **Base**: a proton acceptor.
- **Conjugate pairs**: HA / A⁻.
- **Strong acid**: fully dissociated in water (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄). **Weak acid**: partially dissociated (CH₃COOH).
- Strong and weak acids differ in **degree of dissociation**; concentrated and dilute differ in amount per volume.
- **pH = −log[H⁺]**. For a strong acid, [H⁺] = [acid] (× 2 for the second proton of H₂SO₄, roughly).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- The definition of dynamic equilibrium needs **closed system**, **equal rates**, and **constant concentrations**.
- In Le Chatelier answers, say what changes **and** why: "pressure increases, so equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer gas moles, to reduce the pressure".
- A catalyst does **not** change the yield or K.
