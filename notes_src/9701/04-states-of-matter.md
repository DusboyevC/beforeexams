# 4 States of matter

## Gases
- **Ideal gas assumptions** (kinetic theory): the molecules have **negligible volume**; there are **no intermolecular forces**; collisions are **perfectly elastic**; the molecules move randomly.
- **pV = nRT** (p in Pa, V in m³, T in K, R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹).
- **Finding Mᵣ**: Mᵣ = mRT/(pV).
- Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour at **high pressure** (the molecules' own volume becomes significant compared with the container volume) and **low temperature** (intermolecular forces become significant because the molecules move slowly).
- The most ideal gases are small, non-polar molecules with few electrons (e.g. He).

## Lattice structures
| Structure | Particles | Forces | Properties | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Giant ionic** | Ions | Ionic bonds | High mp; conduct when molten or in solution (mobile ions); often soluble in water; brittle | NaCl, MgO |
| **Simple molecular** | Molecules | Weak intermolecular forces | Low mp; do not conduct; usually insoluble in water | I₂, ice, CO₂ |
| **Giant molecular (covalent)** | Atoms | Strong covalent bonds | Very high mp; hard; insoluble | Diamond, graphite, SiO₂ |
| **Giant metallic** | Positive ions + delocalised electrons | Metallic bonds | High mp; conduct (delocalised electrons); malleable | Cu, Fe |

- **Diamond**: each C is bonded to 4 others (tetrahedral). Very hard; does not conduct.
- **Graphite**: each C is bonded to 3 others in layers, with one **delocalised electron** per atom. It **conducts**, and the layers slide over each other because only weak forces act between them.
- **Ice**: a hydrogen-bonded open lattice, so it is less dense than water.
- **Explaining melting point**: the stronger the bonds or forces between the particles, the more energy is needed to overcome them.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Melting a simple molecular solid overcomes **intermolecular forces**, not covalent bonds.
- To conduct, there must be **mobile charged particles**: electrons or ions.
- Use pV = nRT with SI units throughout.
