# 2 Atoms, molecules and stoichiometry

## Key definitions
- **Unified atomic mass unit**: one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
- **Relative atomic mass (Aᵣ)**: the weighted mean mass of an atom of an element, compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
- **Relative isotopic mass**: the mass of one atom of an isotope compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
- **Relative molecular mass (Mᵣ)** and **relative formula mass**: the sum of the Aᵣ values of all the atoms in the formula.
- **Mole**: the amount of substance containing **6.02 × 10²³** particles (the Avogadro constant, L).
- **Empirical formula**: the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
- **Molecular formula**: the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.

## Calculating Aᵣ from a mass spectrum
Aᵣ = Σ(isotopic mass × abundance) / Σ abundance
Example: Cl has ³⁵Cl at 75% and ³⁷Cl at 25%. Aᵣ = (35 × 75 + 37 × 25)/100 = **35.5**.

## Mole calculations
- **n = m/M**
- **n = c × V** (c in mol dm⁻³, V in dm³; 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³)
- **Gases**: 1 mol occupies **24.0 dm³ at room conditions** (r.t.p.). **pV = nRT** (p in Pa, V in m³, T in K).
- **Percentage yield** = actual yield / theoretical yield × 100.
- **Atom economy** = Mᵣ of the desired product / total Mᵣ of all products × 100.

## Empirical formula method
1. Divide the mass (or %) of each element by its Aᵣ.
2. Divide each result by the smallest.
3. Round to whole numbers (multiply if you get values such as 1.5 or 1.33).
- Example: C 40.0%, H 6.7%, O 53.3% → 3.33 : 6.7 : 3.33 → 1 : 2 : 1 → **CH₂O**. If Mᵣ = 60, the molecular formula is C₂H₄O₂.

## Equations
- Balance equations with state symbols. Write **ionic equations** by removing the spectator ions.
- **Titration calculations**: find the moles of the known reagent → use the equation ratio → find the moles of the unknown → find its concentration or mass.
- **Limiting reagent**: compare the mole ratio needed with the moles available.

## Worked example
25.0 cm³ of NaOH is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄. Find [NaOH].
n(H₂SO₄) = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol; ratio 2 NaOH : 1 H₂SO₄ → n(NaOH) = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
[NaOH] = 4.00 × 10⁻³ / 0.0250 = **0.160 mol dm⁻³**

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Convert cm³ to dm³ (÷ 1000) for concentrations, and to m³ (÷ 10⁶) for pV = nRT.
- Use the **mole ratio** from the balanced equation; don't skip this step.
- Give answers to the same number of significant figures as the data (usually 3).
