# 17 Carbonyl compounds (aldehydes and ketones)

## Structure and preparation
- Aldehyde: RCHO (the C=O is at the end of the chain). Ketone: RCOR′ (the C=O is in the middle).
- Made by oxidising alcohols: primary → aldehyde (distil); secondary → ketone (reflux), using acidified K₂Cr₂O₇.

## Reactions
- **Reduction** with **NaBH₄** (or LiAlH₄): an aldehyde gives a **primary alcohol**; a ketone gives a **secondary alcohol**.
- **Nucleophilic addition of HCN** (with KCN and a trace of acid, or HCN with a CN⁻ catalyst) gives a **hydroxynitrile**.
  - **Mechanism**: the CN⁻ nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon; the π bond breaks and the electrons move to O, forming O⁻; the O⁻ takes H⁺ from HCN (regenerating CN⁻).
  - With an aldehyde or an unsymmetrical ketone, a **racemic mixture** forms, because the planar C=O can be attacked from **either side** equally.

## Tests
| Test | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|---|---|---|
| **2,4-DNPH (Brady's reagent)** | **Orange precipitate** | **Orange precipitate** (tests for C=O) |
| **Tollens' reagent** (warm) | **Silver mirror** | No change |
| **Fehling's solution** (warm) | **Brick-red precipitate** | Stays blue |
| Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (warm) | Orange → green | No change |
- The melting point of the purified 2,4-DNPH derivative identifies the specific carbonyl compound.
- **Tri-iodomethane test**: a yellow precipitate forms with **methyl ketones** (CH₃CO–) and with ethanal.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In the nucleophilic addition mechanism, the curly arrow goes from the **lone pair on C of CN⁻** to the carbonyl C, and from the C=O π bond to O.
- Explain the racemic product: the carbonyl group is **planar**, so it can be attacked equally from **above and below**.
- Tollens' and Fehling's distinguish aldehydes from ketones; 2,4-DNPH only shows that a C=O is present.
