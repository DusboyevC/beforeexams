# 3 Chemical bonding

## Types of bonding
- **Ionic bonding**: the **electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions**.
- **Metallic bonding**: the electrostatic attraction between **positive metal ions** and **delocalised electrons**.
- **Covalent bond**: the electrostatic attraction between the nuclei of two atoms and a **shared pair of electrons**.
- **Co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond**: a covalent bond in which **both electrons come from the same atom**, e.g. NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺, Al₂Cl₆.

## Electronegativity
- **Electronegativity**: the power of an atom to **attract the bonding electrons** in a covalent bond.
- It increases **across** a period (greater nuclear charge, smaller radius) and decreases **down** a group (larger radius, more shielding).
- A big electronegativity difference gives an ionic bond; a small difference gives a **polar covalent** bond (δ+ and δ−); no difference gives a non-polar bond.

## Orbital overlap
- **σ bond**: formed by **direct (end-on) overlap** of orbitals along the line between the nuclei.
- **π bond**: formed by **sideways overlap** of p orbitals, with electron density above and below the line between the nuclei. It is weaker than a σ bond.
- A double bond = 1σ + 1π; a triple bond = 1σ + 2π.
- **Hybridisation**: sp³ (tetrahedral, e.g. CH₄), sp² (trigonal planar, e.g. C₂H₄), sp (linear, e.g. C₂H₂).

## Shapes of molecules (VSEPR)
Electron pairs repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible. Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs: **lone–lone > lone–bond > bond–bond**.
| Bonding pairs + lone pairs | Shape | Angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 + 0 | Linear | 180° | CO₂, BeCl₂ |
| 3 + 0 | Trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃ |
| 4 + 0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, NH₄⁺ |
| 3 + 1 | Pyramidal | 107° | NH₃ |
| 2 + 2 | Non-linear (bent) | 104.5° | H₂O |
| 5 + 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90°, 120° | PCl₅ |
| 6 + 0 | Octahedral | 90° | SF₆ |

## Intermolecular forces
- **Instantaneous dipole–induced dipole (London) forces**: present between **all** molecules. Electrons move randomly, creating a temporary dipole that induces a dipole in a neighbouring molecule. They are **stronger with more electrons** and with greater surface contact (straight chains more than branched).
- **Permanent dipole–permanent dipole forces**: between polar molecules.
- **Hydrogen bonding**: between an H atom bonded to **N, O or F** and a lone pair on an N, O or F atom of another molecule. It is the strongest intermolecular force.
  - **Anomalous properties of water**: a high boiling point; ice is **less dense** than water (an open lattice held by hydrogen bonds); high surface tension.
- A molecule is **polar overall** only if it has polar bonds **and** an unsymmetrical shape (CO₂ has polar bonds but is non-polar).

## Bond energy and bond length
- **Bond energy**: the energy needed to break **one mole of a particular covalent bond in the gaseous state**.
- Shorter bonds are generally stronger (e.g. C≡C is stronger than C=C, which is stronger than C–C).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In shape questions, state the **number of bonding pairs and lone pairs**, then the shape and angle.
- Draw hydrogen bonds with the **lone pair** shown and a linear arrangement of the atoms.
- Breaking a covalent molecular substance on boiling breaks **intermolecular forces**, not covalent bonds.
