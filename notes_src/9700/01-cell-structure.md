# 1 Cell structure

## Microscopy
- **Magnification** = image size ÷ actual size. Convert to the same units: 1 mm = 1000 μm; 1 μm = 1000 nm.
- **Resolution**: the ability to **distinguish between two points that are close together** as separate.
- **Light microscope**: resolution about **200 nm** (limited by the wavelength of light), maximum magnification about ×1500. Specimens can be living, and colour is seen.
- **Electron microscope**: resolution about **0.5 nm** (electrons have a much shorter wavelength), so it shows ultrastructure. Specimens are dead, in a vacuum, and the image is black and white.
  - **TEM**: electrons pass **through** a thin section, giving a 2D image of internal detail.
  - **SEM**: electrons bounce **off** the surface, giving a 3D image of the surface.
- **Eyepiece graticule** calibrated against a **stage micrometer**: find how many μm one eyepiece unit (epu) represents at that magnification, then measure the cells in epu.

## Cell structures and functions
| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Nucleus** (nuclear envelope with pores, nucleolus, chromatin) | Contains DNA and controls the cell; the nucleolus makes ribosomes |
| **Rough endoplasmic reticulum** | Has ribosomes; makes and transports proteins |
| **Smooth ER** | Makes lipids and steroids; detoxification |
| **Golgi body** | Modifies and packages proteins (e.g. glycosylation); forms vesicles and lysosomes |
| **Mitochondria** (double membrane, cristae, matrix) | Aerobic respiration, making ATP |
| **Ribosomes**: 80S in the cytoplasm, 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts | Protein synthesis (translation) |
| **Lysosomes** | Contain hydrolytic enzymes; break down old organelles and pathogens |
| **Centrioles** | Organise the spindle microtubules in cell division (animal cells) |
| **Microtubules** | Cytoskeleton; spindle; movement of organelles and vesicles; cilia |
| **Microvilli** | Folds of the cell surface membrane that increase the surface area for absorption |
| **Cilia** | Beat to move fluids or mucus over the cell surface |
| **Chloroplasts** (plants) | Photosynthesis (thylakoids, grana, stroma) |
| **Cell wall** (cellulose, in plants) | Support and prevents bursting |
| **Large permanent vacuole** (plants) | Contains cell sap; keeps cells turgid |
| **Plasmodesmata** | Cytoplasmic connections between plant cells |

## ATP
- ATP is the **universal energy currency**. It is a phosphorylated nucleotide (adenine + ribose + 3 phosphates). Hydrolysis to ADP + Pᵢ releases energy in small, usable amounts.

## Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
| Prokaryote (bacteria) | Eukaryote |
|---|---|
| No nucleus; circular DNA free in the cytoplasm; plasmids | DNA in a nucleus; linear chromosomes with histones |
| 70S ribosomes | 80S ribosomes |
| No membrane-bound organelles | Mitochondria, ER, Golgi etc. |
| Cell wall made of **peptidoglycan (murein)** | Cellulose wall (plants), chitin (fungi), or none (animals) |
| Small (1–5 μm) | Larger (10–100 μm) |
| May have a capsule, flagella, pili | — |

## Viruses
- **Non-cellular**: a nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) core surrounded by a protein **capsid**; some have an outer **envelope** (a lipid membrane from the host). They can only reproduce **inside host cells**.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Magnification calculations: convert units **first**, and give the answer as ×number.
- Resolution is not the same as magnification. Electron microscopes have higher **resolution** because electrons have a **shorter wavelength**.
- Name **specific** organelles and functions, e.g. "rough ER – the site of protein synthesis and transport", not just "makes things".

## Quick self-check
1. A mitochondrion measures 25 mm in a ×10 000 micrograph. Find its actual length in μm. *(2.5 μm)*
2. State three differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
3. Explain why ribosomes are visible with an electron microscope but not with a light microscope.
