# 12 Energy and respiration

## ATP
- ATP is used for active transport, muscle contraction, anabolic reactions (synthesis), and nerve impulses.
- It is made by **substrate-level phosphorylation** (in glycolysis and the Krebs cycle) and by **oxidative phosphorylation** (in the electron transport chain).
- **Respiratory substrates** give different amounts of energy: lipids (about 39 kJ g⁻¹) > proteins (about 17) ≈ carbohydrates (about 16). Lipids have more hydrogen atoms per molecule, so they produce more reduced NAD.
- **Respiratory quotient (RQ)** = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ used. Carbohydrate = 1.0; lipid ≈ 0.7; protein ≈ 0.9.

## Aerobic respiration
1. **Glycolysis** (in the cytoplasm): glucose is phosphorylated (2 ATP used) and split into 2 triose phosphate, which is oxidised to **2 pyruvate**. Net yield: **2 ATP + 2 reduced NAD**.
2. **Link reaction** (mitochondrial matrix): pyruvate is decarboxylated (CO₂ released) and dehydrogenated (forming reduced NAD), then combined with coenzyme A to give **acetyl CoA**.
3. **Krebs cycle** (matrix): acetyl (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → citrate (6C). In the cycle, **decarboxylation** releases 2 CO₂, and **dehydrogenation** produces 3 reduced NAD and 1 reduced FAD per turn, plus 1 ATP (substrate-level). Oxaloacetate is regenerated.
4. **Oxidative phosphorylation** (inner membrane and cristae): reduced NAD and FAD release hydrogen, which splits into H⁺ and electrons.
   - The electrons pass along the **electron transport chain**, releasing energy that is used to **pump H⁺ into the intermembrane space**.
   - H⁺ flows back through **ATP synthase**, which makes ATP. This is **chemiosmosis**.
   - **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor**: it combines with electrons and H⁺ to form **water**.

## Anaerobic respiration
- Without O₂, the electron transport chain stops, so reduced NAD is not reoxidised. **NAD must be regenerated** for glycolysis to continue.
  - **Mammals**: pyruvate → **lactate** (lactate dehydrogenase). This builds up an **oxygen debt**; the lactate is later converted back in the liver.
  - **Yeast and plants**: pyruvate → **ethanol + CO₂** (via ethanal).
- **Rice adaptations** to flooded conditions: aerenchyma tissue (air spaces), tolerance of ethanol, and faster growth of the stems above the water.

# 13 Photosynthesis

## Chloroplast structure
- **Thylakoids** stacked into **grana** (the light-dependent reactions), and the **stroma** (the Calvin cycle). Pigments are arranged in **photosystems**.
- **Pigments**: chlorophyll a and b, carotenoids. They absorb different wavelengths, which widens the range of light used. They can be separated by **chromatography** (calculate Rf values).
- **Absorption spectrum**: the light absorbed by the pigments. **Action spectrum**: the rate of photosynthesis at each wavelength.

## Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes)
- **Photolysis of water**: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂. Light energy splits the water.
- Light excites electrons in the photosystems, and the electrons pass along an electron transport chain, which pumps H⁺ into the thylakoid space. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase, making ATP (**photophosphorylation**).
  - **Non-cyclic**: involves PSII and PSI; produces ATP + **reduced NADP** + O₂.
  - **Cyclic**: involves PSI only; produces ATP only.

## Light-independent reactions: the Calvin cycle (stroma)
1. **CO₂ fixation**: CO₂ + **RuBP** (5C), catalysed by **rubisco**, gives 2 × **GP** (3C).
2. **Reduction**: GP is reduced to **triose phosphate (TP)**, using **ATP and reduced NADP**.
3. **Regeneration**: most TP is used to regenerate RuBP (using ATP); some is used to make glucose, starch, amino acids and lipids.

## Limiting factors
- The rate is limited by whichever factor is in **shortest supply**: light intensity, CO₂ concentration, or temperature.
- **Glasshouses**: supply extra CO₂, lighting and heating to raise yields.
- **Experiment**: use pondweed and count the O₂ bubbles (or use a redox indicator such as DCPIP), varying the light intensity by changing the distance of the lamp (intensity ∝ 1/d²).

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Give **locations** for each stage (cytoplasm, matrix, cristae, thylakoids, stroma).
- Oxygen's role in respiration: the **final electron acceptor**, forming water.
- In the Calvin cycle, name the molecules (RuBP, GP, TP) and what ATP and reduced NADP are used for.
