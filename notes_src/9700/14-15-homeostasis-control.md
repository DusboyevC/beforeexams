# 14 Homeostasis

## Principles
- **Homeostasis**: maintaining a **constant internal environment** (temperature, blood glucose, water potential) within narrow limits, even when external conditions change.
- **Negative feedback**: a change from the set point is detected by **receptors**, and **effectors** bring about a response that **reverses** the change.

## Excretion and the kidney
- **Urea** is made in the liver by **deamination** of excess amino acids (followed by the ornithine cycle).
- **Ultrafiltration** (in the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule): a high hydrostatic pressure (the afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent) forces small molecules (water, glucose, urea, ions) through the capillary endothelium, the **basement membrane** (the filter) and the **podocytes**. Proteins and cells stay in the blood.
- **Selective reabsorption** (in the proximal convoluted tubule): **all the glucose** and amino acids, and much of the water and ions, are reabsorbed, by **co-transport with Na⁺** (driven by a Na⁺ gradient set up by Na⁺/K⁺ pumps). The cells have microvilli and many mitochondria.
- **Osmoregulation**: osmoreceptors in the **hypothalamus** detect a low blood water potential, so the **posterior pituitary releases ADH**. ADH makes the collecting duct cells insert **aquaporins** into their membranes, which increases water reabsorption, so a small volume of concentrated urine is produced.

## Blood glucose
- **High glucose** → the **β-cells** of the islets of Langerhans release **insulin** → cells take up more glucose (more GLUT4 transporters); the liver converts glucose to glycogen (**glycogenesis**).
- **Low glucose** → the **α-cells** release **glucagon** → the liver breaks down glycogen to glucose (**glycogenolysis**) and makes glucose from other compounds (**gluconeogenesis**).
- **Second messenger**: glucagon binds to a receptor, which activates adenylyl cyclase to make **cyclic AMP**, which activates a cascade of enzymes (amplification).
- **Diabetes**: tested with **test strips / biosensors** (glucose oxidase).

## Plants
- **Stomatal control**: **abscisic acid (ABA)**, released in water stress, causes the guard cells to lose K⁺ and water, so the **stomata close**.

# 15 Control and coordination

## Nervous system
- **Resting potential** (about −70 mV): maintained by the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in) and by the membrane being more permeable to K⁺ (K⁺ leaks out).
- **Action potential**:
  1. A stimulus opens **voltage-gated Na⁺ channels**, Na⁺ floods in, and the membrane **depolarises** (to about +40 mV).
  2. The Na⁺ channels close and **K⁺ channels open**, K⁺ leaves, and the membrane **repolarises**.
  3. There is a brief **hyperpolarisation**, then the resting potential is restored.
- **All-or-nothing** response: a threshold must be reached. A stronger stimulus gives a **higher frequency** of impulses, not bigger ones.
- **Refractory period**: makes impulses one-way and separate, and limits their frequency.
- **Myelination**: Schwann cells insulate the axon, so depolarisation happens only at the **nodes of Ranvier**. The impulse jumps between nodes (**saltatory conduction**), which is much faster.
- **Synapse (cholinergic)**:
  1. The action potential arrives at the presynaptic knob and Ca²⁺ channels open, so Ca²⁺ enters.
  2. Vesicles fuse with the membrane and release **acetylcholine** by exocytosis.
  3. ACh diffuses across the cleft and binds to receptors, opening Na⁺ channels, which depolarises the postsynaptic membrane.
  4. **Acetylcholinesterase** breaks down the ACh, so the signal stops.
- **Muscle contraction (sliding filament model)**: an action potential triggers the release of Ca²⁺ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Ca²⁺ binds to **troponin**, which moves **tropomyosin** and exposes the binding sites on actin. **Myosin heads** form cross-bridges, perform the power stroke, and detach using **ATP**. The sarcomere shortens: the H zone and I band narrow, while the A band stays the same length.

## Hormones
- Endocrine glands secrete hormones into the blood, which travel to target cells with specific receptors.
- **Menstrual cycle**: FSH and LH (from the anterior pituitary), oestrogen and progesterone (from the ovaries), with feedback between them. Contraceptive pills contain synthetic oestrogen and/or progesterone, which inhibit FSH and LH release, so there is no ovulation.

## Plant responses
- **Auxin (IAA)**: promotes cell elongation. **Gibberellin**: promotes stem elongation and seed germination (it activates amylase production in seeds).
- **Venus flytrap**: when the trigger hairs are touched twice, action potentials cause rapid changes in turgor, and the trap closes.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Use the exact terms: **ultrafiltration**, **selective reabsorption**, **aquaporins**, **ADH**.
- For action potentials, name **which ion moves, through which channels, and in which direction**.
- The role of ATP in muscle is to **detach** myosin from actin (and to re-cock the myosin head).
