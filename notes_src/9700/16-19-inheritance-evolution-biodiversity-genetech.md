# 16 Inheritance

## Meiosis
- **Meiosis** produces **four genetically different haploid** cells (gametes). It halves the chromosome number, so the diploid number is restored at fertilisation.
- **Sources of variation**:
  - **Crossing over** in prophase I: non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes exchange alleles at the **chiasmata**.
  - **Independent assortment** in metaphase I: homologous pairs line up randomly.
  - **Random fertilisation**.
- **Homologous chromosomes**: pairs with the same genes at the same loci (one maternal, one paternal).

## Genetics
- **Allele**: an alternative form of a gene. **Genotype**: the alleles present. **Phenotype**: the observable characteristics.
- **Dominant / recessive**; **codominance** (both alleles are expressed, e.g. the I^A I^B blood group); **multiple alleles**; **sex linkage** (genes on the X chromosome, e.g. haemophilia: X^H X^h is a carrier).
- **Monohybrid** 3 : 1; **dihybrid** 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
- **Autosomal linkage**: genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together, so offspring ratios differ from the expected values (recombinants arise only by crossing over).
- **Epistasis**: one gene affects the expression of another.
- **Chi-squared test**: χ² = Σ(O − E)²/E. Degrees of freedom = number of categories − 1. If χ² > the critical value at p = 0.05, the difference is **significant**, i.e. not due to chance.
- **Gene control**: in prokaryotes, the **lac operon** is switched on when lactose binds to the repressor. In eukaryotes, **transcription factors** bind to promoters to switch genes on or off. Gibberellin causes the breakdown of DELLA proteins, which releases transcription factors.

# 17 Selection and evolution
- **Variation**: **discontinuous** (distinct categories; one or a few genes; little environmental effect) versus **continuous** (a range of values; many genes (polygenic) plus the environment).
- **Natural selection**:
  1. There is variation, arising from mutation.
  2. A selection pressure acts (predation, disease, competition).
  3. Individuals with advantageous alleles **survive and reproduce** more.
  4. They pass on those alleles, so the **allele frequency** changes over generations.
- **Types**: stabilising, directional, disruptive selection. **Genetic drift**: random changes in allele frequency, important in small populations (the founder effect, bottlenecks).
- **Hardy–Weinberg**: p + q = 1 and **p² + 2pq + q² = 1**. Assumptions: a large population, random mating, no mutation, no migration, no selection.
- **Speciation**: populations become **reproductively isolated**.
  - **Allopatric**: geographical separation.
  - **Sympatric**: in the same place, e.g. polyploidy or behavioural isolation.
  - The separated gene pools are affected by different selection pressures until the populations can no longer interbreed to produce **fertile offspring**.
- **Artificial selection**: humans choose the parents with the desired traits, e.g. dairy cattle or disease-resistant wheat.

# 18 Classification, biodiversity and conservation
- **Taxonomic hierarchy**: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. **Three domains**: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
- **Biodiversity**: the variety of ecosystems, species, and genetic diversity within species.
- **Sampling**:
  - **Quadrats**: frequency, % cover, density.
  - **Line or belt transects**: show changes across a habitat.
  - **Mark–release–recapture**: N = (n₁ × n₂)/m.
- **Simpson's index of diversity**: D = 1 − Σ(n/N)². A value close to 1 means high diversity.
- **Spearman's rank correlation** and **Pearson's correlation coefficient**: measure the strength of an association between two variables.
- **Conservation**: protected areas, zoos and captive breeding, seed banks, controlling invasive species, and CITES (controls trade in endangered species).

# 19 Genetic technology
- **Recombinant DNA**:
  1. The gene is cut out with **restriction enzymes** (leaving sticky ends), or made from mRNA with **reverse transcriptase**.
  2. It is inserted into a **plasmid** vector cut with the same enzyme, and joined with **DNA ligase**.
  3. The plasmid is put into a host (bacteria are made to take it up by heat shock).
  4. Transformed cells are identified using **marker genes**, e.g. antibiotic resistance or GFP fluorescence.
- **PCR**: amplifies DNA.
  1. Denature at **95 °C** (the strands separate).
  2. Anneal at **about 55 °C** (primers bind).
  3. Extend at **72 °C** (**Taq polymerase**, from a thermophilic bacterium, adds nucleotides).
  4. Repeat; the DNA doubles each cycle.
- **Gel electrophoresis**: DNA (negatively charged) moves towards the **anode**, and smaller fragments move further. Used in DNA profiling and genetic testing.
- **Microarrays**: detect which genes are being expressed, or the presence of alleles.
- **Applications**: insulin produced by bacteria; GM crops (e.g. Bt maize, which makes an insecticidal protein; Golden Rice, with added vitamin A precursor); gene therapy; genetic screening.
- **Ethical and social issues**: cross-pollination with wild plants, monopoly by seed companies, and consent and privacy in genetic testing.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In natural selection answers, finish with "**allele frequency changes** over generations".
- In χ², state the null hypothesis, the degrees of freedom, the critical value, and the conclusion.
- In PCR, give the **temperatures** and the reason for each step.
