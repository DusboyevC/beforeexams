# 3 Enzymes

## Key ideas
- **Enzyme**: a **globular protein** that acts as a **biological catalyst**. It **lowers the activation energy** of a reaction and is not used up. It can be intracellular or extracellular.
- **Active site**: the region of the enzyme, with a specific 3D shape (from its tertiary structure), that binds the substrate.
- **Lock and key** model: the substrate's shape is **complementary** to the active site.
- **Induced fit** model: the active site **changes shape slightly** as the substrate binds, giving a closer fit, which puts strain on the substrate's bonds and lowers the activation energy.
- An **enzyme–substrate complex** forms, then an enzyme–product complex, and the product is released.

## Factors affecting the rate
| Factor | Effect and explanation |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Rising temperature: more kinetic energy, so more frequent successful collisions and E–S complexes, up to the **optimum**. Above the optimum, bonds (especially hydrogen bonds) in the tertiary structure break, the active site changes shape, the substrate no longer fits, and the enzyme is **denatured** |
| **pH** | Away from the optimum, the **ionic and hydrogen bonds** in the tertiary structure are disrupted (H⁺ and OH⁻ ions change the charges on R groups), so the shape of the active site changes and the enzyme is denatured |
| **Enzyme concentration** | More active sites, so the rate increases (if there is excess substrate) |
| **Substrate concentration** | The rate increases until **all active sites are occupied** (saturated), then levels off at **Vmax** |

## Inhibitors
- **Competitive inhibitor**: has a **similar shape** to the substrate and binds to the **active site**, which reduces the number of E–S complexes. **Increasing [substrate] reduces its effect**; Vmax is unchanged but **Km increases**.
- **Non-competitive inhibitor**: binds **elsewhere** (an allosteric site) and changes the shape of the active site. Increasing [substrate] does **not** overcome it; **Vmax is reduced** and Km is unchanged.
- **End-product inhibition**: the product of a pathway inhibits an enzyme early in the pathway (control by negative feedback).

## Vmax and Km
- **Vmax**: the maximum rate, when all the active sites are saturated.
- **Km (Michaelis–Menten constant)**: the substrate concentration that gives **half Vmax**. A **lower Km means a higher affinity** of the enzyme for its substrate.

## Immobilised enzymes
- Enzymes trapped in alginate beads (or bound to a surface).
- Benefits: they can be **reused**; the **product is not contaminated** by the enzyme; they are **more stable** (tolerate wider temperature and pH ranges); they suit continuous processes.

## Practical skills
- Measure the rate as **product formed (or substrate used) per unit time**, using the initial gradient (a tangent at t = 0).
- Control variables: temperature (a water bath), pH (buffers), concentrations and volumes.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Explain denaturation as **bonds breaking**, so the **active site shape changes** and the substrate is no longer complementary. Enzymes aren't "killed".
- Use the terms "complementary" and "enzyme–substrate complex".
- Competitive versus non-competitive: link each to its effect on **Vmax and Km**.

1. Explain why the rate levels off at high substrate concentrations.
2. Explain the difference between competitive and non-competitive inhibition.
3. What does a low Km tell you about an enzyme?
