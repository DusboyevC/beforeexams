# 8 Transport in plants

## Xylem and phloem
- **Xylem** transports water and mineral ions, and gives support.
- **Phloem** transports sucrose and amino acids.

**Position in a non-woody dicotyledonous plant**
- **Root**: xylem in the centre, often star-shaped, with phloem between its arms.
- **Stem**: vascular bundles in a ring near the edge, with xylem on the inside and phloem on the outside.
- **Leaf**: in the veins, with xylem at the top and phloem underneath.

**Xylem structure (Extended)**
- thick walls with **lignin** for strength
- no cell contents, so water flows freely
- cells joined end to end with no cross walls, forming a long continuous tube

## Water uptake
- **Root hair cells** absorb water and mineral ions from the soil.
- Their large surface area increases this uptake.
- **Pathway of water**: root hair cells → root cortex cells → xylem → mesophyll cells.
- **Investigation**: stand a plant in a coloured stain. Cut across the stem and leaf to see stained xylem.

## Transpiration
- **Transpiration** is the loss of water vapour from leaves.
- Water evaporates from the surfaces of mesophyll cells into the air spaces. It then diffuses out through the stomata as water vapour.

**Extended**
- The interconnecting air spaces give a large internal surface area for evaporation.
- More and larger stomata mean more water vapour can leave.

**Transpiration pull (Extended)**
1. Water leaves the leaf, which pulls water up the xylem.
2. Water molecules are held together by forces of attraction between them.
3. So a whole column of water is drawn upwards.

## Rate of transpiration
| Factor | Effect | Explanation (Extended) |
|---|---|---|
| Higher temperature | faster | water molecules have more kinetic energy, so they evaporate and diffuse faster |
| Higher wind speed | faster | water vapour is blown away, keeping the concentration gradient steep |
| Higher humidity | slower | more water vapour in the air makes the concentration gradient smaller |

**Investigating**: use a potometer, or weigh a potted plant before and after.

**Wilting (Extended)**: when a plant loses water faster than it takes it up, cells become flaccid. They lose turgor pressure, so the leaves and stem droop.

## Translocation (Extended)
- **Translocation** is the movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources to sinks.
- **Sources** release sucrose or amino acids, such as leaves making sugar.
- **Sinks** use or store them, such as roots, fruits and growing buds.
- A part can be both at different times. A potato tuber is a sink in summer, when it stores starch. It is a source in spring, when the stored food moves to new shoots.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and diffuses out through stomata. Use both words correctly.
- Xylem carries water up. Phloem carries sucrose both ways.
- Humidity slows transpiration. It does not speed it up.

2. Why does wind increase transpiration? *(It removes water vapour, keeping the gradient steep.)*
