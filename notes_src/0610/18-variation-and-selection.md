# 18 Variation and selection

## Variation
**Variation** is differences between individuals of the same species.

| | Continuous | Discontinuous |
|---|---|---|
| Phenotypes | a range between two extremes | a limited number, no intermediates |
| Examples | body length, body mass | ABO blood groups, seed shape and colour in peas |
| Cause | genes and the environment | usually genes only |
| Graph | smooth curve or histogram | bar chart |

## Mutation
- **Mutation** is genetic change.
- It is the way new alleles are formed.
- **Ionising radiation** and some **chemicals** increase the rate of mutation.
- **Extended**: **gene mutation** is a random change in the base sequence of DNA.
- **Extended**: sources of genetic variation are mutation, meiosis, random mating and random fertilisation.

## Adaptive features
An **adaptive feature** is an inherited feature that helps an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment.

**Plant adaptations (Extended)**

| Xerophytes (dry places) | Hydrophytes (water) |
|---|---|
| thick waxy cuticle, reducing water loss | thin or no cuticle |
| small leaves or spines, less surface for water loss | large floating leaves |
| sunken stomata or rolled leaves, trapping humid air | stomata on the upper surface |
| long or widespread roots | little xylem or root; air spaces for floating |

## Natural selection
1. There is **genetic variation** within a population.
2. Many **offspring** are produced.
3. There is a **struggle for survival**, including competition for resources.
4. Individuals better adapted to the environment have a **greater chance of reproducing**.
5. They **pass on their alleles** to the next generation.

**Extended**
- **Adaptation** is the process, resulting from natural selection, by which populations become more suited to their environment over many generations.
- **Antibiotic resistance**: a mutation makes some bacteria resistant. The antibiotic kills the others. Resistant bacteria survive, reproduce and pass on the resistance allele.

## Selective breeding
1. Humans select individuals with desirable features.
2. These are crossed to produce the next generation.
3. Offspring showing the desirable features are selected.
4. This repeats over many generations.

Examples: cows with high milk yield, disease-resistant wheat.

**Natural and artificial selection (Extended)**

| Natural | Artificial |
|---|---|
| caused by the environment | caused by humans |
| gives features useful for survival | gives features useful to humans |
| slower | faster |

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Individuals do not choose to adapt. Selection acts on features already present.
- Use the word 'alleles' when describing what is passed on.
- Say 'more likely to survive and reproduce', not 'survive' alone.
