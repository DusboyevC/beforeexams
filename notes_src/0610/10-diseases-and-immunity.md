# 10 Diseases and immunity

## Pathogens
- A **pathogen** is a disease-causing organism.
- A **transmissible disease** is one where the pathogen can be passed from one host to another.
- **Direct transmission**: by contact, including blood and other body fluids.
- **Indirect transmission**: from contaminated surfaces, food, animals and air.

## Body defences
- **Skin**: a barrier to pathogens.
- **Hairs in the nose**: trap particles.
- **Mucus**: traps pathogens.
- **Stomach acid**: kills pathogens in food.
- **White blood cells**: destroy pathogens.

## Controlling the spread of disease
- **Clean water supply**: stops pathogens spreading in water.
- **Hygienic food preparation**: stops food being contaminated.
- **Good personal hygiene**: washing hands removes pathogens.
- **Waste disposal**: removes food for flies and rats, which carry disease.
- **Sewage treatment**: removes pathogens before water is released.

## Active immunity (Extended)
- **Active immunity** is defence against a pathogen by antibody production in the body.
- Each pathogen has its own **antigens**, with specific shapes.
- **Antibodies** are proteins that bind to antigens. This either destroys pathogens directly or marks them for destruction by phagocytes.
- Specific antibodies have **complementary shapes** that fit specific antigens.
- Active immunity is gained after an infection or by **vaccination**.

**Vaccination**
1. Weakened pathogens or their antigens are put into the body.
2. The antigens stimulate an immune response by lymphocytes, which produce antibodies.
3. **Memory cells** are produced, giving long-term immunity.

**Controlling spread**: if enough people are vaccinated, the pathogen has fewer hosts to infect, so its spread slows and even unvaccinated people are protected.

## Passive immunity (Extended)
- **Passive immunity** is short-term defence from antibodies acquired from another individual, including across the placenta and in breast milk.
- **Memory cells are not produced.**
- **Breast-feeding** matters because it gives infants antibodies while their own immune system develops.

## Cholera (Extended)
- **Cholera** is caused by a bacterium transmitted in contaminated water.
1. The bacterium produces a **toxin**.
2. The toxin causes secretion of **chloride ions** into the small intestine.
3. Water moves into the gut by **osmosis**.
4. This causes **diarrhoea, dehydration and loss of ions from the blood**.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Antibodies are made by lymphocytes. Antigens are on the pathogen.
- Say 'complementary shape', and do not say 'the same shape'.
- Vaccines do not contain antibodies. They stimulate the body to make them.

## Quick self-check
1. What is a pathogen? *(A disease-causing organism.)*
2. Why does a vaccine give long-term protection? *(Memory cells are made.)*
3. Why is passive immunity short-lived? *(The antibodies are broken down, and no memory cells are made.)*
