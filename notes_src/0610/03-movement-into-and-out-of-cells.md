# 3 Movement into and out of cells

## Diffusion
- **Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.
- The energy comes from the **kinetic energy** of random movement of molecules and ions.
- Some substances move into and out of cells by diffusion through the cell membrane.
- **Importance**: oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse in gas exchange. Digested food diffuses into the blood.

**What affects the rate**
- **Surface area**: larger is faster.
- **Temperature**: higher is faster.
- **Concentration gradient**: steeper is faster.
- **Distance**: shorter is faster.

## Water as a solvent
Water dissolves substances, which allows:
- **digestion**: enzymes and food are in solution
- **excretion**: urea dissolves in urine
- **transport**: substances are carried dissolved in blood and phloem

## Osmosis
- Water diffuses through **partially permeable membranes** by **osmosis**.
- Water moves into and out of cells by osmosis through the cell membrane.
- **Extended**: osmosis is the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.

**Investigating osmosis**
- Fill **dialysis tubing** with sugar solution and place it in water. Water enters, so the tubing swells.
- Put potato cylinders in sugar solutions of different concentrations. Measure the length or mass before and after.

## Effect on plant tissue
| Solution | What happens |
|---|---|
| Water or dilute solution | cells gain water, tissue gets firmer and longer |
| Concentrated solution | cells lose water, tissue gets softer and shorter |

**Extended terms**
- **Turgid**: a cell full of water, with the membrane pushing on the wall.
- **Turgor pressure**: the pressure of water pushing outwards on the cell wall.
- **Flaccid**: a cell that has lost water and become soft.
- **Plasmolysis**: the membrane pulls away from the cell wall after a lot of water is lost.

Plants are supported by the pressure of water inside the cells pressing outwards on the cell wall. The cell wall stops plant cells from bursting.

**Animal cells**: they have no cell wall, so they burst in water and shrink in concentrated solutions.

**Extended**: water potential differences explain how root hair cells take up water from soil, and why cells in a concentrated solution lose water.

## Active transport
- **Active transport** is the movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, against a concentration gradient, using energy from respiration.
- **Extended**: **protein carriers** move molecules or ions across the membrane.
- **Extended**: **root hairs** take up mineral ions from soil, even though the soil concentration is lower than in the cell.

## Comparing the three
| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance | any particles | water only | molecules and ions |
| Direction | down the gradient | high to low water potential | against the gradient |
| Energy from respiration | no | no | yes |

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- Always say 'net movement'.
- For osmosis, name the partially permeable membrane.
- Say the water potential is higher, rather than saying more water.

## Quick self-check
1. Where does the energy for active transport come from? *(Respiration)*
2. Why don't plant cells burst in water? *(The cell wall resists the pressure.)*
3. A potato cylinder shrinks in a solution. What moved? *(Water left by osmosis.)*
