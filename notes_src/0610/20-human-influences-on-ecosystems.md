# 20 Human influences on ecosystems

## Food supply
Humans have increased food production by using:
- **agricultural machinery**, to use larger areas of land and improve efficiency
- **chemical fertilisers**, to improve yields
- **insecticides**, to improve quality and yield
- **herbicides**, to reduce competition with weeds
- **selective breeding**, to improve crop plants and livestock

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Large monocultures | efficient, easy to harvest, cheaper food | less biodiversity, pests and disease spread easily, soil loses nutrients |
| Intensive livestock | more meat and milk, cheaper food | disease spreads, poor animal welfare, waste and methane pollution |

## Habitat destruction
- **Biodiversity** is the number of different species that live in an area.

**Reasons for habitat destruction**
- more land for housing, crops and livestock
- extraction of natural resources
- freshwater and marine pollution

By altering food webs and food chains, humans can have a negative impact on habitats.

**Effects of deforestation**
- less biodiversity
- extinction
- loss of soil
- flooding
- more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere

## Pollution
- **Untreated sewage and excess fertiliser** harm aquatic ecosystems.
- **Non-biodegradable plastics** harm land and water habitats. Animals may eat them or become trapped. They last a long time.
- **Carbon dioxide** comes from burning fossil fuels and deforestation. **Methane** comes from cattle and rotting waste.
- More of these gases cause the **enhanced greenhouse effect** and **climate change**.

**Eutrophication (Extended)**
1. More nitrate and other ions are available.
2. Producers grow faster.
3. Producers die, so more decomposition takes place.
4. Decomposers carry out more aerobic respiration.
5. Dissolved oxygen falls.
6. Organisms that need dissolved oxygen die.

## Conservation
- A **sustainable resource** is produced as rapidly as it is removed, so it does not run out.
- **Forests** and **fish stocks** can be managed sustainably.

**Why species become endangered or extinct**: climate change, habitat destruction, hunting, overharvesting, pollution and introduced species.

**Conserving endangered species**
- monitoring and protecting species and habitats
- education
- captive breeding programmes
- seed banks

**Extended**
- **Forests**: education, protected areas, quotas and replanting.
- **Fish stocks**: education, closed seasons, protected areas, controlled net types and mesh size, quotas and monitoring.

**Why conserve? (Extended)**
- to maintain or increase biodiversity
- to reduce extinction
- to protect vulnerable ecosystems
- to maintain ecosystem functions, such as nutrient cycling and providing food, drugs, fuel and genes

**Captive breeding (Extended)**: **artificial insemination** and **in vitro fertilisation** are used to help species reproduce.

**Small populations (Extended)**: a smaller population has less genetic variation, so it is more at risk from disease or changes in the environment.

## Exam tips and common mistakes
- In eutrophication, oxygen falls because decomposers respire, not because the algae use it up.
- Give a reason with each effect of deforestation.
- Sustainable means the resource is replaced as fast as it is used.
