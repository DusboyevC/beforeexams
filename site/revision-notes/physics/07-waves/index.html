AS & A Level Physics 9702

7 Waves

Key definitions

  • Progressive wave: a wave that transfers energy from one place to another without transferring matter.
  • Displacement: the distance of a point on the wave from its equilibrium position.
  • Amplitude: the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.
  • Wavelength (λ): the distance between two adjacent points that are in phase, e.g. two crests.
  • Period (T): the time for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): the number of oscillations per unit time: f = 1/T.
  • Phase difference: how far one oscillation is ahead of another, as a fraction of a cycle, in degrees or radians (one cycle = 360° = 2π rad).
  • Wave speed: v = fλ.
  • Intensity: power per unit area (W m⁻²). Intensity ∝ amplitude².
  • Transverse wave: the particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer (e.g. light, waves on a string).
  • Longitudinal wave: the particles oscillate parallel to the direction of energy transfer, forming compressions and rarefactions (e.g. sound).
  • Polarisation: oscillations restricted to a single plane. Only transverse waves can be polarised.

Derivation of v = fλ

In one period T the wave moves forward one wavelength λ, so v = λ/T = fλ.

Doppler effect (sound, source moving)

  • When the source moves towards the observer, the waves are squashed together, so the observed frequency is higher; moving away gives a lower frequency.
  • f₀ = fₛ v / (v ± vₛ): use v − vₛ when the source approaches and v + vₛ when it recedes (v is the speed of sound).

Electromagnetic spectrum

  • All EM waves are transverse, can travel in a vacuum, and travel at c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in a vacuum.
  • In order of decreasing wavelength: radio, microwaves, infrared, visible (700 nm red → 400 nm violet), ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
  • Approximate wavelengths: radio > 10⁻¹ m; microwave 10⁻³–10⁻¹ m; IR 10⁻⁶–10⁻³ m (roughly); visible 4–7 × 10⁻⁷ m; UV 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ m; X-ray 10⁻¹²–10⁻⁸ m; gamma < 10⁻¹¹ m.

Polarisation and Malus's law

  • An unpolarised wave passing through a polariser emerges plane-polarised with half the intensity.
  • Malus's law: when plane-polarised light passes through an analyser at angle θ to the plane of polarisation, I = I₀ cos²θ.

Using a CRO (oscilloscope)

  • Period = number of horizontal divisions for one cycle × time-base setting (s div⁻¹); then f = 1/T.
  • Amplitude = number of vertical divisions from the centre to the peak × Y-gain setting (V div⁻¹).

Graphs

  • Displacement–distance graph: a snapshot of the wave at one instant; you can read λ and the amplitude from it.
  • Displacement–time graph: one point on the wave over time; you can read T and the amplitude from it.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Intensity ∝ (amplitude)²: doubling the amplitude gives four times the intensity.
  • Only transverse waves can be polarised. This is the classic way to show light is transverse.
  • In Doppler questions, decide first whether the observed frequency should go up or down.
  • Convert the CRO time-base unit carefully (ms div⁻¹ or μs div⁻¹).

Quick self-check

  1. Two points on a wave are 0.25λ apart. What is their phase difference? (90° or π/2 rad)
  2. A siren of frequency 800 Hz moves towards you at 30 m s⁻¹ (speed of sound 340 m s⁻¹). What frequency do you hear? (877 Hz)
  3. Polarised light of intensity 8.0 W m⁻² passes through an analyser at 60°. Find the transmitted intensity. (2.0 W m⁻²)
