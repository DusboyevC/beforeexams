AS & A Level Physics 9702

4 Forces, density and pressure

Key definitions

  • Moment of a force: force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.
  • Couple: a pair of forces that are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and act along parallel lines (not the same line). It produces rotation only, with no resultant force.
  • Torque of a couple: one of the forces × the perpendicular distance between the two forces.
  • Principle of moments: for an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point = the sum of anticlockwise moments about that point.
  • Equilibrium: there is no resultant force and no resultant moment (torque).
  • Centre of gravity: the point through which the whole weight of an object can be considered to act.
  • Density: mass per unit volume: ρ = m/V.
  • Pressure: normal force per unit area: p = F/A (unit Pa = N m⁻²).
  • Upthrust: the upward force on an object in a fluid, caused by the fluid pressure being greater at the bottom of the object than at the top.

Types of force

Weight, friction, drag (air resistance or viscous force), normal contact force, tension, upthrust, electric and magnetic forces.

Equilibrium of three forces

  • If an object is in equilibrium under three coplanar forces, the forces drawn head to tail form a closed triangle, and their lines of action pass through one point.
  • Solve using a scale drawing, the sine or cosine rule, or by resolving in two perpendicular directions: the sum of the components in each direction is zero.

Pressure in a fluid

  • Δp = ρgΔh: the pressure difference between two depths in a fluid of density ρ.
  • Derivation: imagine a column of fluid of height h and cross-section A. Its mass = ρAh, its weight = ρAhg, so the pressure = weight/A = ρgh.
  • The total pressure at depth h = atmospheric pressure + ρgh.

Upthrust and Archimedes' principle

  • Upthrust = weight of fluid displaced: F = ρgV, where ρ is the density of the fluid and V is the volume submerged.
  • Origin: the pressure on the bottom surface is greater than on the top (it is deeper), so there is a net upward force.
  • An object floats when the upthrust equals its weight.

Worked example (moments)

A uniform beam of weight 200 N and length 4.0 m rests on a pivot 1.0 m from its left end. What force at the left end keeps it level? The weight acts at the centre, 1.0 m right of the pivot: clockwise moment = 200 × 1.0 = 200 N m. F × 1.0 = 200, so F = 200 N.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Use the perpendicular distance in moments: resolve the force or the distance if it acts at an angle.
  • To find an unknown force, take moments about the point where another unknown force acts, so that force drops out.
  • A couple produces a resultant moment but zero resultant force.
  • In upthrust questions, ρ is the density of the fluid, not the object.

Quick self-check

  1. State the two conditions for equilibrium.
  2. Find the pressure 15 m below the surface of sea water (ρ = 1030 kg m⁻³) due to the water alone. (1.5 × 10⁵ Pa)
  3. Explain why a ship floats.
