AS & A Level Physics 9702

11 Particle physics

Atoms and nuclei

  • α-particle scattering (Geiger and Marsden): α-particles were fired at thin gold foil.
  • Most passed straight through, so the atom is mostly empty space.

  • A few were deflected through large angles (some by more than 90°), so there is a small, dense, positively charged nucleus that contains most of the atom's mass.

  • Nucleon number (A): the number of protons + neutrons. Proton number (Z): the number of protons.

  • Nuclide notation: ᴬ_Z X.
  • Isotopes: nuclei of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
  • In nuclear processes, nucleon number, charge (proton number) and mass–energy are conserved.

Radioactive decay

Radiation Nature Charge Change to the nucleus Penetration
α Helium nucleus ⁴₂He +2e A − 4, Z − 2 Stopped by paper or a few cm of air; strongly ionising
β⁻ Fast electron −e Z + 1 (a neutron becomes a proton); an antineutrino is also emitted Stopped by a few mm of aluminium
β⁺ Positron +e Z − 1 (a proton becomes a neutron); a neutrino is also emitted —
γ High-frequency electromagnetic radiation 0 No change in A or Z Reduced by thick lead or concrete; weakly ionising
  • Energy spectrum: α-particles from one source have discrete energies. β-particles have a continuous range of energies, because the energy is shared with the (anti)neutrino. This is evidence that the (anti)neutrino exists.
  • Unified atomic mass unit: 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

Fundamental particles

  • Quarks: up (u, charge +⅔e), down (d, −⅓e), strange (s, −⅓e), charm (c, +⅔e), top (t, +⅔e), bottom (b, −⅓e). Each has an antiquark with the opposite charge.
  • Hadrons: particles made of quarks.
  • Baryons: three quarks, e.g. proton = uud, neutron = udd.

  • Mesons: a quark + an antiquark, e.g. π⁺ = u d̄.

  • Leptons: fundamental particles, not made of quarks, e.g. electron, positron, neutrino. They are not affected by the strong force.

  • Antiparticle: same mass but opposite charge (and other quantum numbers) to its particle.

Quark changes in beta decay (the weak interaction)

  • β⁻ decay: d → u + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ (a neutron, udd, becomes a proton, uud).
  • β⁺ decay: u → d + e⁺ + νₑ (a proton becomes a neutron).

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • For α-scattering, link each observation to its conclusion.
  • In β⁻ decay, say an antineutrino is emitted; in β⁺ decay, a neutrino.
  • Check charge conservation in quark questions: uud = ⅔ + ⅔ − ⅓ = +1.
  • Leptons are fundamental; hadrons are made of quarks.

Quick self-check

  1. Write the decay equation for ²³⁸₉₂U emitting an α-particle. (→ ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He)
  2. Show that the quark composition udd gives a charge of zero.
  3. Explain why β-particles are emitted with a range of kinetic energies.
