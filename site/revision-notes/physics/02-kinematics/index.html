AS & A Level Physics 9702

2 Kinematics

Key definitions

  • Distance: the total length of path travelled (scalar).
  • Displacement: the distance in a straight line from a reference point in a stated direction (vector).
  • Speed: rate of change of distance (scalar).
  • Velocity: rate of change of displacement (vector).
  • Acceleration: rate of change of velocity (vector).

Equations of uniformly accelerated motion (SUVAT)

Valid only for constant acceleration in a straight line.

  • v = u + at
  • s = ½(u + v)t
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as

Derivation (often asked): from a v–t graph, a = (v − u)/t gives v = u + at. The area is s = ½(u + v)t. Substitute for v to get s = ut + ½at², and eliminate t to get v² = u² + 2as.

Free fall

  • Near the Earth's surface, with air resistance neglected, every object accelerates downwards at g = 9.81 m s⁻².
  • Experiment to find g: drop a ball through a measured height h and time it with light gates (or an electromagnet and timer). Then use s = ut + ½at² with u = 0, so g = 2h/t². Better: plot h against t²; the gradient = g/2.

Projectile motion

  • The horizontal and vertical motions are independent.
  • Horizontally: constant velocity (no horizontal force if air resistance is ignored), so x = uₓt.
  • Vertically: constant acceleration g downwards, so use SUVAT with a = −9.81 m s⁻² (taking up as positive).
  • The time of flight is set by the vertical motion.

Worked example: a ball is kicked at 20 m s⁻¹ at 30° above the horizontal from level ground. Find the range.

  • uₓ = 20 cos 30° = 17.3 m s⁻¹; u_y = 20 sin 30° = 10.0 m s⁻¹
  • Time to land: 0 = 10t − 4.905t², so t = 2.04 s
  • Range = 17.3 × 2.04 = 35.3 m

Effect of air resistance

  • Drag increases with speed. A falling object accelerates until drag + upthrust = weight, so the resultant force is zero and it falls at terminal velocity (constant speed).
  • On a v–t graph: the gradient starts at g and decreases to zero, and the line levels off at terminal velocity.
  • A projectile with air resistance has a lower maximum height and shorter range, and its path is not symmetrical (the descent is steeper).

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Decide on a positive direction and keep to it. Signs on a, u and s matter.
  • Do not use SUVAT when the acceleration is changing.
  • For projectiles, find the time from the vertical motion, then use it horizontally.
  • When you read a gradient from a graph, use a large triangle.

Quick self-check

  1. A car accelerates uniformly from 5 m s⁻¹ to 25 m s⁻¹ in 8 s. Find the distance travelled. (120 m)
  2. A ball is thrown horizontally at 12 m s⁻¹ from a 20 m cliff. Find the time to hit the ground. (2.0 s)
  3. Sketch the v–t graph of a skydiver from jumping until reaching terminal velocity.
