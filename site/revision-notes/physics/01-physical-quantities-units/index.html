AS & A Level Physics 9702

1 Physical quantities and units

Key definitions

  • Physical quantity: a quantity that can be measured; it has a numerical magnitude and a unit.
  • SI base quantities and units: mass (kg), length (m), time (s), current (A), temperature (K), amount of substance (mol).
  • Derived unit: a combination of base units, e.g. N = kg m s⁻², J = kg m² s⁻², Pa = kg m⁻¹ s⁻², C = A s, V = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹.
  • Homogeneous equation: every term has the same base units. A homogeneous equation is not necessarily correct; for example, numerical constants can be wrong.
  • Scalar: has magnitude only (mass, distance, speed, energy, time, temperature).
  • Vector: has magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, weight).
  • Systematic error: an error that makes every reading differ from the true value by the same amount or the same factor, e.g. a zero error. It cannot be reduced by repeating readings; it affects accuracy.
  • Random error: an error that makes readings scatter unpredictably about the true value. It is reduced by averaging repeated readings; it affects precision.
  • Accuracy: how close a measurement is to the true value.
  • Precision: how close repeated measurements are to each other (a small spread).

Estimates to know

Mass of an adult ≈ 70 kg; height of an adult ≈ 1.7 m; walking speed ≈ 1.5 m s⁻¹; mass of a car ≈ 1000 kg; volume of air in a room ≈ 50 m³; wavelength of visible light ≈ 400–700 nm; atom diameter ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m; nucleus ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m.

Checking homogeneity (base units)

Example: check s = ut + ½at². s → m; ut → (m s⁻¹)(s) = m; at² → (m s⁻²)(s²) = m. Every term is in m, so the equation is homogeneous.

Uncertainties

  • Absolute uncertainty: ±Δx. Fractional = Δx/x. Percentage = (Δx/x) × 100%.
  • Adding or subtracting quantities: add the absolute uncertainties.
  • Multiplying or dividing: add the fractional (percentage) uncertainties.
  • Powers: if y = xⁿ, the fractional uncertainty of y = n × the fractional uncertainty of x.
  • Give the final uncertainty to 1 significant figure (2 at most), and round the value to the same decimal place.

Worked example: the radius of a wire is r = 0.40 ± 0.01 mm. Find the percentage uncertainty in its cross-sectional area A = πr². %Δr = 0.01/0.40 × 100 = 2.5%, so %ΔA = 2 × 2.5 = 5%.

Vectors

  • Add vectors head to tail (scale diagram or triangle/parallelogram) or by resolving.
  • Resolving a force F at angle θ to the horizontal: horizontal component = F cos θ, vertical component = F sin θ.
  • Resultant of perpendicular components: R = √(x² + y²), with direction tan⁻¹(y/x).

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • A unit question means SI base units. Express N, J, W, V and Ω in kg, m, s and A.
  • Accuracy ≠ precision. A precise instrument can still be inaccurate if it has a zero error.
  • For uncertainty in a mean, use half the range of the repeated readings (unless told otherwise).
  • Always state the direction for a vector resultant.

Quick self-check

  1. Express the ohm in SI base units. (kg m² s⁻³ A⁻²)
  2. Explain why repeating readings does not reduce a systematic error.
  3. A = 4.0 ± 0.1 and B = 2.0 ± 0.1. Find the absolute uncertainty in A × B. (8.0 ± 0.6)
