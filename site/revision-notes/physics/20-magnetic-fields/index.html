AS & A Level Physics 9702

20 Magnetic fields

Key definitions

  • Magnetic field: a region in which a magnet, a magnetic material or a moving charge experiences a force.
  • Magnetic flux density (B): the force per unit current per unit length on a straight conductor placed perpendicular to the field: B = F/(IL).
  • Tesla: the flux density that produces a force of 1 N on a 1 m length of wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field.
  • Magnetic flux (Φ): Φ = BA, where A is the area perpendicular to the field (weber, Wb).
  • Magnetic flux linkage: NΦ (the flux through a coil of N turns).
  • Faraday's law: the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
  • Lenz's law: the direction of the induced e.m.f. (and current) is such that it opposes the change that produces it.

Forces

  • On a current-carrying wire: F = BIL sin θ. The direction is given by Fleming's left-hand rule (First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current, thuMb = Motion).
  • On a moving charge: F = Bqv sin θ. The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so the charge moves in a circle: Bqv = mv²/r, giving r = mv/(Bq).
  • Velocity selector: crossed electric and magnetic fields. Particles go straight through when qE = Bqv, i.e. when v = E/B.
  • Hall effect: charge carriers moving through a conductor in a magnetic field are pushed to one side, which builds up a Hall voltage V_H = BI/(ntq) across the conductor (t = thickness). A Hall probe uses this to measure B; semiconductors are used because their low n gives a larger V_H.

Fields from currents

  • Long straight wire: concentric circles (direction by the right-hand grip rule). The field gets weaker with distance.
  • Flat coil: like a short bar magnet. Solenoid: a uniform field inside, like a bar magnet outside.
  • An iron core increases the field strength.
  • Force between parallel wires: currents in the same direction attract; opposite directions repel. Each wire is in the other's field.

Electromagnetic induction

  • An e.m.f. is induced when the flux linking a circuit changes, e.g. a magnet moves into a coil, a coil rotates in a field, or the current in a nearby coil changes.
  • E = −N dΦ/dt (the minus sign expresses Lenz's law).
  • Lenz's law follows from conservation of energy: if the induced current helped the change, energy would be created from nothing.
  • Example: a magnet pushed into a coil makes the near end of the coil the same pole, which repels the magnet, so work must be done to push it in.

Worked example

An electron (m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg) moves at 2.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a field of 5.0 mT. Find the radius of its path. r = mv/(Bq) = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 2.0 × 10⁷ / (5.0 × 10⁻³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 2.3 × 10⁻² m

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • The magnetic force on a moving charge does no work (it is perpendicular to the velocity), so the speed stays constant.
  • In the definition of B, include "perpendicular to the field".
  • For Lenz's law, say what change is being opposed and how.

Quick self-check

  1. A 0.20 m wire carries 3.0 A at right angles to a 0.40 T field. Find the force. (0.24 N)
  2. Explain why a charged particle moves in a circle in a uniform magnetic field.
  3. Use Lenz's law to explain why a falling magnet slows down inside a copper tube.
