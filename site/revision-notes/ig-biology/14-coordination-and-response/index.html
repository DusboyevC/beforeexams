IGCSE Biology 0610

14 Coordination and response

The nervous system

  • Electrical impulses travel along neurones.
  • Central nervous system (CNS): the brain and spinal cord.
  • Peripheral nervous system (PNS): the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.
  • Its role is the coordination and regulation of body functions.
  • There are sensory, relay and motor neurones.

Reflex arc

Stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone → motor neurone → effector → response

A reflex action is a means of automatically and rapidly integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of effectors (muscles and glands).

Synapses

A synapse is a junction between two neurones.

Extended: it has vesicles containing neurotransmitter molecules, a synaptic gap and receptor proteins.

  1. An impulse stimulates the release of neurotransmitter from vesicles into the gap.
  2. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap.
  3. It binds with receptor proteins on the next neurone.
  4. An impulse is stimulated in the next neurone.

Synapses ensure impulses travel in one direction only.

Sense organs

Sense organs are groups of receptor cells responding to specific stimuli: light, sound, touch, temperature and chemicals.

The eye

  • Cornea: refracts light.
  • Iris: controls how much light enters the pupil.
  • Lens: focuses light on to the retina.
  • Retina: contains light receptors, some sensitive to different colours.
  • Optic nerve: carries impulses to the brain.
  • Blind spot: where the optic nerve leaves, with no receptors.

Pupil reflex

  • In bright light, the pupil gets smaller.
  • In dim light, the pupil gets wider.

Extended: the circular and radial muscles of the iris are antagonistic.

Light Circular muscles Radial muscles Pupil
Bright contract relax narrows
Dim relax contract widens

Rods and cones (Extended)

  • Rods are more sensitive, for night vision. They are found across the retina, except at the fovea.
  • Cones have three kinds, absorbing different colours, for colour vision. They are concentrated in the fovea.
  • The fovea gives the sharpest, most detailed vision.

Hormones

A hormone is a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.

Gland Hormone
Adrenal glands adrenaline
Pancreas insulin (Extended: also glucagon)
Testes testosterone
Ovaries oestrogen

Adrenaline is secreted in 'fight or flight' situations. It increases breathing rate, heart rate and pupil diameter. Extended: it also increases blood glucose concentration.

Speed very fast slower

Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment.

Negative feedback (Extended): a change from the set point is detected, and a response brings the level back.

Blood glucose

  • Insulin decreases blood glucose concentration.
  • Extended: when glucose is high, insulin makes the liver turn glucose into glycogen. When glucose is low, glucagon makes the liver turn glycogen into glucose.
  • Extended, Type 1 diabetes: treated with insulin injections, monitoring blood glucose and controlling the diet.

Temperature control Skin structures: hairs, hair erector muscles, sweat glands, receptors, sensory neurones, blood vessels and fatty tissue.

  • Too hot: sweating, as evaporation cools the skin.
  • Too cold: shivering, as muscle contractions release heat. Hairs stand up, trapping insulating air.
  • Fatty tissue insulates. The brain receives information and coordinates the response.

Extended

  • Vasodilation: arterioles to the skin surface widen, so more blood flows there and more heat is lost.
  • Vasoconstriction: arterioles narrow, so less blood flows near the surface.

Tropic responses

  • Gravitropism: parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity.
  • Phototropism: parts of a plant grow towards or away from the direction of light.
  • Shoots grow towards light and away from gravity. Roots grow towards gravity.

Auxin (Extended)

  1. Auxin is made in the shoot tip.
  2. It diffuses through the plant from the tip.
  3. It is unequally distributed in response to light and gravity. More collects on the shaded side.
  4. Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so the shaded side grows longer and the shoot bends towards the light.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Capillaries do not move up and down. Arterioles widen or narrow.
  • Hormones travel in the blood, and neurones carry electrical impulses.
  • Glycogen is the store, glucagon is the hormone.

Quick self-check

  1. List the parts of a reflex arc in order. (Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector)
  2. Which hormone lowers blood glucose? (Insulin)
  3. What happens to the lens for near vision? (It becomes fatter.)
