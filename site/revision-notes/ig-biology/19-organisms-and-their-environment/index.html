IGCSE Biology 0610

19 Organisms and their environment

Energy flow

  • The Sun is the principal source of energy input to biological systems.
  • Light energy is changed to chemical energy in plants. It passes along food chains and is eventually transferred to the environment.

Food chains and webs

  • A food chain shows the transfer of energy from one organism to the next, beginning with a producer.
  • A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.
  • Producer: makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight.
  • Consumer: gets energy by feeding on other organisms. Consumers are primary, secondary, tertiary or quaternary.
  • Herbivore: eats plants.
  • Carnivore: eats other animals.
  • Decomposer: gets energy from dead or waste organic material.
  • Trophic level: the position of an organism in a food chain, web or pyramid.

Human impact: overharvesting a species reduces the food of its predators and raises the number of its prey. A foreign species may compete with or eat native species.

Ecological pyramids

  • Pyramid of numbers: shows the number of organisms at each level. It can be an odd shape: one tree supports many insects.
  • Pyramid of biomass: shows the dry mass at each level. It is better, as it takes the size of organisms into account.

Extended

  • Pyramid of energy: shows the energy at each level. It is best, as it is always pyramid-shaped and shows energy transferred over time.

Energy loss (Extended)

Energy transfer between levels is often not efficient, because:

  • not all the organism is eaten
  • not all that is eaten is digested
  • energy is lost in respiration, as heat
  • energy is lost in waste

So food chains usually have fewer than five trophic levels, as too little energy remains.

Crops or livestock? Eating crops directly is more energy efficient, as livestock lose energy before it reaches humans.

Carbon cycle

  • Photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the air.
  • Respiration returns CO₂ to the air.
  • Feeding passes carbon along food chains.
  • Decomposition returns CO₂ to the air by decomposers respiring.
  • Fossil fuels form from dead organisms, over millions of years.
  • Combustion returns CO₂ to the air.

Nitrogen cycle (Extended)

  • Nitrogen fixation: lightning and bacteria turn nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds.
  • Absorption: plants absorb nitrate ions and make amino acids and proteins.
  • Feeding and digestion: animals digest proteins to amino acids.
  • Deamination: excess amino acids are broken down, forming urea.
  • Decomposition: decomposers turn plant and animal protein into ammonium ions.
  • Nitrification: bacteria turn ammonium ions into nitrate ions.
  • Denitrification: bacteria turn nitrate ions into nitrogen gas.

Microorganisms carry out decomposition, nitrification, nitrogen fixation and denitrification.

Populations

  • Population: a group of organisms of one species, living in the same area at the same time.
  • Community: all the populations of different species in an ecosystem.
  • Ecosystem: a unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together.
  • Factors affecting growth: food supply, competition, predation and disease.

The sigmoid growth curve

Phase What happens Why (Extended)
Lag slow growth organisms are adapting, few are reproducing
Exponential (log) rapid growth plenty of resources, birth rate above death rate
Stationary level limiting factors; birth rate equals death rate
Death decline resources run out or waste builds up; death rate above birth rate

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Arrows in a food chain show the direction of energy flow.
  • Decomposers are not part of the food chain levels, but they recycle nutrients.
  • Energy is lost and nutrients are recycled.

Quick self-check

  1. What does a producer use as its energy source? (Sunlight)
  2. Why is a pyramid of biomass better than one of numbers? (It accounts for the size of organisms.)
  3. Name the process that turns nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds. (Nitrogen fixation)
