1 Characteristics and classification of living organisms

Characteristics of living organisms

  • Movement: an action by an organism, or part of it, causing a change of position or place.
  • Respiration: chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism.
  • Sensitivity: the ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment.
  • Growth: a permanent increase in size and dry mass.
  • Reproduction: the processes that make more of the same kind of organism.
  • Excretion: removal of the waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements.
  • Nutrition: taking in materials for energy, growth and development.

Memory aid: MRS GREN.

Classification

  • Organisms can be classified into groups by the features they share.
  • A species is a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
  • The binomial system is an internationally agreed system. The scientific name has two parts showing the genus and species, as in Homo sapiens.
  • A dichotomous key uses a series of paired choices based on identifiable features.

Extended

  • Classification systems aim to reflect evolutionary relationships.
  • Sequences of bases in DNA are used to classify organisms.
  • Groups that share a more recent ancestor have more similar base sequences than those sharing only a distant ancestor.

The five kingdoms

Kingdom Main features
Animal many cells, no cell wall, no chloroplasts, feed on organic substances
Plant many cells, cell walls of cellulose, chloroplasts, photosynthesise
Fungus cell walls not of cellulose, no chloroplasts, feed on dead organic matter or as parasites
Prokaryote single cells, no nucleus (bacteria)
Protoctist mostly single cells with a nucleus

Core needs animals and plants. All five are Extended.

Vertebrates

Group Skin Features
Mammals fur or hair live young, feed young on milk
Birds feathers wings, beak, hard-shelled eggs
Reptiles dry scales soft leathery eggs on land
Amphibians moist skin eggs laid in water, adults live on land and in water
Fish wet scales fins, gills

Arthropods

All have jointed legs and an exoskeleton.

Group Features
Insects 3 pairs of legs, usually wings, 1 pair of antennae, 3 body parts
Arachnids 4 pairs of legs, no antennae, 2 body parts
Crustaceans more than 4 pairs of legs, 2 pairs of antennae
Myriapods many body segments, with legs on each, 1 pair of antennae

Plants (Extended)

  • Ferns: have leaves called fronds, and reproduce by spores, not flowers.
  • Flowering plants reproduce with flowers and seeds.

Viruses (Extended)

  • A virus is made of a protein coat and genetic material. It is not placed in any of the five kingdoms.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Growth means a permanent increase in dry mass.
  • Excretion is not the same as egestion.
  • Write the genus name with a capital letter and the species name in lower case.

Quick self-check

  1. Name the two parts of a binomial name. (Genus and species)
  2. An animal has 8 legs. Which arthropod group is it? (Arachnid)
  3. What are the features of a virus? (A protein coat and genetic material.)
