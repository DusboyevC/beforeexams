16 Reproduction

Asexual reproduction

Asexual reproduction results in genetically identical offspring from one parent. Examples: bacteria dividing, potato tubers, strawberry runners.

Advantages Disadvantages
In the wild fast, needs one parent, can spread quickly in good conditions no variation, so a change could kill all of them
Crops good features kept, fast and predictable all can be killed by one disease

Sexual reproduction

  • It involves the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote. The offspring are genetically different from each other.
  • Fertilisation is the fusion of the nuclei of gametes.
  • Extended: gamete nuclei are haploid, and the zygote nucleus is diploid.
Advantages Disadvantages
In the wild variation helps survival when conditions change slower, needs a mate
Crops new varieties can be bred offspring vary, slower

Pollination and fertilisation

  • Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.
  • Fertilisation occurs when a pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule.

Extended

  • Self-pollination: within the same flower or the same plant.
  • Cross-pollination: to a flower on a different plant of the same species.
  • Self-pollination gives less variation and less ability to respond to change, but needs no pollinators. Cross-pollination gives more variation, but relies on pollinators.
  • A pollen tube grows down the style and enters the ovule, then the nuclei fuse.

Germination needs water, oxygen and a suitable temperature. Test each by leaving one out, with a control that has all three.

Human reproduction

Male

  • Testes: make sperm and testosterone.
  • Scrotum: holds the testes.
  • Sperm ducts: carry sperm.
  • Prostate gland: adds fluid.
  • Urethra: carries semen out.
  • Penis: delivers sperm.

Female

  • Ovaries: make eggs and hormones.
  • Oviducts: carry eggs; fertilisation happens here.
  • Uterus: where the fetus develops.
  • Cervix: the ring of muscle at the entrance to the uterus.
  • Vagina: receives sperm.

Gametes

  • Sperm have a flagellum to swim, mitochondria to release energy, and enzymes in the acrosome to get into the egg.
  • Egg cells have energy stores, and a jelly coat that changes at fertilisation, so only one sperm gets in.
Sperm Egg
Size small large
Structure head, flagellum round, with a jelly coat
Motility swim move by cilia in the oviduct
Numbers very many few

Development

  • The zygote forms an embryo, a ball of cells, which implants into the lining of the uterus.
  • Umbilical cord: joins the fetus to the placenta.
  • Placenta: exchanges substances with the mother's blood.
  • Amniotic sac: holds the amniotic fluid.
  • Amniotic fluid: protects the fetus from bumps.

Extended: dissolved nutrients and oxygen pass to the fetus. Carbon dioxide and urea pass to the mother. Some pathogens and toxins can cross the placenta and affect the fetus.

Sex hormones

  • Testosterone and oestrogen cause secondary sexual characteristics at puberty.
  • Menstrual cycle: about 28 days. The uterus lining breaks down (a period), then rebuilds. An egg is released from an ovary around the middle.

Extended

Hormone Made in Role
FSH pituitary egg matures in the ovary; oestrogen is made
LH pituitary triggers ovulation
Oestrogen ovary builds the uterus lining
Progesterone ovary after ovulation, then the placenta in pregnancy maintains the uterus lining

Sexually transmitted infections

  • An STI is an infection transmitted through sexual contact.
  • HIV is a pathogen that causes an STI. It may lead to AIDS.
  • HIV spreads by infected blood, sexual contact, sharing needles, and from mother to baby.
  • Control: condoms, fewer sexual partners, testing and treatment, and education.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Pollination comes before fertilisation.
  • Haploid means one set of chromosomes. Diploid means two.
  • Wind pollen is light and smooth.

Quick self-check

  1. Which part of a flower receives pollen? (Stigma)
  2. What three things does germination need? (Water, oxygen, a suitable temperature)
  3. Which hormone triggers ovulation? (LH)
