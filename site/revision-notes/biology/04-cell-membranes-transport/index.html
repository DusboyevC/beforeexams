AS & A Level Biology 9700

4 Cell membranes and transport

Fluid mosaic model

  • A phospholipid bilayer: hydrophilic heads face outwards and hydrophobic tails face inwards.
  • "Fluid": the phospholipids and proteins can move laterally. "Mosaic": proteins are scattered through it.
  • Components and roles:
  • Cholesterol: regulates fluidity and stability; reduces permeability to ions and polar molecules.
  • Channel proteins and carrier proteins: transport of polar or charged substances.

  • Glycoproteins and glycolipids: cell recognition, receptors (cell signalling), cell adhesion; they form the glycocalyx.

  • Cell signalling: a ligand (e.g. a hormone) binds to a specific receptor on the membrane. This triggers a response inside the cell, e.g. through a second messenger or the activation of enzymes.

Transport mechanisms

Process Energy? Description
Simple diffusion No (passive) Net movement of molecules down a concentration gradient; small or non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) pass through the bilayer
Facilitated diffusion No Down the gradient through channel or carrier proteins, for polar or charged substances (glucose, ions)
Osmosis No Net movement of water from a region of higher water potential to lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane
Active transport Yes (ATP) Movement against a concentration gradient by carrier proteins (e.g. the Na⁺/K⁺ pump)
Endocytosis / exocytosis Yes Bulk transport in vesicles (phagocytosis, pinocytosis; secretion)

Factors affecting the rate of diffusion

  • The surface area, the steepness of the concentration gradient, the diffusion distance (thickness), and temperature. Larger cells have a smaller surface area : volume ratio, so diffusion alone is too slow for them, and they need exchange surfaces and transport systems.

Water potential (ψ)

  • Measured in kPa. Pure water = 0; adding solutes makes ψ more negative.
  • Animal cells: in a solution of lower (more negative) ψ, water leaves and the cell shrinks (crenates); in a solution of higher ψ, water enters and the cell bursts (lyses).
  • Plant cells: water enters and the cell becomes turgid (the wall stops it bursting); water leaves and the cell becomes plasmolysed (the membrane pulls away from the wall).
  • Experiment: put potato cylinders in sucrose solutions of a range of concentrations and measure the % change in mass. The concentration that gives 0% change has the same ψ as the tissue.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Use water potential, not "water concentration", in osmosis answers.
  • Active transport: against the gradient, carrier proteins, ATP from respiration.
  • In surface area : volume questions, calculate the ratio and link it to the efficiency of diffusion.

Quick self-check

  1. Explain why red blood cells burst when placed in distilled water.
  2. Describe the role of cholesterol in the membrane.
  3. Calculate the surface area : volume ratio of a 2 cm cube. (24 : 8 = 3 : 1)
