IGCSE Mathematics 0580

9 Statistics

Organising data

  • Tally tables count data.
  • Two-way tables show two features.
  • When comparing data, compare an average and the range. Say what they mean in context.
  • Be careful with conclusions: small or unrepresentative samples may not show the full picture.

Averages and range

  • Mean = total of values ÷ number of values.
  • Median: the middle value when in order.
  • Mode: the most common value.
  • Range: largest − smallest.

When to use each: the median is not affected by extreme values. The mode is best for non-numerical data. The mean uses all the data.

  • Mean = (1 × 2 + 2 × 5 + 3 × 3) ÷ 10 = 21 ÷ 10 = 2.1
  • Median: 5th and 6th values are both 2, so 2.
  • Mode = 2

Extended, grouped data: use the midpoint of each class. The mean is an estimate. The class with the highest frequency is the modal class.

Extended, quartiles: the interquartile range is IQR = upper quartile − lower quartile. It shows the spread of the middle half.

Charts and diagrams

  • Bar charts: including composite (stacked) and dual (side by side).
  • Pie charts: angle = frequency ÷ total × 360°.
  • Pictograms: always use a key.
  • Stem-and-leaf diagrams: ordered, with a key.
  • Frequency distributions: show how data are spread.

Scatter diagrams

  • Positive correlation: as one goes up, the other goes up.
  • Negative correlation: as one goes up, the other goes down.
  • Zero correlation: no link.
  • Line of best fit: a single ruled line across the data, with a roughly even spread of points on both sides. Use it for estimates.

Cumulative frequency (Extended)

  • Add up the frequencies as a running total.
  • Plot each at the upper class boundary, marked with a small cross.
  • Join with a smooth curve.

With a total of 80:

  • Median: read at 40.
  • Lower quartile: read at 20.
  • Upper quartile: read at 60.
  • Percentile: for the 90th, read at 72.

Histograms (Extended)

  • The vertical axis is frequency density.
  • Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
  • The area of each bar shows the frequency.
  • Example: 10 < x ≤ 30 with frequency 16. Class width = 20, so frequency density = 0.8.
  • To read a frequency: frequency = frequency density × class width.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Order values before finding a median.
  • Do not plot cumulative frequency at the midpoint.
  • Correlation does not prove one thing causes another.
  • A line of best fit does not need to go through the origin.

Quick self-check

  1. Find the median of 3, 8, 2, 9, 5. (5)
  2. In a pie chart, 15 of 60 people are shown. Find the angle. (90°)
  3. A class of width 5 has frequency 30. Find the frequency density. (6)
