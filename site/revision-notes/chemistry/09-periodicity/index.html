AS & A Level Chemistry 9701

9 The Periodic Table: chemical periodicity (Period 3)

Physical trends across Period 3 (Na → Ar)

  • Atomic radius: decreases (the nuclear charge increases with similar shielding).
  • Ionic radius: decreases from Na⁺ to Al³⁺; there is a big jump up to P³⁻ (an extra shell); then it decreases to Cl⁻.
  • Electrical conductivity: increases from Na to Al (more delocalised electrons per atom); low for Si (a semiconductor); P, S, Cl and Ar do not conduct.
  • Melting point: rises from Na to Si, then falls.
  • Na, Mg, Al: metallic bonding gets stronger (more delocalised electrons, higher ion charge, smaller ions).
  • Si: giant covalent, so it has the highest melting point.

  • P₄, S₈, Cl₂, Ar: simple molecular, with weak London forces. Their order S₈ > P₄ > Cl₂ > Ar follows the number of electrons in the molecule.

  • First ionisation energy: generally increases, with dips at Al and S (see topic 1).

Reactions of the elements

  • With oxygen: Na → Na₂O (bright yellow flame); Mg → MgO (bright white); Al → Al₂O₃; Si → SiO₂; P → P₄O₁₀; S → SO₂ (blue flame).
  • With chlorine: NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃ (Al₂Cl₆), SiCl₄, PCl₅ (with excess Cl₂).
  • With water: Na reacts vigorously (forming NaOH + H₂); Mg reacts very slowly with cold water, but with steam it forms MgO + H₂.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Explain melting points using the structure and the type of bonding that must be overcome.
  • Always link the pH of an oxide in water to whether it is acidic or basic, and write the equations.
  • Remember Al₂O₃ is amphoteric, and give both equations.

Quick self-check

  1. Explain why silicon has the highest melting point in Period 3.
  2. Write an equation for SiCl₄ reacting with water and state the pH. (SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl; pH about 2)
  3. Explain why the melting point of sulfur is higher than that of phosphorus.
