AS & A Level Chemistry 9701

14 Hydrocarbons

Alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, saturated)

  • Unreactive, because the C–C and C–H bonds are strong and non-polar.
  • Combustion: complete combustion gives CO₂ + H₂O. Incomplete combustion gives CO (toxic) or C (soot).
  • Free-radical substitution with Cl₂ in UV light:
  • Initiation: Cl₂ → 2Cl• (homolytic fission, caused by UV light).
  • Propagation: CH₄ + Cl• → CH₃• + HCl, then CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•.
  • Termination: two radicals combine: Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂; CH₃• + Cl• → CH₃Cl; CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆.

  • It produces a mixture of products (further substitution, e.g. CH₂Cl₂), so it is not useful for making a single product.

  • Cracking: long-chain alkanes are broken into shorter alkanes and alkenes, using heat (and a catalyst such as Al₂O₃ or zeolite). This produces more useful fuels and alkenes for polymers.

  • Pollution: CO, NOₓ and unburnt hydrocarbons are removed by catalytic converters: 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.

Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ, unsaturated, C=C)

  • The π bond is an area of high electron density that attracts electrophiles.
  • Electrophilic addition reactions:
  • H₂ (Ni catalyst, 150 °C) → alkane (hydrogenation; used to make margarine).
  • Steam (H₃PO₄ catalyst, 300 °C, 60 atm) → alcohol.
  • HBr → bromoalkane.

  • Br₂ → dibromoalkane. Test for C=C: bromine water is decolourised (orange → colourless).

  • Mechanism (with HBr): the π electrons attack the δ+ H; the H–Br bond breaks heterolytically; a carbocation forms; Br⁻ attacks the carbocation.

  • Markovnikov's rule: the major product forms through the more stable carbocation. Tertiary > secondary > primary, because alkyl groups donate electrons (the inductive effect) and stabilise the positive charge.
  • Oxidation with KMnO₄:
  • Cold, dilute, acidified: a diol forms (the purple solution turns colourless).

  • Hot, concentrated: the C=C bond breaks. The products depend on the groups attached: =CH₂ gives CO₂ + H₂O; =CHR gives RCOOH; =CR₂ gives a ketone R₂C=O. This is used to find the position of the double bond.

  • Addition polymerisation: see topic 20.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Free-radical mechanisms: show the radical dot, and label initiation, propagation and termination.
  • In electrophilic addition mechanisms, curly arrows go from the C=C to H, from the H–Br bond to Br, and from the Br⁻ lone pair to C⁺.
  • Explain Markovnikov's rule in terms of carbocation stability due to the inductive effect of alkyl groups.

Quick self-check

  1. Write the propagation steps for the reaction of ethane with chlorine.
  2. Predict the major product when propene reacts with HBr, and explain. (2-bromopropane, via the more stable secondary carbocation)
  3. What forms when 2-methylbut-2-ene is heated with hot, concentrated, acidified KMnO₄? (propanone + ethanoic acid)
