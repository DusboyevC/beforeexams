AS & A Level Chemistry 9701

11 Group 17 (halogens)

Physical properties

Halogen State at r.t.p. Colour
F₂ Gas Pale yellow
Cl₂ Gas Yellow-green
Br₂ Liquid Red-brown (orange in solution)
I₂ Solid Grey-black solid, purple vapour (brown in water, violet in hexane)
- Volatility decreases down the group: there are more electrons, so stronger London forces between the molecules.
- Bond strength (X–X) decreases from Cl₂ to I₂ (longer bonds). F–F is anomalously weak because of repulsion between lone pairs on the small atoms.

Oxidising power

  • Decreases down the group: atoms are larger, with more shielding, so they are less able to gain an electron.
  • Displacement reactions: a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halide from solution. For example, Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂ (the solution turns orange).

Hydrogen halides

  • Thermal stability decreases down the group: HF > HCl > HBr > HI. The H–X bond gets longer and weaker. A hot glass rod in HI gives purple iodine vapour.

Halide ions

Test with silver nitrate (acidify with HNO₃ first, then add AgNO₃, then test the precipitate with ammonia):

Ion Precipitate Dilute NH₃ Concentrated NH₃
Cl⁻ White AgCl Dissolves Dissolves
Br⁻ Cream AgBr Insoluble Dissolves
I⁻ Yellow AgI Insoluble Insoluble

Reducing power of halide ions increases down the group (Cl⁻ < Br⁻ < I⁻), because the larger ions lose electrons more easily.

With concentrated sulfuric acid:

  • NaCl: NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl only (misty white fumes). Cl⁻ is not a strong enough reducing agent to reduce H₂SO₄.
  • NaBr: HBr forms, then Br⁻ reduces the H₂SO₄ to SO₂ and forms Br₂ (orange-brown fumes).
  • NaI: HI forms, then I⁻ reduces the H₂SO₄ all the way to SO₂, S and H₂S (rotten-egg smell), and forms I₂ (purple fumes or a black solid).

Reactions of chlorine

  • With cold dilute NaOH: Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O (disproportionation: Cl goes from 0 to −1 and +1).
  • With hot concentrated NaOH: 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O (Cl goes from 0 to −1 and +5).
  • Water treatment: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO. The HClO (chloric(I) acid) kills bacteria.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Explain volatility with London forces between molecules, not bond strength.
  • Explain oxidising power using atomic radius, shielding and electron affinity (attraction for an incoming electron).
  • For concentrated H₂SO₄, list the products and the observations for each halide.

Quick self-check

  1. Write the ionic equation for chlorine displacing iodide ions. (Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂)
  2. Explain why HI is less thermally stable than HCl.
  3. State the oxidation numbers of chlorine in NaCl, NaClO and NaClO₃. (−1, +1, +5)
