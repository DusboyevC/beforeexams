AS & A Level Chemistry 9701

25 Equilibria (A Level)

pH calculations

  • pH = −log[H⁺]; [H⁺] = 10^(−pH).
  • Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 298 K; pKw = 14.
  • Strong base: [OH⁻] = [base], then [H⁺] = Kw/[OH⁻].
  • Weak acid: Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]. Assume [H⁺] = [A⁻] and that [HA] is unchanged, so [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]). pKa = −log Ka.

Buffers

  • Buffer solution: a solution that resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.
  • Acidic buffer: a weak acid + its conjugate base (salt), e.g. CH₃COOH / CH₃COONa.
  • Adding H⁺: CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃COOH (the large reservoir of A⁻ removes it).

  • Adding OH⁻: CH₃COOH + OH⁻ → CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O (the large reservoir of HA removes it).

  • pH of a buffer: [H⁺] = Ka × [HA]/[A⁻], or pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

  • Blood buffer: H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻ keeps blood at about pH 7.4.

Indicators and titration curves

  • Choose an indicator whose colour-change range lies within the vertical (steep) part of the titration curve.
  • Strong acid + strong base: the vertical section runs from about pH 3 to 11, so most indicators work.
  • Weak acid + strong base: the equivalence point is above 7, so use phenolphthalein.

  • Strong acid + weak base: the equivalence point is below 7, so use methyl orange.

  • At the half-equivalence point of a weak acid titration, pH = pKa.

Solubility product

  • Ksp: for MₓAᵧ(s) ⇌ xMʸ⁺ + yAˣ⁻, Ksp = [Mʸ⁺]ˣ[Aˣ⁻]ʸ (for a saturated solution).
  • Will a precipitate form? If the ionic product > Ksp, it will.
  • Common ion effect: adding an ion that is already in the equilibrium reduces the solubility of the salt.

Partition coefficient

  • Kpc = [X in solvent 1] / [X in solvent 2] at equilibrium, for a solute distributed between two immiscible solvents.

Worked example

Find the pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.75 × 10⁻⁵). [H⁺] = √(1.75 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.100) = 1.32 × 10⁻³, so pH = 2.88.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Explain how a buffer works using equations, and mention the large reservoirs of HA and A⁻.
  • State the assumptions in weak-acid calculations.
  • Ksp units depend on the formula; work them out each time.

Quick self-check

  1. Find the pH of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. (12.7)
  2. A buffer has 0.20 mol dm⁻³ HA and 0.10 mol dm⁻³ A⁻, with Ka = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵. Find its pH. (4.70)
  3. The Ksp of AgCl is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. Find its solubility in mol dm⁻³. (1.3 × 10⁻⁵)
