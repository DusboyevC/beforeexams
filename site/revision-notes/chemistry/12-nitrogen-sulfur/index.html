AS & A Level Chemistry 9701

12 Nitrogen and sulfur

Nitrogen

  • N₂ is unreactive because of the strong N≡N triple bond (a high bond energy) and its lack of polarity.
  • Ammonia, NH₃: a pyramidal molecule; it acts as a base (it accepts H⁺, using its lone pair): NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺.
  • Ammonium ion, NH₄⁺: tetrahedral, formed through a co-ordinate bond.
  • Test for NH₄⁺: warm with NaOH(aq), which gives off NH₃ gas that turns damp red litmus blue: NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃ + H₂O.
  • Fertilisers: ammonium nitrate and ammonium sulfate.

Nitrogen oxides and the environment

  • Formation of NO in car engines: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO, at the high temperatures of combustion (lightning does the same).
  • NO is oxidised in air: 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂. NO₂ dissolves in rain to form HNO₃, which causes acid rain.
  • Catalytic converters (Pt, Rh, Pd): 2NO + 2CO → N₂ + 2CO₂.
  • Photochemical smog: NO₂ and unburnt hydrocarbons react in sunlight to form ozone and PAN, which irritate the eyes and lungs.
  • NO₂ as a catalyst in the oxidation of SO₂ in the atmosphere:
  • NO₂ + SO₂ → NO + SO₃

  • NO + ½O₂ → NO₂ (the NO₂ is regenerated)

  • Eutrophication: nitrate fertilisers wash into water and cause algal blooms. These block light; when the algae die, bacteria decompose them and use up the dissolved oxygen, so fish die.

Sulfur

  • SO₂ forms when sulfur-containing fuels burn. It dissolves in water to form H₂SO₃; it is oxidised to SO₃, which forms H₂SO₄, causing acid rain.
  • Acid rain damages buildings (limestone), forests, and aquatic life.
  • SO₂ as a food preservative: it is an antioxidant and kills microbes.
  • Contact process: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (V₂O₅ catalyst, 450 °C, about 2 atm), then SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum), then H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Explain the unreactivity of N₂ in terms of bond energy, not just "a triple bond".
  • In the NO₂ catalysis cycle, show the NO₂ is regenerated.
  • Explain eutrophication as a sequence of steps.

Quick self-check

  1. Write the equations showing how NO₂ catalyses the oxidation of SO₂.
  2. Describe the test for ammonium ions.
  3. Explain how nitrogen oxides are formed in a car engine.
