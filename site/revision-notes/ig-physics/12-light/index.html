IGCSE Physics 0625

12 Light

Reflection

  • The normal is a line at 90° to the surface at the point where the ray hits it.
  • The angle of incidence and angle of reflection are both measured from the normal.
  • Angle of incidence = angle of reflection.

Image in a plane mirror

  • same size as the object
  • same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front
  • virtual: it cannot be formed on a screen

Refraction

  • Light changes direction when it passes between materials, because its speed changes.
  • Going into a denser material such as glass, it slows down and bends towards the normal.
  • Leaving glass into air, it speeds up and bends away from the normal.
  • A ray along the normal does not bend.
  • Experiment: shine a ray box at a transparent block, mark the rays and measure the angles from the normal.

Critical angle and total internal reflection

  • The critical angle is the angle of incidence for which the angle of refraction is 90°.
  • If the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle, all the light reflects back inside. This is total internal reflection.
  • It only happens when light goes from a denser to a less dense material.
  • Examples: prisms in periscopes and binoculars, reflectors on roads, optical fibres.

Refractive index (Extended)

  • Refractive index n is the ratio of the speeds of a wave in two different regions.
  • n = sin i / sin r
  • n = 1 / sin c, where c is the critical angle.
  • Worked example: for glass with n = 1.5, sin c = 1 / 1.5 = 0.667, so c = 42°.

Optical fibres (Extended): light travels along a glass fibre by repeated total internal reflection. They carry telephone, TV and internet data in telecommunications.

Thin lenses

  • A converging lens brings a parallel beam together at a point.
  • A diverging lens spreads a parallel beam out.
  • Principal axis: the line through the centre of the lens.
  • Principal focus (focal point): the point where a parallel beam meets after a converging lens.
  • Focal length: the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus.

Drawing a ray diagram for a converging lens

  1. Draw a ray parallel to the axis. It passes through the focus on the other side.
  2. Draw a ray through the centre of the lens. It does not bend.
  3. The image forms where the rays cross.

Describing an image: use enlarged, same size or diminished; upright or inverted; real or virtual.

  • An object far away gives a real, inverted, diminished image.
  • A virtual image forms when diverging rays are traced backwards. It cannot be shown on a screen.

Extended

  • An object closer than the focal length gives a virtual, upright, enlarged image on the same side as the object. This is a magnifying glass.
  • Short sight: the image forms in front of the retina. Corrected with a diverging lens.
  • Long sight: the image forms behind the retina. Corrected with a converging lens.

Dispersion

  • White light splits into colours when refracted by a glass prism, because each colour refracts by a different amount.
  • Seven colours in order of increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength): red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
  • Red refracts least and violet refracts most.
  • Extended: light of a single frequency is monochromatic.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Measure every angle from the normal, not from the surface.
  • Use a ruler for rays, and add arrows.
  • Do not say that a virtual image is imaginary. Say it cannot be projected on a screen.

Quick self-check

  1. A ray hits a mirror at 35° to the normal. Find the angle of reflection. (35°)
  2. Light hits glass at i = 40° and r = 25°. Find n. (1.5)
  3. Which lens corrects short sight? (Diverging)
