IGCSE Physics 0625

2 Motion

Speed and velocity

  • Speed is distance travelled per unit time: v = s / t.
  • Velocity is speed in a given direction.
  • Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time taken.

Distance–time graphs

  • Flat line: at rest.
  • Straight sloping line: constant speed.
  • Getting steeper: accelerating.
  • Getting less steep: decelerating.
  • Gradient = speed.

Speed–time graphs

  • Flat line: constant speed (a line along the axis means at rest).
  • Straight line going up: constant acceleration.
  • Straight line going down: constant deceleration.
  • Curved line: changing acceleration (Extended).
  • Area under the graph = distance travelled.
  • Gradient = acceleration (Extended).

Acceleration (Extended)

  • Acceleration is change in velocity per unit time: a = Δv / Δt. The unit is m/s².
  • Deceleration is a negative acceleration.
  • Worked example: a car goes from 25 m/s to 5 m/s in 4.0 s. a = (5 − 25) / 4.0 = −5.0 m/s².

Free fall

  • Near the Earth's surface, the acceleration of free fall g is approximately constant, about 9.8 m/s².

Falling with air resistance (Extended)

  1. At first the speed is low, so air resistance is small and the object accelerates at about g.
  2. As speed increases, air resistance increases, so the resultant force and the acceleration decrease.
  3. When air resistance equals weight, the resultant force is zero. The object falls at constant terminal velocity.

Without air resistance, an object keeps accelerating at g.

Worked example: distance from a graph

A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s, then travels at 20 m/s for 15 s.

  • Triangle: ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 m
  • Rectangle: 15 × 20 = 300 m
  • Total distance = 400 m. Average speed = 400 / 25 = 16 m/s.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Always check whether the graph shows distance or speed. A flat line means different things.
  • Average speed is not the average of two speeds. Use total distance ÷ total time.
  • Convert units first, for example km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6.

Quick self-check

  1. What does the area under a speed–time graph show? (Distance travelled)
  2. A runner covers 100 m in 12.5 s. Find the average speed. (8.0 m/s)
  3. Why does a falling skydiver reach a constant speed? (Air resistance rises until it equals weight, so the resultant force is zero.)
