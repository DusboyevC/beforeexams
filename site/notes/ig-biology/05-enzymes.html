5 Enzymes

What enzymes are

  • A catalyst increases the rate of a chemical reaction and is not changed by the reaction.
  • Enzymes are proteins involved in all metabolic reactions, where they function as biological catalysts.
  • They are important because they give the reaction rate needed to sustain life.

How enzymes work

  • The enzyme's active site has a shape complementary to its substrate.
  • The substrate fits in the active site, and products form.

Extended

  1. The substrate collides with the active site.
  2. An enzyme-substrate complex forms.
  3. The substrate changes into products.
  4. The products leave and the enzyme can be used again.

Specificity (Extended): an enzyme only works on one substrate, because only that substrate has a shape that fits the active site.

Effect of temperature

  • Activity increases up to the optimum temperature.
  • Above the optimum, activity falls quickly, as the enzyme is denatured.

Extended

  • Below the optimum, a rise in temperature gives substrate and enzyme more kinetic energy, so there are more effective collisions.
  • Above the optimum, the active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits. This is denaturation, and it cannot be reversed.

Effect of pH

  • Each enzyme has an optimum pH.
  • Moving away from it, on either side, lowers activity, and the enzyme denatures.
  • Extended: extreme pH changes the shape of the active site, so the substrate no longer fits.

Investigating enzymes

Temperature with amylase and starch

  1. Set up water baths at different temperatures.
  2. Mix starch solution and amylase at each temperature.
  3. Take drops at regular intervals and test with iodine on a spotting tile.
  4. Time how long it takes until iodine no longer turns blue-black.

The fastest time is near the optimum.

pH: repeat at one temperature, with buffer solutions of different pH.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Enzymes are not killed, because they are not alive. Say denatured.
  • At low temperature, the enzyme is inactive but not denatured.
  • Link your answer to the shape of the active site.

Quick self-check

  1. What is the shape of the active site compared with the substrate? (Complementary)
  2. Why does activity fall above the optimum? (The active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits.)
  3. Is denaturation reversible? (No)
