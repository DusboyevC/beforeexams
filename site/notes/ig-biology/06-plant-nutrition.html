6 Plant nutrition

Photosynthesis

  • Photosynthesis is the process by which plants synthesise carbohydrates from raw materials, using energy from light.
  • Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, in the presence of light and chlorophyll.
  • Extended: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Chlorophyll is a green pigment found in chloroplasts. It transfers energy from light into energy in chemicals, for the synthesis of carbohydrates.

Uses of the carbohydrates

  • Starch: an energy store.
  • Cellulose: builds cell walls.
  • Glucose: used in respiration to provide energy.
  • Sucrose: transported in the phloem.
  • Nectar: attracts insects for pollination.

Mineral ions

  • Nitrate ions are needed for making amino acids. Lack of them gives poor growth with yellow leaves.
  • Magnesium ions are needed for making chlorophyll. Lack of them gives yellow leaves.

Investigations

Testing a leaf for starch

  1. Boil the leaf in water, to kill it and break the membranes.
  2. Boil it in ethanol in a water bath, to remove the chlorophyll.
  3. Dip it in water, to soften it.
  4. Add iodine solution. Blue-black shows starch.

First destarch the plant by keeping it in the dark for 48 hours.

Need for each factor (with a control)

  • Chlorophyll: test a variegated leaf. Only the green parts turn blue-black.
  • Light: cover part of a leaf with black paper. Only the uncovered part turns blue-black.
  • Carbon dioxide: put one plant in a sealed bag with soda lime, which absorbs CO₂. Use a second plant without it as the control.

Rate of photosynthesis: count oxygen bubbles from pondweed, changing one factor at a time.

  • Light intensity: move a lamp nearer. The rate rises, then levels off.
  • Carbon dioxide: add sodium hydrogencarbonate. The rate rises, then levels off.
  • Temperature: the rate rises to an optimum, then falls as enzymes denature.

Gas exchange and hydrogencarbonate indicator

Condition Change Indicator
Bright light, photosynthesis faster than respiration CO₂ falls purple
Medium light, equal rates no change orange-red
Dark, only respiration CO₂ rises yellow

Limiting factors (Extended)

A limiting factor is the one in shortest supply that keeps the rate low. It is usually light, carbon dioxide or temperature.

  • At night: light is limiting.
  • On a sunny, warm day: carbon dioxide is usually limiting.
  • On a cold, bright day: temperature may be limiting.
  • If the rate is on a rising line, the factor on the x-axis is limiting. On a level part, another factor is limiting.

Leaf structure

Most leaves have a large surface area to absorb light and are thin so gases can diffuse quickly.

Structure How it adapts the leaf
Cuticle waxy and waterproof, reduces water loss
Upper epidermis transparent, lets light through
Palisade mesophyll packed with chloroplasts, absorbs most of the light
Spongy mesophyll has air spaces for gas diffusion
Air spaces let CO₂ reach cells
Stomata and guard cells let gases in and out; guard cells open and close them
Xylem brings water and mineral ions
Phloem carries sucrose and amino acids away

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Test leaves for starch, not for glucose.
  • A plant respires all the time, in light and dark.
  • Keep a control in every investigation, and change one factor only.

Quick self-check

  1. Why is a leaf boiled in ethanol? (To remove the chlorophyll.)
  2. Which ion is needed for chlorophyll? (Magnesium)
  3. What colour is hydrogencarbonate indicator in high CO₂? (Yellow)
