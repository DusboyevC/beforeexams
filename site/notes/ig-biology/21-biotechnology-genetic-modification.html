21 Biotechnology and genetic modification

Why use bacteria

  • They reproduce rapidly.
  • They can make complex molecules.
  • Extended: there are few ethical concerns over their manipulation and growth.
  • Extended: they have plasmids, which can carry new genes.

Biotechnology

  • Biofuels: yeast respires anaerobically to make ethanol from sugars.
  • Bread: yeast respires anaerobically to make carbon dioxide, and the bubbles make the dough rise.
  • Fruit juice: pectinase breaks down pectin in cell walls, so more juice is released and it is clearer.
  • Biological washing powders: contain enzymes that break down stains, working well at lower temperatures.

Extended

  • Lactose-free milk: lactase breaks down lactose into glucose and galactose, for people who are lactose intolerant.

Fermenters (Extended)

Fermenters grow bacteria and fungi on a large scale, to make products such as insulin, penicillin and mycoprotein.

Condition Why it is controlled
Temperature for the best enzyme activity; cooled as respiration releases heat
pH keeps enzymes at their optimum
Oxygen needed for aerobic respiration
Nutrient supply provides energy and materials for growth
Waste products removed so they do not harm the organisms

Genetic modification

Genetic modification is changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.

Examples

  • human genes in bacteria, to produce human proteins such as insulin
  • genes in crop plants for resistance to herbicides
  • genes in crop plants for resistance to insect pests
  • genes in crop plants to improve nutritional qualities

The process (Extended)

  1. Cut out the human gene with restriction enzymes, forming sticky ends.
  2. Cut bacterial plasmid DNA with the same restriction enzymes, forming complementary sticky ends.
  3. Join human DNA to plasmid DNA with DNA ligase, forming a recombinant plasmid.
  4. Insert the recombinant plasmids into bacteria.
  5. Allow the bacteria to multiply.
  6. The bacteria express the human gene and make the human protein.

GM crops such as soya, maize and rice (Extended)

Advantages Disadvantages
higher yields seed may be expensive
less insecticide needed genes may spread to wild plants
weeds can be sprayed without harming the crop less biodiversity
better nutrition, as in vitamin-rich rice possible health concerns

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Use the same restriction enzyme on both pieces of DNA.
  • Ligase joins DNA, and restriction enzymes cut it.
  • Yeast makes bread rise with carbon dioxide. The alcohol evaporates in baking.

Quick self-check

  1. Which gas makes bread rise? (Carbon dioxide)
  2. What does pectinase do? (It breaks down pectin, releasing more juice.)
  3. Why must a fermenter be cooled? (Respiration by the microorganisms releases heat.)
