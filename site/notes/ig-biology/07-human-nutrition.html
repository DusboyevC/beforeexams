7 Human nutrition

Balanced diet

A balanced diet contains all the nutrients in the right amounts for a person's needs.

Nutrient Sources Importance
Carbohydrates bread, rice, pasta energy
Fats and oils butter, oil, nuts energy store, insulation
Proteins meat, fish, eggs, beans growth and repair
Vitamin C citrus fruit healthy skin and gums
Vitamin D oily fish, eggs, sunlight on skin helps absorb calcium
Calcium milk, cheese strong bones and teeth
Iron red meat, spinach making haemoglobin
Fibre vegetables, wholegrains helps food move through the gut, prevents constipation
Water drinks, food solvent, transport
  • Scurvy is caused by a lack of vitamin C.
  • Rickets is caused by a lack of vitamin D or calcium.

The digestive system

Alimentary canal: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (duodenum and ileum), large intestine (colon, rectum, anus). Associated organs: salivary glands, pancreas, liver, gall bladder.

  • Ingestion: taking substances such as food and drink into the body.
  • Digestion: the breakdown of food.
  • Absorption: movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood.
  • Assimilation: uptake and use of nutrients by cells.
  • Egestion: removal of undigested food from the body as faeces.

Physical digestion

  • Physical digestion is the breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules.
  • It increases the surface area for the action of enzymes.
  • Stomach: muscular walls churn the food.
  • Bile emulsifies fats and oils, increasing their surface area for chemical digestion.

Teeth

  • Incisors: chisel-shaped, for biting.
  • Canines: pointed, for tearing and gripping.
  • Premolars and molars: broad, for grinding and chewing.

Structure of a tooth: outer enamel, then dentine, then pulp containing nerves and blood vessels. Cement fixes the tooth in the jawbone. Teeth are embedded in bone and gums.

Chemical digestion

  • Chemical digestion is the breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules, which can be absorbed.
Enzyme Substrate → product Secreted by Acts in
Amylase starch → simple reducing sugars salivary glands, pancreas mouth, small intestine
Protease protein → amino acids stomach, pancreas stomach, small intestine
Lipase fats and oils → fatty acids and glycerol pancreas small intestine

Hydrochloric acid in gastric juice kills harmful microorganisms in food and gives an acidic pH for optimum enzyme activity.

Extended

  • Amylase breaks starch to maltose. Maltase breaks maltose to glucose, on the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine.
  • Pepsin breaks down protein in the acidic stomach.
  • Trypsin breaks down protein in the alkaline small intestine.
  • Bile is alkaline, so it neutralises the acid mixture entering the duodenum, giving a suitable pH for enzymes.

Absorption

  • The small intestine is where nutrients are absorbed.
  • Most water is absorbed in the small intestine, and some in the colon.

Extended: villi

  • Villi and microvilli greatly increase the internal surface area.
  • A villus has a thin wall, one cell thick, with a good blood supply.
  • Capillaries absorb glucose and amino acids.
  • Lacteals absorb fatty acids and glycerol.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Bile is not an enzyme. It emulsifies fats.
  • Bile is made by the liver and stored in the gall bladder.
  • Egestion removes undigested food. Excretion removes metabolic waste.

Quick self-check

  1. Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy? (Vitamin C)
  2. Give the products of lipase. (Fatty acids and glycerol)
  3. How do villi help absorption? (They increase the surface area.)
