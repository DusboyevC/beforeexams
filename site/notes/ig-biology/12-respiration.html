12 Respiration

Uses of energy

Energy from respiration is used for:

  • muscle contraction
  • protein synthesis
  • cell division
  • active transport
  • growth
  • the passage of nerve impulses
  • maintaining a constant body temperature

Aerobic respiration

  • Aerobic respiration is the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy.
  • Word equation: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
  • Extended: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

Anaerobic respiration

  • Anaerobic respiration is the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen.
  • It releases much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.
  • In yeast: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide
  • Extended, in yeast: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
  • In muscles during vigorous exercise: glucose → lactic acid

Oxygen debt (Extended)

Lactic acid builds up in muscles and blood during vigorous exercise, causing an oxygen debt.

It is removed after exercise by:

  • a continued fast heart rate, transporting lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver
  • continued deeper and faster breathing, supplying oxygen for aerobic respiration of lactic acid
  • aerobic respiration of lactic acid in the liver

Effect of temperature on respiration in yeast

  1. Mix yeast with glucose solution in a test tube, in a water bath.
  2. Add a layer of oil, to keep oxygen out.
  3. Count the bubbles of carbon dioxide per minute.
  4. Repeat at different temperatures.

The rate rises to an optimum, then falls as the enzymes denature.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Respiration is not breathing. It happens in every living cell.
  • Respiration releases energy. It does not make energy.
  • In muscles, anaerobic respiration gives no carbon dioxide.

Quick self-check

  1. Give the word equation for aerobic respiration. (Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water)
  2. What does yeast make without oxygen? (Alcohol and carbon dioxide)
  3. Why do you keep breathing fast after exercise? (To supply oxygen to respire lactic acid.)
