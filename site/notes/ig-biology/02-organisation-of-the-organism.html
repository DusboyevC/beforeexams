2 Organisation of the organism

Cell structures and functions

Structure Function Plant Animal Bacterium
Cell wall supports and keeps the shape yes no yes
Cell membrane controls what enters and leaves yes yes yes
Nucleus contains DNA, controls the cell yes yes no

Mitochondria aerobic respiration yes yes no
Large vacuole stores cell sap, keeps the cell firm yes no no
Circular DNA controls the cell no no yes
Plasmids small rings of extra DNA no no yes

New cells are produced by division of existing cells.

Specialised cells

  • Ciliated cells: move mucus in the trachea and bronchi.
  • Root hair cells: absorption.
  • Palisade mesophyll cells: photosynthesis.
  • Neurones: conduct electrical impulses.
  • Red blood cells: transport oxygen.
  • Sperm and egg cells: reproduction.

Levels of organisation

  • Cell: the basic unit, such as a muscle cell.
  • Tissue: a group of similar cells working together, such as muscle tissue.
  • Organ: different tissues working together, such as the stomach.
  • Organ system: organs working together, such as the digestive system.
  • Organism: a living thing made of organ systems.

Magnification

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size

Use the same units for both.

Worked example: a cell drawing is 40 mm long. The cell is really 0.2 mm long. Magnification = 40 ÷ 0.2 = ×200.

Extended: 1 mm = 1000 μm.

Worked example (Extended): an image is 30 mm at ×1500. Actual size = 30 ÷ 1500 = 0.02 mm = 20 μm.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Mitochondria release energy. They do not make energy.
  • Convert to the same unit before you divide.
  • Magnification has no unit. Write ×200.
  • Bacteria have no nucleus, but they do have DNA.
