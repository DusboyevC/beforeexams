6 Electrochemistry (AS)

Redox

  • Oxidation: loss of electrons, or an increase in oxidation number. Reduction: gain of electrons, or a decrease in oxidation number. (OIL RIG)
  • Oxidising agent: accepts electrons and is itself reduced. Reducing agent: donates electrons and is itself oxidised.
  • Disproportionation: the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced, e.g. Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O.

Oxidation number rules

  1. Elements = 0.
  2. In a compound, the oxidation numbers add up to 0; in an ion, to the charge on the ion.
  3. Group 1 = +1, Group 2 = +2, F = −1.
  4. H = +1 (except in metal hydrides, where it is −1).
  5. O = −2 (except in peroxides, −1, and in OF₂, +2). - Roman numerals give the oxidation number in names: iron(III) chloride, FeCl₃; chlorate(V), ClO₃⁻.

Balancing redox equations

Half-equation method (acidic solution):

  1. Balance the atoms other than O and H.
  2. Add H₂O to balance O.
  3. Add H⁺ to balance H.
  4. Add e⁻ to balance the charge.
  5. Multiply the half-equations so the electrons cancel, then add them.

Example: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O and Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ (× 5) give MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺

Oxidation number method: find the total change in oxidation number for each species, and make the increase equal the decrease.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Check that both atoms and charge balance in the final equation.
  • Write the oxidation number with its sign (+3, not 3+); 3+ is an ion charge.
  • Name the oxidising agent as the species that is reduced, e.g. "MnO₄⁻ is the oxidising agent".

Quick self-check

  1. Find the oxidation number of Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻. (+6)
  2. Write the half-equation for Cr₂O₇²⁻ forming Cr³⁺ in acid. (Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O)
  3. Explain why the reaction of chlorine with water is a disproportionation.
