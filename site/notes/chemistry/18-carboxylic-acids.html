18 Carboxylic acids and derivatives (AS)

Making carboxylic acids

  • Oxidise a primary alcohol or an aldehyde with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, heating under reflux.
  • Hydrolyse a nitrile by heating under reflux with dilute acid: RCN + 2H₂O + H⁺ → RCOOH + NH₄⁺.
  • Hydrolyse an ester.

Reactions of carboxylic acids

  • They are weak acids: RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺.
  • With reactive metals: a salt + H₂. With alkalis: a salt + water. With carbonates: a salt + water + CO₂ (fizzing; this is a test for –COOH).
  • Esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, with concentrated H₂SO₄ as catalyst, heating under reflux.

  • Example: ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate + water. Name the alcohol part first: ethyl, then -oate.

  • Reduction with LiAlH₄ gives a primary alcohol.

Esters

  • Uses: perfumes and flavourings (fruity smells), and solvents.
  • Hydrolysis:
  • Acid hydrolysis (dilute acid, reflux) is reversible and gives the acid + alcohol.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis (NaOH, reflux) goes to completion and gives the carboxylate salt + alcohol.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Name esters as alkyl alkanoate, from the alcohol part plus the acid part.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis gives a salt, not the acid; acidify afterwards to get the acid.
  • The test for a carboxylic acid: add Na₂CO₃ and look for effervescence of CO₂, which turns limewater milky.

Quick self-check

  1. Name the ester formed from propanoic acid and methanol. (methyl propanoate)
  2. Write the equation for the alkaline hydrolysis of ethyl ethanoate. (CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH)
  3. Give two ways to make ethanoic acid.
