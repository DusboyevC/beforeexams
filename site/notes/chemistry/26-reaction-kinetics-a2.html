26 Reaction kinetics (A Level)

Rate equations

  • Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ. m and n are the orders, found by experiment (not from the balanced equation). The overall order = m + n.
  • Rate constant k: depends on temperature (it increases as temperature increases). Work out its units from the rate equation.

Finding orders

  • Initial rates method: change the concentration of one reactant at a time.
  • Concentration doubles, rate unchanged → order 0.
  • Rate doubles → order 1.

  • Rate quadruples → order 2.

  • Concentration–time graphs:

  • Zero order: a straight line going down.
  • First order: a curve with a constant half-life.

  • Second order: a curve whose half-life increases.

  • Rate–concentration graphs: zero order is horizontal; first order is a straight line through the origin; second order is a curve upwards (a parabola).

  • First order half-life: k = 0.693/t½ (the half-life does not depend on concentration).

Mechanisms

  • Rate-determining step: the slowest step in the mechanism. Species in the rate equation appear in (or before) this step.
  • Example: if rate = k[(CH₃)₃CBr], the slow step involves only (CH₃)₃CBr, so the mechanism is S_N1. For primary halogenoalkanes, rate = k[RBr][OH⁻], which is S_N2.
  • A proposed mechanism must add up to the overall equation and agree with the rate equation.

Catalysis

  • Homogeneous catalysis works through an intermediate that is formed and then used up.
  • Example: Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ catalysing S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂. Two negative ions repel, so the direct reaction is slow; Fe²⁺ reduces S₂O₈²⁻, and the Fe³⁺ formed then oxidises I⁻.

  • Example: NO₂ in the atmospheric oxidation of SO₂.

  • Heterogeneous catalysis (e.g. Fe in the Haber process; catalytic converters): 1. adsorption of the reactants onto active sites on the surface; 2. bonds in the reactants weaken and react; 3. desorption of the products.

Worked example

[A] [B] Rate
0.10 0.10 2.0 × 10⁻³
0.20 0.10 8.0 × 10⁻³
0.10 0.20 2.0 × 10⁻³
Doubling [A] quadruples the rate, so the reaction is 2nd order in A; [B] has no effect, so it is zero order in B.
Rate = k[A]², so k = 2.0 × 10⁻³ / 0.01 = 0.20 dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Orders come from experimental data, not the equation.
  • Always give the units of k.
  • Link the rate-determining step to the rate equation: "both A and B appear in the rate equation, so both take part in the slow step".

Quick self-check

  1. Give the units of k for a third-order overall reaction. (dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹)
  2. A first-order reaction has a half-life of 40 s. Find k. (0.0173 s⁻¹)
  3. Describe how a heterogeneous catalyst works.
