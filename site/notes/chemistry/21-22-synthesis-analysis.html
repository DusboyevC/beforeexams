21 Organic synthesis (AS)

Key reaction map (learn the reagents and conditions)

  • Alkane → halogenoalkane: Cl₂ or Br₂, UV light.
  • Alkene → alkane: H₂, Ni, 150 °C. → Alcohol: steam, H₃PO₄, 300 °C, 60 atm. → Halogenoalkane: HX. → Dihalide: X₂. → Diol: cold dilute acidified KMnO₄.
  • Halogenoalkane → alcohol: NaOH(aq), reflux. → Nitrile: KCN in ethanol, reflux. → Amine: excess NH₃ in ethanol, sealed tube. → Alkene: NaOH in ethanol, heat.
  • Alcohol → alkene: concentrated H₂SO₄ or Al₂O₃, heat. → Halogenoalkane: PCl₅, SOCl₂ or HX. → Aldehyde / ketone / acid: acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (distil for an aldehyde, reflux for an acid). → Ester: carboxylic acid + concentrated H₂SO₄.
  • Aldehyde or ketone → alcohol: NaBH₄. → Hydroxynitrile: HCN / KCN.
  • Nitrile → carboxylic acid: dilute HCl, reflux.
  • In multi-step routes, give the intermediate, the reagent, and the conditions for each step.
  • Increasing the chain length by one carbon: halogenoalkane + KCN → nitrile, or carbonyl + HCN.

22 Analytical techniques (AS)

Infrared spectroscopy

  • Bonds absorb IR radiation at characteristic frequencies (wavenumbers, cm⁻¹), which makes them vibrate (stretch or bend).
Bond Wavenumber / cm⁻¹ Appearance
O–H (alcohol) 3200–3600 Broad
O–H (carboxylic acid) 2500–3000 Very broad
N–H 3300–3500 Medium
C–H 2850–2950 Sharp
C=O 1670–1740 Strong, sharp
C=C 1620–1680 Medium
C–O 1040–1300 —
- Uses: identify functional groups; monitor air pollution (CO, NOₓ); breathalysers (the C–H peak of ethanol).

Mass spectrometry

  • Molecular ion peak M⁺: the peak with the highest m/z (ignoring M+1) gives the Mᵣ.
  • M+1 peak: due to ¹³C. Number of carbon atoms = (height of M+1 ÷ height of M⁺) × 100 / 1.1.
  • M+2 peak: due to Cl or Br isotopes. Cl gives M⁺ : M+2 = 3 : 1; Br gives 1 : 1.
  • Fragmentation peaks show parts of the molecule, e.g. m/z 15 = CH₃⁺, 29 = C₂H₅⁺ or CHO⁺, 43 = C₃H₇⁺ or CH₃CO⁺.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • In IR, quote the range and the bond; for a carboxylic acid, mention both the broad O–H and the C=O.
  • In the M+1 calculation, the ratio is (M+1)/M, multiplied by 100/1.1.
  • Fragment ions are positive and must carry a + charge.

Quick self-check

  1. A compound shows a broad peak at 2500–3000 and a strong peak at 1710 cm⁻¹. Identify the functional group. (carboxylic acid)
  2. M⁺ = 60% and M+1 = 2.0% relative abundance. How many carbon atoms are there? (3)
  3. Give the reagents for converting ethanol into propanoic acid in three steps. (PCl₅ → chloroethane; KCN in ethanol → propanenitrile; dilute HCl, reflux → propanoic acid)
