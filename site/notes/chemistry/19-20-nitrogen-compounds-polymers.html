19 Nitrogen compounds (AS)

Amines

  • Primary amine: RNH₂. It is made by heating a halogenoalkane with excess ammonia in ethanol in a sealed tube: RBr + 2NH₃ → RNH₂ + NH₄Br.

  • Excess ammonia reduces further substitution, which would give secondary and tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts.

  • Nitriles are made from halogenoalkanes and KCN in ethanol. Nitriles can be:

  • hydrolysed to carboxylic acids (dilute acid, reflux), or

  • reduced to primary amines (LiAlH₄, or H₂ with a Ni catalyst).

  • Hydroxynitriles are made from carbonyl compounds and HCN (topic 17).

20 Polymerisation (AS)

Addition polymerisation

  • Monomers with a C=C double bond join together; the polymer is the only product.
  • Example: n CH₂=CHCl → –[CH₂–CHCl]ₙ– (poly(chloroethene), PVC).
  • Repeat unit: draw two carbons with the substituents, and extension bonds going out through the brackets.
  • Identifying the monomer: take the repeat unit and put back the C=C.
  • Disposal problems:
  • Poly(alkenes) are non-biodegradable (strong, non-polar C–C and C–H bonds), so they persist in landfill and the environment.
  • Burning releases CO₂ (a greenhouse gas), and toxic gases such as HCl from PVC.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • In a repeat unit, the extension bonds must pass through the brackets, and there is no double bond in the polymer chain.
  • State that addition polymerisation forms no other product. Condensation polymerisation (A Level) forms a small molecule such as water.

  2. Explain why poly(alkenes) are non-biodegradable.
  3. Give the reagents and conditions to convert bromoethane into ethylamine. (Excess NH₃ in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube)
