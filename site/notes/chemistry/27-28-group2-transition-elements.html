27 Group 2 (A Level)

  • Thermal stability of carbonates and nitrates increases down the group: the cation radius increases, so its charge density decreases and it polarises the large anion less.
  • Solubility of hydroxides and sulfates: explained using lattice energy and hydration enthalpy (see topic 23). For sulfates, the hydration enthalpy falls by more than the lattice energy, so ΔH_sol becomes more endothermic and the sulfates become less soluble down the group.

28 Chemistry of transition elements

Definitions and properties

  • Transition element: a d-block element that forms one or more stable ions with an incomplete d subshell. Sc and Zn are not transition elements (Sc³⁺ is d⁰; Zn²⁺ is d¹⁰).
  • Properties: variable oxidation states; coloured compounds; catalytic activity; they form complex ions.
  • Variable oxidation states: the 3d and 4s electrons have similar energies, so different numbers can be lost.

Complexes

  • Ligand: a species with a lone pair of electrons that forms a dative covalent bond to a central metal ion.
  • Monodentate (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻), bidentate (ethanedioate, ethane-1,2-diamine "en"), polydentate (EDTA⁴⁻ is hexadentate).
  • Co-ordination number: the number of dative bonds to the central ion.
  • Shapes: octahedral (6, e.g. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺); tetrahedral (4, e.g. [CuCl₄]²⁻); square planar (4, e.g. cisplatin); linear (2, e.g. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺).
  • Isomerism: cis/trans (square planar or octahedral complexes); optical isomerism (octahedral complexes with three bidentate ligands, e.g. [Ni(en)₃]²⁺). Cisplatin is cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], an anticancer drug.

Colour

  • In a complex, the d orbitals split into two energy levels (ΔE), caused by the ligands.
  • The ion absorbs light of the frequency that matches ΔE (hf = ΔE), promoting an electron to the higher level. The colour seen is the complementary colour of the light absorbed (the light transmitted).
  • A different ligand, oxidation state or co-ordination number gives a different ΔE, so a different colour.
  • d⁰ and d¹⁰ ions are colourless, because no d–d transition is possible.

Ligand exchange (copper(II))

  • [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (blue) + a little NH₃ or OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ (pale blue precipitate).
  • With excess NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ (deep blue solution).
  • With concentrated HCl → [CuCl₄]²⁻ (yellow; tetrahedral, because Cl⁻ is large).
  • Stability constant: Kstab = [complex]/([metal ion][ligand]ⁿ). A larger Kstab means a more stable complex. Polydentate ligands form very stable complexes because of an entropy increase (more particles are released).

Redox titrations

  • MnO₄⁻ (purple → colourless, it acts as its own indicator) in acid: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.
  • Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange → green).
  • Iodine–thiosulfate: I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻, with a starch indicator (blue-black → colourless).

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Define a transition element using ions with an incomplete d subshell.
  • Colour: d-orbital splitting, absorption of certain frequencies, and the complementary colour seen.
  • In ligand exchange, give the formula, the colour, and the type of observation (precipitate or solution).
