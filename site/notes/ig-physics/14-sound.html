14 Sound

How sound is made and travels

  • Sound is produced by vibrating sources.
  • Sound waves are longitudinal. The air vibrates parallel to the direction of travel.
  • A medium is needed. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum.
  • Extended: compressions are regions where particles are close together, at higher pressure. Rarefactions are regions where particles are spread out, at lower pressure.

Hearing and speed

  • Humans can hear about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz.
  • The speed of sound in air is about 330–350 m/s.
  • Extended: sound generally travels fastest in solids, then liquids, and slowest in gases.

Measuring the speed of sound

  1. Stand a measured distance from a large wall, for example 100 m.
  2. Clap and listen for the echo. Adjust the rate of clapping until each clap matches the echo.
  3. Time 20 claps. Find the time for one clap.
  4. The sound travels 200 m in that time.
  5. Speed = distance ÷ time.

Another method: one person fires a starting pistol, and another far away times from seeing the flash to hearing the bang.

Loudness and pitch

  • Greater amplitude means louder.
  • Higher frequency means higher pitch.

Echoes

  • An echo is the reflection of sound waves. Hard flat surfaces reflect sound best.
  • The sound travels there and back, so distance to the wall = speed × time ÷ 2.

Ultrasound

  • Ultrasound is sound with a frequency above 20 kHz.

Uses (Extended)

  • Non-destructive testing: pulses reflect from cracks inside metal.
  • Medical scanning of soft tissue: for example, checking an unborn baby. It is safe, unlike X-rays.
  • Sonar: pulses reflect from the sea bed or from fish.

Worked example (Extended): a sonar pulse returns after 0.40 s. Speed in water is 1500 m/s. Distance there and back = 1500 × 0.40 = 600 m. Depth = 300 m.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Divide by 2 in echo questions.
  • Pitch depends on frequency, not on amplitude.
  • A longer wave on a screen means lower frequency if the time scale is the same.

Quick self-check

  1. Can you hear a sound of 25 kHz? (No, it is ultrasound.)
  2. An echo returns after 2.0 s at 340 m/s. How far away is the cliff? (340 m)
  3. Why can't sound travel in space? (There is no medium to carry the vibrations.)
