22 Stars and the Universe

The Sun as a star

  • The Sun is a star of medium size, mostly hydrogen and helium.
  • It radiates most of its energy as infrared, visible light and ultraviolet.
  • Extended: stars are powered by nuclear reactions. In stable stars, this is the fusion of hydrogen into helium.

Galaxies and distances

  • Galaxies are each made up of many billions of stars.
  • The Sun is a star in the Milky Way.
  • Other stars in the Milky Way are much further from the Earth than the Sun is.
  • A light-year is the distance travelled in space by light in one year. It is a distance, not a time.
  • 1 light-year = 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m.

Life cycle of a star (Extended)

  1. A star forms from interstellar clouds of gas and dust containing hydrogen.
  2. A protostar is a cloud collapsing and heating up because of its own gravity.
  3. It becomes a stable star when the inward force of gravity is balanced by an outward force due to the high temperature at the centre.
  4. All stars eventually run out of hydrogen fuel.

Less massive stars

  • expand to form a red giant
  • then form a planetary nebula with a white dwarf at its centre

More massive stars

  • expand to form a red supergiant
  • then explode as a supernova, forming a nebula of hydrogen and new heavier elements
  • leave behind a neutron star or a black hole

The nebula from a supernova may form new stars with orbiting planets.

The Universe (Extended)

  • The Milky Way is one of many billions of galaxies. Its diameter is about 100 000 light-years.

Redshift

  • Redshift is an increase in the observed wavelength of electromagnetic radiation from receding stars and galaxies.
  • Light from distant galaxies appears redshifted compared with light emitted on Earth.
  • This is evidence that the Universe is expanding, and it supports the Big Bang Theory.

Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR)

  • Microwave radiation of a specific frequency is observed at all points in space.
  • It was produced shortly after the Universe formed. It has been expanded into the microwave region as the Universe expanded.

The Hubble constant

  • The speed v of a galaxy moving away is found from the redshift of its starlight.
  • The distance d of a far galaxy is found from the brightness of a supernova in that galaxy.
  • H₀ = v / d. The current estimate of H₀ is 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second.
  • d / v = 1 / H₀ is an estimate for the age of the Universe.
  • This is evidence that all the matter in the Universe was present at a single point.

Worked example: 1 / (2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸) = 4.5 × 10¹⁷ s. Dividing by 3.2 × 10⁷ s in a year gives about 14 billion years.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Redshift means wavelength gets longer. It does not mean the star looks red.
  • Learn the two life-cycle paths in order.
  • A light-year measures distance.

Quick self-check

  1. What balances gravity in a stable star? (An outward force due to the high temperature at the centre.)
  2. What is left after a supernova? (A neutron star or a black hole, in a nebula.)
  3. What two pieces of evidence support the Big Bang? (Redshift of distant galaxies and the CMBR)
