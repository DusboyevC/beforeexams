15 Simple magnetism

Magnets and poles

  • A magnet has a north pole (N) and a south pole (S).
  • Like poles repel. Unlike poles attract.
  • A magnet attracts magnetic materials such as iron, steel, nickel and cobalt. These can be magnetised or unmagnetised.
  • Non-magnetic materials, such as wood, plastic, copper and aluminium, are not attracted.
  • Repulsion is the only sure test that an object is a magnet.

Induced magnetism

  • A magnet near a piece of unmagnetised iron or steel makes it a magnet too. This is induced magnetism.
  • The near end gets the opposite pole, so the two attract.

Magnetic fields

  • A magnetic field is a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.
  • The direction of the field at a point is the direction of the force on the N pole of a magnet at that point.
  • Field lines go from N to S outside a magnet.
  • Extended: magnetic forces are due to interactions between magnetic fields.
  • Extended: the closer the field lines, the stronger the field.

Plotting field lines

  • Iron filings: put a sheet of paper on a magnet, sprinkle filings and tap gently. They show the pattern but not the direction.
  • Plotting compass: place it near the magnet and mark the direction the needle points. Move it along and repeat, then join the dots. This shows the direction.

Uses

  • Permanent magnets: fridge door seals, compasses, loudspeakers, motors and generators.
  • Electromagnets: scrapyard cranes, relays, electric bells. They are useful because they can be switched on and off.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Field lines never cross.
  • Draw arrows on field lines going from N to S.
  • Attraction does not prove something is a magnet. A magnetic material is also attracted.

Quick self-check

  1. What happens when two south poles are brought together? (They repel.)
  2. Why is soft iron used in an electromagnet? (It loses its magnetism when the current stops.)
  3. Which way does a compass needle point near a magnet's north pole? (Its N pole points away from the magnet's north pole.)
