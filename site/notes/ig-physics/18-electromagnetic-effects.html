18 Electromagnetic effects

Electromagnetic induction

  • A conductor moving across a magnetic field, or a changing magnetic field linking with a conductor, can induce an e.m.f. in the conductor.
  • Experiment: connect a wire to a sensitive voltmeter or galvanometer and move it between the poles of a magnet. Or push a magnet into a coil. The meter deflects only while there is movement.

The induced e.m.f. increases with

  • faster movement
  • a stronger magnetic field
  • more turns on the coil

Extended

  • The direction of an induced e.m.f. opposes the change causing it.
  • The relative directions of force, field and induced current are at right angles. Use Fleming's right-hand rule: thumb for motion, first finger for field, second finger for current.

The a.c. generator

  • A coil rotates in a magnetic field, or a magnet rotates inside a coil.
  • Slip rings and brushes connect the rotating coil to the outside circuit.
  • Graph of e.m.f. against time: a smooth wave. The e.m.f. is largest when the plane of the coil is parallel to the field, because the sides cut the field fastest. It is zero when the coil is perpendicular to the field.
  • Faster rotation gives taller and closer peaks.

Magnetic effect of a current

  • Straight wire: circular field lines around the wire. Use the right-hand grip rule: thumb along the current, fingers show the field.
  • Solenoid: like a bar magnet. The field inside is strong and uniform.
  • Experiment: pass the wire through a card, sprinkle iron filings, and use a plotting compass for the direction.
  • Extended: the field is stronger closer to the wire and inside a solenoid.
  • Extended: more current gives a stronger field. Reversing the current reverses the field.

Uses

  • Relay: a current in a coil magnetises an iron core, which pulls a switch closed.
  • Loudspeaker: a varying current in a coil, inside a magnetic field, moves the coil and the cone back and forth, making sound.

Force on a current-carrying conductor

  • A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force. Reversing the current or the field reverses the force.
  • Extended: use Fleming's left-hand rule: thumb for force, first finger for field, second finger for conventional current.
  • Extended: a beam of charged particles is also deflected. Positive particles act like conventional current, and electrons act like current in the opposite direction.

The d.c. motor

  • A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field may experience a turning effect.
  • The turning effect increases with more turns, more current and a stronger magnetic field.
  • Extended: current flows in opposite directions in the two sides of the coil, so one side is pushed up and the other down.
  • Extended: the split-ring commutator reverses the current every half turn, so the coil keeps turning the same way. Brushes connect it to the supply.

The transformer

  • A soft-iron core with a primary coil and a secondary coil.
  • Step-up: more turns on the secondary, so voltage goes up. Step-down: fewer turns on the secondary.
  • Vₚ / Vₛ = Nₚ / Nₛ

How it works (Extended): alternating current in the primary makes a changing magnetic field in the core. This changing field links with the secondary coil and induces an alternating e.m.f. It only works with a.c.

At 100% efficiency (Extended): IₚVₚ = IₛVₛ.

Worked example: 230 V supply, 1000 primary turns, 50 secondary turns. Vₛ = 230 × 50 / 1000 = 11.5 V.

High-voltage transmission

  • Step-up transformers raise the voltage at the power station, and step-down transformers lower it near homes.
  • Advantages: less energy is lost in the cables, and thinner, cheaper cables can be used.
  • Extended: P = I²R. For the same power, a higher voltage means a smaller current, so the power lost as heat in the cables is much smaller.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Use the left hand for motors and the right hand for generators.
  • A transformer needs a.c., because a steady current gives no changing field.
  • Say a current or e.m.f. is induced only while the field is changing or the wire is moving.

Quick self-check

  1. How can you increase the e.m.f. from a generator? (Rotate it faster, use a stronger magnet or use more turns.)
  2. A transformer has 200 primary and 800 secondary turns and a 12 V input. Find the output. (48 V)
  3. What does the commutator do? (It reverses the current every half turn.)
