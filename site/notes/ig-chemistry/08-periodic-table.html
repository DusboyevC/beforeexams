8 The Periodic Table

How it is arranged

  • Elements are arranged in periods (rows) and groups (columns), in order of increasing proton number.
  • Across a period, elements change from metallic to non-metallic.
  • The group number links to the charge on the ion: Group I gives 1+, Group II gives 2+, Group III gives 3+, Group VI gives 2−, Group VII gives 1−.
  • Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties, because they have the same number of outer shell electrons.
  • An element's position can be used to predict its properties.

Group I: alkali metals

Lithium, sodium and potassium are relatively soft metals. Going down the group:

  • melting point decreases
  • density increases
  • reactivity increases

With cold water they form an alkaline hydroxide and hydrogen: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

Prediction example: rubidium is below potassium, so it is softer, has a lower melting point and reacts more violently.

Group VII: halogens

Chlorine, bromine and iodine are diatomic non-metals. Going down the group:

  • density increases
  • reactivity decreases
Halogen Appearance at r.t.p.
Chlorine pale yellow-green gas
Bromine red-brown liquid
Iodine grey-black solid

Displacement reactions: a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halide from solution.

  • Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂. The solution turns orange-brown.
  • Cl₂ + 2KI → 2KCl + I₂. The solution turns brown.
  • Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂
  • Iodine does not displace chloride or bromide.

Ionic equation: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂

Transition elements

They are metals that:

  • have high densities
  • have high melting points
  • form coloured compounds
  • often act as catalysts, as elements and in compounds

Extended: their ions have variable oxidation numbers, including iron(II) and iron(III).

Noble gases

  • Group VIII noble gases are unreactive, monatomic gases.
  • They have a full outer shell, so they do not need to lose, gain or share electrons.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Group I gets more reactive down the group. Group VII gets less reactive down the group.
  • When explaining trends, refer to the group data you are given.
  • Chlorine is yellow-green and bromine is red-brown. Learn these exact words.

Quick self-check

  1. Would chlorine displace iodine from sodium iodide? (Yes)
  2. Why are all the Group I elements similar? (They all have one outer shell electron.)
  3. Why is argon unreactive? (It has a full outer shell.)
