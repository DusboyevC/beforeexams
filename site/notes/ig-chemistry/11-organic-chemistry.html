11 Organic chemistry

Key terms

  • Displayed formula: shows all the atoms and all the bonds.
  • Homologous series: a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties, due to the same functional group.
  • Functional group: an atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a series.
  • Saturated: all carbon–carbon bonds are single.
  • Unsaturated: one or more carbon–carbon bonds are not single.

Extended

  • A structural formula shows how atoms are arranged, such as CH₂=CH₂, CH₃CH₂OH and CH₃COOCH₃.
  • Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. C₄H₁₀ can be CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ or CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃. C₄H₈ can be CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ or CH₃CH=CHCH₃.
  • A homologous series has the same functional group, the same general formula, a –CH₂– difference between members, a trend in physical properties and similar chemical properties.

Names by carbon number: meth- (1), eth- (2), prop- (3), but- (4). The number in a name shows the position, as in but-1-ene, propan-2-ol and butan-1-ol.

Esters (Extended) are named from the alcohol, then the acid. Ethanol + ethanoic acid gives ethyl ethanoate.

Fuels

  • Fossil fuels: coal, natural gas and petroleum. Methane is the main part of natural gas.
  • Hydrocarbons contain hydrogen and carbon only.
  • Petroleum is a mixture of hydrocarbons, separated by fractional distillation.

From the bottom to the top of the column: chain length decreases, volatility increases, boiling point falls, viscosity falls.

Alkanes

  • Saturated hydrocarbons with single covalent bonds.
  • Generally unreactive, except for combustion and substitution by chlorine.
  • Extended: in substitution, one atom or group is replaced by another. With chlorine, ultraviolet light gives the activation energy: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl.

Alkenes

  • Unsaturated hydrocarbons with a C=C double bond.
  • Cracking: larger alkanes are broken into alkenes and hydrogen (or smaller alkanes), using a high temperature and a catalyst.
  • Why crack? There is more demand for short chains such as petrol, and alkenes are useful for making polymers and chemicals.
  • Test: aqueous bromine goes from orange to colourless with an alkene. It stays orange with an alkane.

Addition reactions (Extended): only one product forms.

  • With bromine: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂
  • With hydrogen, nickel catalyst: C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆
  • With steam, acid catalyst: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH

Alcohols

Making ethanol

  • Fermentation: aqueous glucose with yeast, at 25–35 °C, with no oxygen.
  • From ethene: add steam at 300 °C and 6000 kPa with an acid catalyst.

Combustion: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Uses: a solvent and a fuel.

Carboxylic acids

  • Ethanoic acid is a weak acid. It reacts with metals, bases and carbonates to form ethanoate salts.
  • Example: 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂.

Extended

  • Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid by acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII), or by bacteria during vinegar production.
  • Making esters: carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester + water, with an acid catalyst.

Polymers

  • Polymers are large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called monomers.
  • Poly(ethene) forms by addition polymerisation of ethene.
  • Plastics are made from polymers.

Problems with plastics: they do not break down easily, so they fill landfill sites, build up in oceans, and form toxic gases when burned.

only one product polymer and a small molecule, such as water
  • Addition repeat unit: open the C=C and draw bonds out of both sides.
  • Polyamides (such as nylon) form from a dicarboxylic acid and a diamine. The link is –CO–NH–.
  • Polyesters (such as PET) form from a dicarboxylic acid and a diol. The link is –CO–O–. PET can be converted back into monomers and re-polymerised.
  • Proteins are natural polyamides made from amino acid monomers.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Draw every bond in a displayed formula, including O–H.
  • Bromine water turns colourless, not clear. Clear is not the same as colourless.
  • Ester names start with the alcohol part.

Quick self-check

  1. Give the formula of the alkane with 3 carbons. (C₃H₈)
  2. What is the test for an alkene? (Aqueous bromine goes orange to colourless.)
  3. Name the ester from methanol and propanoic acid. (Methyl propanoate)
