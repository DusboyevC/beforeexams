17 Oscillations

Key definitions

  • Displacement (x): distance from the equilibrium position. Amplitude (x₀): the maximum displacement.
  • Period (T): the time for one complete oscillation. Frequency (f): the number of oscillations per unit time.
  • Angular frequency (ω): ω = 2πf = 2π/T.
  • Phase difference: the fraction of a cycle (in rad or degrees) by which one oscillation leads another.
  • Simple harmonic motion (SHM): motion in which the acceleration is proportional to the displacement from a fixed point and always directed towards that point: a = −ω²x.
  • Damping: the loss of energy from an oscillating system (to the surroundings), which makes the amplitude decrease. Light damping: slow decay of the amplitude. Critical damping: returns to equilibrium in the shortest possible time without oscillating. Heavy damping: returns slowly, without oscillating.
  • Forced oscillation: oscillation driven by a periodic external force, at the driving frequency.
  • Resonance: the amplitude is maximum when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency of the system.

SHM equations

  • x = x₀ sin ωt (starting at equilibrium) or x = x₀ cos ωt (starting at maximum displacement).
  • v = v₀ cos ωt, where v₀ = ωx₀ (maximum speed, at equilibrium).
  • v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²).
  • Maximum acceleration = ω²x₀ (at maximum displacement).

Graphs

  • x, v and a against time are all sinusoidal. v leads x by π/2, and a is in antiphase with x (a phase difference of π).
  • a against x is a straight line through the origin with a negative gradient of −ω².

Energy in SHM

  • Total energy: E = ½mω²x₀² (constant if undamped).
  • Kinetic energy: Eₖ = ½mω²(x₀² − x²); potential energy Eₚ = ½mω²x².
  • The energy changes between kinetic and potential twice per cycle.

Damping and resonance

  • As damping increases: the resonance peak becomes lower and broader, and the peak shifts to a slightly lower frequency.
  • Useful resonance: musical instruments, microwave ovens (water molecules), MRI, tuning a radio circuit.
  • Unwanted resonance: bridges, buildings in earthquakes, car vibration. It is reduced by damping, e.g. shock absorbers (critical damping) in cars.

Worked example

A mass on a spring oscillates with amplitude 4.0 cm and period 0.50 s. Find its maximum speed and maximum acceleration. ω = 2π/0.50 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹; v₀ = ωx₀ = 12.6 × 0.040 = 0.50 m s⁻¹; a_max = ω²x₀ = 158 × 0.040 = 6.3 m s⁻²

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • In the definition of SHM, the acceleration must be directed towards the fixed point (the minus sign).
  • Critical damping = shortest time without oscillation; don't just say "no oscillation".
  • Resonance: the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, giving maximum amplitude.
