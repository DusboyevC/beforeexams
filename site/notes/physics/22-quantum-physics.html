22 Quantum physics

Key definitions

  • Photon: a quantum (discrete packet) of electromagnetic energy: E = hf = hc/λ.
  • Photoelectric effect: the emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation above a certain frequency falls on it.
  • Threshold frequency (f₀): the minimum frequency of radiation needed to release electrons from the surface.
  • Work function (Φ): the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of the metal.
  • Electronvolt: the energy gained by an electron accelerated through a p.d. of 1 V: 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
  • de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p = h/(mv).
  • Photon momentum: p = E/c = h/λ.

The photoelectric effect

Einstein's equation: hf = Φ + ½mv²_max

Observations, and why they support the particle model of light:

  1. Electrons are emitted only if f ≥ f₀, however intense the light. One photon interacts with one electron, so it must have enough energy (hf ≥ Φ) on its own.
  2. The maximum kinetic energy depends on the frequency, not the intensity: KE_max = hf − Φ.
  3. Emission is instantaneous: the energy is delivered in one packet; there is no build-up over time.
  4. A higher intensity (above f₀) gives more photons per second, so more electrons per second: a larger photocurrent, but the same KE_max.

A graph of KE_max against f is a straight line: gradient = h, x-intercept = f₀, and y-intercept = −Φ.

Wave–particle duality

  • Light shows wave behaviour (diffraction, interference) and particle behaviour (the photoelectric effect).
  • Electron diffraction: electrons passed through thin graphite form a ring pattern, so particles have wave properties. A higher accelerating voltage gives a larger momentum, a shorter λ, and smaller rings.

Energy levels and line spectra

  • Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels.
  • When an electron drops from a higher to a lower level, it emits a photon of energy hf = E₁ − E₂. Only certain energy differences are possible, so only certain frequencies appear: a line emission spectrum.
  • Absorption spectrum: white light passed through a cool gas has dark lines at the frequencies whose photon energies exactly match the gaps between levels. Those photons are absorbed and the electrons are excited.

Worked example

Light of wavelength 400 nm falls on a metal with Φ = 2.3 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons. E = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 400 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.1 eV. KE_max = 3.1 − 2.3 = 0.8 eV (1.3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Intensity affects the number of electrons; frequency affects their maximum kinetic energy.
  • Convert eV ↔ J with 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹.
  • Explain line spectra in terms of discrete levels and photon energy = the difference between levels.
