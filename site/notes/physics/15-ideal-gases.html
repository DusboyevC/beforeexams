15 Ideal gases

Key definitions

  • Mole: the amount of substance containing the same number of particles as there are atoms in 12 g of carbon-12; that number is the Avogadro constant, Nₐ = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
  • Ideal gas: a gas that obeys pV ∝ T (pV = nRT) at all pressures, volumes and temperatures.
  • Equation of state: pV = nRT = NkT (R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; k = R/Nₐ = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹; T in kelvin).

Kinetic theory assumptions

  1. The gas contains a very large number of molecules in random motion.
  2. The molecules' volume is negligible compared with the volume of the container.
  3. There are no intermolecular forces except during collisions.
  4. Collisions (with each other and the walls) are perfectly elastic.
  5. The time spent in collisions is negligible compared with the time between collisions.

Pressure from molecular motion

pV = ⅓ Nm⟨c²⟩, where ⟨c²⟩ is the mean square speed.

Derivation outline:

  • A molecule of mass m moves with velocity c in a cube of side L. It hits a wall and rebounds: change of momentum = 2mc.
  • Time between hits on the same wall = 2L/c, so the force = Δp/Δt = 2mc ÷ (2L/c) = mc²/L.
  • For N molecules moving randomly, a third of the motion is in each direction: F = Nm⟨c²⟩/(3L).
  • Pressure = F/L² = Nm⟨c²⟩/(3L³) = Nm⟨c²⟩/(3V).

Explaining pressure: molecules collide with the walls and change momentum, so the walls exert a force on the molecules and (by Newton's third law) the molecules exert a force on the walls. Pressure = total force per unit area.

Temperature and kinetic energy

  • Comparing pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ with pV = NkT: mean kinetic energy of a molecule = ½m⟨c²⟩ = (3/2)kT.
  • So the mean kinetic energy is proportional to the thermodynamic temperature.
  • Root-mean-square speed: c_rms = √⟨c²⟩ = √(3kT/m).

Worked example

Find the r.m.s. speed of nitrogen molecules (m = 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶ kg) at 300 K. c_rms = √(3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 / 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶) = 517 m s⁻¹

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Always use T in kelvin in gas equations.
  • n = number of moles; N = number of molecules. Use R with n and k with N.
  • Real gases behave most like ideal gases at low pressure and high temperature.
  • ⟨c²⟩ (mean square speed) is not the same as the square of the mean speed.

Quick self-check

  1. A gas at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K is heated to 450 K at constant volume. Find the new pressure. (3.0 × 10⁵ Pa)
  2. Explain, using kinetic theory, why pressure rises when the temperature rises at constant volume.
  3. Find the mean kinetic energy of a gas molecule at 27 °C. (6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J)
