3 Dynamics

Key definitions

  • Mass: the property of an object that resists a change in its motion (inertia).
  • Weight: the force on a mass due to a gravitational field: W = mg.
  • Momentum: mass × velocity: p = mv (vector, unit kg m s⁻¹ or N s).
  • Newton's first law: an object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
  • Newton's second law: the resultant force on an object is proportional to its rate of change of momentum, and acts in the same direction: F = Δp/Δt. For constant mass, F = ma.
  • Newton's third law: if body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and of the same type.
  • Principle of conservation of momentum: for a system of interacting objects, the total momentum stays constant provided no resultant external force acts on the system.
  • Impulse: force × time = change in momentum (FΔt = Δp); it is the area under a force–time graph.
  • Elastic collision: total kinetic energy is conserved. Inelastic collision: kinetic energy is not conserved (some is transferred to heat or sound), but momentum still is.

Newton's third law pairs

They act on different bodies and are the same type of force. Example: the Earth pulls a book down with its weight (gravitational) and the book pulls the Earth up with an equal gravitational force. The normal contact force from the table is not the third-law partner of the weight.

Drag and terminal velocity

  • Drag: a frictional force on an object moving through a fluid. It acts opposite to the velocity and increases with speed.
  • For an object falling from rest: at first only weight acts, so a = g. As the speed rises, drag rises and the resultant force falls, so the acceleration falls. When drag = weight (minus upthrust), the resultant force is 0 and the object moves at terminal velocity.

Collisions

  • Momentum before = momentum after: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂. Take care with the signs of the velocities.
  • For an elastic collision, the relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation: u₁ − u₂ = v₂ − v₁.
  • Explosions: the total momentum is zero before, so it is zero after; the fragments move apart with equal and opposite momenta.

Worked example: a 2.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they stick together. 2.0 × 3.0 = 3.0 × v, so v = 2.0 m s⁻¹. KE before = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J; KE after = ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 J. Energy is lost, so the collision is inelastic.

Two-dimensional collisions

  • Momentum is conserved separately in two perpendicular directions. Resolve each velocity into components.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Quote Newton's second law as rate of change of momentum, not just "F = ma".
  • Third-law answers need: equal magnitude, opposite direction, same type, different bodies.
  • Always state the condition for conservation of momentum: no external resultant force (an isolated system).
  • Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions; total energy is always conserved.

Quick self-check

  2. State two differences between mass and weight.
  3. Explain why a passenger in a car crash is safer with a crumple zone, using impulse.
