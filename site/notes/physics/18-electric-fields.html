18 Electric fields

Key definitions

  • Electric field: a region in which a charge experiences a force.
  • Electric field strength (E): the force per unit positive charge: E = F/q (N C⁻¹ or V m⁻¹).
  • Coulomb's law: the force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: F = Qq/(4πε₀r²).
  • Electric potential (V) at a point: the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to that point: V = Q/(4πε₀r).
  • Electric potential energy: Eₚ = qV = Qq/(4πε₀r).

Uniform fields (parallel plates)

  • E = ΔV/Δd: the field is the same everywhere between the plates (except near the edges). The field lines are parallel and equally spaced, from + to −.
  • A charged particle entering perpendicular to the field follows a parabolic path, like a projectile:
  • parallel to the plates: constant velocity;

  • perpendicular to the plates: constant acceleration a = qE/m.

  • A charge accelerated from rest through a p.d. V gains kinetic energy qV = ½mv².

Point charges (radial fields)

  • E = Q/(4πε₀r²). The field lines are radial: outward from a positive charge, inward to a negative one.
  • Outside a charged conducting sphere, the field and potential are as if all the charge were at the centre.
  • E = −dV/dr: the field strength is minus the potential gradient.
  • The potential is positive near a positive charge and negative near a negative charge.

Comparing electric and gravitational fields

Similarities Differences
Both are inverse-square laws for point sources Gravity is always attractive; electric forces can attract or repel
Field strength = force per unit (mass or charge) Gravitational potential is always negative; electric potential can be + or −
Potential defined as work done from infinity Electric forces are much stronger between subatomic particles

Worked example

Find the force between two charges of +2.0 μC and −3.0 μC that are 0.10 m apart (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹). F = (2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁶) / (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.10²) = 5.4 N (attractive)

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Field strength definitions need "per unit positive charge".
  • Between parallel plates, use d = the separation of the plates.
  • An electron deflects towards the positive plate; draw the curve as a parabola, and as a straight line after it leaves the plates.

Quick self-check

  1. Find the field strength between plates 4.0 mm apart with a p.d. of 2000 V. (5.0 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹)
  2. Describe the path of an electron entering a uniform electric field at right angles to it.
  3. State one similarity and one difference between gravitational and electric potential.
