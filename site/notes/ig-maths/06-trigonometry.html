6 Trigonometry

Pythagoras' theorem

  • In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.
  • Example: sides 5 cm and 12 cm. c = √(25 + 144) = 13 cm.
  • For a shorter side, subtract: b² = c² − a².

Sine, cosine and tangent

SOH CAH TOA

  • sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
  • cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse
  • tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent

Finding a side: hypotenuse 10 cm, angle 35°. Opposite = 10 × sin 35° = 5.74 cm.

Finding an angle: opposite 5 cm, adjacent 8 cm. tan θ = 5/8, so θ = 32.0°.

Trigonometric graphs (Extended)

  • y = sin x: starts at 0, peaks at 90°, returns to 0 at 180°, lowest at 270°, back to 0 at 360°.
  • y = cos x: starts at 1, is 0 at 90°, lowest at 180°, 0 at 270°, 1 at 360°.
  • y = tan x: repeats every 180°, with vertical asymptotes at 90° and 270°.

Solving for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°

  • sin x = √3/2: x = 60° or 120°, as sin(180° − x) = sin x.
  • 2cos x + 1 = 0: cos x = −½, so x = 120° or 240°, as cos(360° − x) = cos x.
  • tan x: add 180° to the first answer.

Any triangle (Extended)

Sides a, b, c are opposite angles A, B, C.

Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C

  • Use with a pair: a side and its opposite angle.
  • Example: A = 40°, B = 65°, a = 8 cm. b = 8 × sin 65° ÷ sin 40° = 11.3 cm.
  • Ambiguous case: sin x = 0.5 gives x = 30° or 150°. Check whether the obtuse angle is possible.

Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

  • Use for two sides and the angle between them, or for three sides.
  • Example: b = 7 cm, c = 9 cm, A = 50°. a² = 49 + 81 − 126 cos 50° = 49.0, so a = 7.00 cm.

Area: ½ab sin C

  • Example: ½ × 7 × 9 × sin 50° = 24.1 cm².

The sine rule, cosine rule and area formula are on the list of formulas.

Bearings

Draw a clear diagram with north lines, then use right-angled trigonometry, or the sine and cosine rules.

Three dimensions (Extended)

Find a right-angled triangle inside the solid.

Example: a cuboid is 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm.

  • Base diagonal = √(9 + 16) = 5 cm
  • Space diagonal = √(25 + 144) = 13 cm
  • Angle between the diagonal and the base: tan θ = 12/5, so θ = 67.4°

Angle between a line and a plane: drop a perpendicular from the line to the plane, and use the angle in the right-angled triangle made.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Use degree mode on your calculator.
  • Keep extra digits in your working. Round only the final answer.
  • The cosine rule needs the included angle.
