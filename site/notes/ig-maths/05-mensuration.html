5 Mensuration

Units

  • 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 km = 1000 m.
  • 1 m² = 10 000 cm².
  • 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.
  • 1 litre = 1000 ml = 1000 cm³.
  • 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
  • 1 kg = 1000 g.

Area and perimeter

  • Rectangle: area = length × width.
  • Triangle: area = ½ × base × height.
  • Parallelogram: area = base × height.
  • Trapezium: area = ½(a + b)h.

Circles

  • Circumference = 2πr = πd.
  • Area = πr².
  • Example: r = 5 cm. Circumference = 31.4 cm. Area = 78.5 cm².

Arcs and sectors

  • Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr.
  • Sector area = (θ/360) × πr².
  • Example: r = 6 cm, θ = 60°. Arc = 6.28 cm. Sector area = 18.8 cm².

The list of formulas on the exam paper gives the curved or full surface area formulas and the volume formulas for the prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone and sphere. Learn the 2D area formulas, other than the triangle.

Worked examples

  • Cylinder, r = 3 cm, h = 10 cm: V = π × 9 × 10 = 283 cm³. Total surface = 60π + 18π = 245 cm².
  • Cone, r = 3 cm, h = 4 cm: slant l = 5 cm by Pythagoras. V = ⅓ × π × 9 × 4 = 37.7 cm³. Curved surface = π × 3 × 5 = 47.1 cm².
  • Half sphere, r = 6 cm: V = ½ × (4/3)π × 216 = 452 cm³.

Compound shapes

  • Split the shape into parts, then add or subtract.
  • Example: a square of side 10 cm with a circle of radius 3 cm cut out. Area = 100 − 9π = 71.7 cm².
  • A sector minus a triangle gives a segment. Extended: use ½ab sin C for the triangle.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Convert all lengths to the same unit first.
  • Do not confuse diameter and radius. Halve the diameter.
  • Answers 'in terms of π' should keep π: 9π, not 28.3.
  • For a closed cylinder's surface, include both ends.

Quick self-check

  1. Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides 4 cm and 6 cm, height 3 cm. (15 cm²)
  2. How many litres are in 2 m³? (2000)
  3. Find the volume of a sphere of radius 3 cm. (113 cm³)
