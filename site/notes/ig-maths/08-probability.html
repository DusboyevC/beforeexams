8 Probability

Basics

  • Probability runs from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
  • Give it as a fraction, decimal or percentage.
  • Probability of an event = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.
  • P(not A) = 1 − P(A). If P(blue) = 0.8, then P(not blue) = 0.2.

Relative and expected frequency

  • Relative frequency = number of times the event happened ÷ number of trials. It estimates the probability, and improves with more trials.
  • Expected frequency = probability × number of trials. If P = 0.2 and there are 150 trials, expect 30.
  • Fair means every outcome is equally likely. Biased means they are not. Random means each has a chance of being chosen.

Combined events

  • AND: multiply along the branches.
  • OR: add the separate outcomes.

Sample space: two dice give 36 outcomes. P(total of 7) = 6/36 = 1/6.

Tree diagram with replacement: a bag has 3 red and 2 blue. Take one, replace it, then take another.

  • P(two red) = 3/5 × 3/5 = 9/25
  • P(one of each colour) = 3/5 × 2/5 + 2/5 × 3/5 = 12/25

Venn diagrams: put the intersection in first. Then add the rest, and check the total.

Without replacement (Extended)

The second probabilities change.

  • A bag has 3 red and 2 blue. Take two, with no replacement.
  • P(two red) = 3/5 × 2/4 = 3/10
  • P(two blue) = 2/5 × 1/4 = 1/10

Conditional probability (Extended)

This is the probability of an event, given that another has happened. Use only the part of the diagram or table that matches the given information.

Example: 30 students. 20 play football and 12 of those also play tennis. Given a student plays football, P(tennis) = 12/20 = 3/5.

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Do not write probability as a ratio, such as 3 : 5, or as words.
  • All the end branches of a tree should add to 1.
  • Reduce the numerators and denominators on the second pick when not replacing.

Quick self-check

  1. P(rain) = 0.3. Find P(no rain). (0.7)
  2. A coin is tossed 200 times. How many heads do you expect? (100)
  3. A fair coin is tossed twice. Find P(two heads). (¼)
