4 Geometry

Terms

  • Acute: under 90°. Obtuse: 90° to 180°. Reflex: over 180°.
  • Parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
  • Similar shapes have the same shape. Congruent shapes have the same shape and size.
  • Triangles: equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right-angled.
  • Quadrilaterals: square, rectangle, kite, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezium.
  • Polygons: pentagon (5), hexagon (6), octagon (8), decagon (10).
  • Circle words: centre, radius, diameter, circumference, semicircle, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment.

Constructions and scale drawings

  • Use a ruler and compasses, and show all construction arcs.
  • To construct a triangle from three sides, draw one side, then draw arcs from each end.
  • Constructing perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors is not required.
  • Nets: a flat shape that folds into a solid.
  • A scale of 1 : 50 means 1 cm on the drawing is 50 cm in real life.

Bearings

  • Measured clockwise from north, with three figures: 025°.
  • Back bearing: add or subtract 180°. If B is on 025° from A, then A is on 205° from B.

Similarity

Find the scale factor, then multiply. If two sides are 4 cm and 10 cm, the scale factor is 2.5.

Extended

  • Area scale factor = k².
  • Volume scale factor = k³.
  • Example: two similar jars have heights 5 cm and 10 cm. The large one has 8 times the volume, as k = 2 and k³ = 8.
  • You may need to show that two triangles are similar, by giving geometric reasons for equal angles.

Symmetry

  • Line symmetry: a mirror line.
  • Order of rotational symmetry: how many times a shape fits onto itself in one turn.
  • A square has 4 lines and order 4. A rectangle has 2 lines and order 2.
  • Extended, solids: prisms, cylinders, pyramids and cones have planes and axes of symmetry. A plane of symmetry divides a solid into two mirror halves.

Angle rules

  • Angles at a point add to 360°.
  • Angles on a straight line add to 180°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Angles in a triangle add to 180°.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral add to 360°.

Parallel lines

  • Corresponding angles are equal.
  • Alternate angles are equal.
  • Co-interior angles add to 180°.

Polygons

  • Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°.
  • Exterior angles of any polygon add to 360°.
  • Regular polygon: exterior angle = 360° ÷ n.
  • Example: regular octagon. Exterior = 360 ÷ 8 = 45°, so interior = 135°.

Circle theorems

Core

  • The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • The angle between a tangent and a radius is 90°.

Extended

  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.
  • The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre.
  • Tangents from an external point are equal in length.

Worked example: a cyclic quadrilateral has one angle of 110°. The opposite angle = 180 − 110 = 70° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral).

Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Always give a reason using the correct geometrical words.
  • Use three-letter notation: angle ABC has its vertex at B.
  • Do not say 'Z angles'. Say 'alternate angles'.

Quick self-check

  1. Find the interior angle of a regular hexagon. (120°)
  2. The bearing of B from A is 300°. Find the bearing of A from B. (120°)
  3. An angle at the centre is 80°. Find the angle at the circumference on the same arc. (40°)
